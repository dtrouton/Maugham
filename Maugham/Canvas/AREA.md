## The architecture, and why the alternative lost

**A single SwiftUI `Canvas` draws every node; exactly one real `NSTextView` is mounted, on the scrap being edited; the camera is a manual CTM inside the draw call.** Hit testing is an inverse transform plus a reverse-z rect test against the model, so it never touches SwiftUI's event machinery. Culling is `guard rect.intersects(viewport)` in the draw loop — no `ForEach` identity to destroy, so virtualisation cannot cost the writer their focus or an in-progress edit.

**Someone will propose `NSScrollView` with real subviews again.** It is the runner-up and it looks better on paper: crisp text, real editing, and `setMagnification(_:centeredAt:)` for free. It is disqualified by measurement, not taste — read `docs/superpowers/notes/2026-07-25-canvas-rendering-spike.md` (Q1) before re-opening it. SwiftUI content hosted inside a magnified `NSScrollView` reports the **same `.global` frame at every zoom** (200 pt at 1.0×, 1.25×, 1.5×, 2×, 3× and 0.6× alike, where AppKit says 250/300/400/600/120), and synthesised clicks land 2/8 — both successes at magnification 1.0. At 1.5× the gesture fires with the point multiplied by the magnification instead of unapplied; **at 2× the mistranslated point falls outside the view's bounds and clicks stop registering entirely.** One `NSHostingView` per node does not escape it. AppKit itself is flawless under magnification; the advantage is only available to a pure-AppKit node layer, which is against the grain of the whole codebase.

## The shared TextKit stack — `ScrapLayout`

§7A.2 names the drawn/edited seam the biggest risk in the design: if the two layouts differ at all, text jumps every time the writer clicks in and again when they click out. The mitigation is structural — **one layout, both consumers read it.** `ScrapLayout` builds one `NSTextContentStorage` / `NSTextLayoutManager` / `NSTextContainer` stack per scrap, and `CanvasRenderer.drawCard` and the mounted `NSTextView` both draw through it.

Three requirements. Each is verbatim from the spike; each breaks the surface silently:

1. **`contentStorage.textStorage = NSTextStorage(...)`, NEVER `contentStorage.attributedString = ...`.** *Symptom:* the scrap lays out and renders perfectly, `textView.textContentStorage` is identity-equal to ours — and `textView.textStorage` is nil, `textView.string` is empty, and both real keystrokes and `insertText` are silent no-ops. A card that draws beautifully and refuses a single character. **This one fails as a UI bug and is a wiring bug**, which is why it has its own tripwire (26).
2. **`lineFragmentPadding = 0`** (`NSTextContainer` defaults to 5), **`widthTracksTextView = false`**, **`textContainerInset = .zero`**. *Symptom:* any one left at its default shifts drawn against edited. `textContainerInset` is the nastiest: it feeds `textContainerOrigin` (where the *view* paints) and reaches the shared container through nothing, so **27% of pixels move while every geometry assertion still passes** — measured during Task 3. Geometry equality is structurally incapable of seeing this class, which is why `ScrapLayoutTests` carries a bitmap diff alongside the signature comparison.
3. **Draw at the window's true `backingScaleFactor` × camera zoom, and NEVER derive that scale by hand.** *Symptom:* deriving it from pixel width bakes in AppKit's frame rounding and shifts glyphs by a subpixel — "text jumps" wearing a measurement-artifact disguise. `CanvasRenderer` satisfies this by doing nothing; `GraphicsContext.withCGContext` already hands over a context at backing scale under the camera CTM. A grep test forbids `backingScaleFactor`/`convertToBacking`/`convertFromBacking`/`pixelsWide`/`pixelsHigh` in code anywhere in this directory.

`ScrapLayout.init(textColor:)` defaults to `.labelColor`, but **every construction under `Maugham/Canvas/` must name `textColor: CanvasRenderer.cardInk`** — grep-enforced, because taking the default gives the same colour today and so nothing would break if you forgot. `cardPaper`/`cardInk` are appearance-dynamic (paper 1.000 under Aqua / `darkCardPaper` (0.235, 0.232, 0.226) under DarkAqua, ink `labelColor` 0.00 / 1.00). A paper-white card in dark mode is a light-mode skeuomorph, not an honest object on the ground (§7.2).

## Card metrics live in `CanvasCardMetrics`, and nowhere else

`CanvasNode.width` is the CARD width; the text box is inset 10 pt on all four sides and the card height is the measured text height plus the same inset twice. `CanvasRenderer` (which draws the text) and `CanvasView` (which mounts the editor over it) both read those functions.

**A second spelling of the inset anywhere puts drawn glyphs and edited glyphs on different rects** — the §7A.2 "text jumps on focus" failure arriving by the back door, past every structural defence above.

**The two halves meet in exactly one function, and it is not in a view.** A scrap card's height is `CanvasCardMetrics.cardHeight(forTextHeight:)` over `ScrapLayout.measuredHeight`, and that pairing is `CanvasScrapMeasure.height(of:)` — the only place in this directory those two are written together. `CanvasView` still builds and caches every `ScrapLayout` (the mounted editor and the draw pass share one TextKit stack per scrap — tripwire 26; **this lifted the height arithmetic, never the layout cache**) and asks for the number; `height(text:cardWidth:)` answers for a caller with no card on screen yet, which is what placing cards without overlapping them needs before anything is drawn. **`CanvasScrapMeasure.scrapFont` is the card face** for the same reason: a measurement taken at the system font is a different number for the same words.

**An ITEM node is MEASURED too, as of 1C-d, and its arithmetic is not a scrap's.** `CanvasCardMetrics.itemCardHeight(forCardWidth:pictureAspect:)` is the one answer: the picture at the card's content width over its own aspect ratio, then `itemPictureGap` (6), then one line of label, inside the inset twice. It is a genuine function of the WIDTH — the same rule §7A.3 already had for a scrap's text, arriving on the second content type — which is what makes Task 6's resize safe rather than a repeat of the Critical below. *This paragraph used to say "nothing MEASURES one", and to park the widening on 1C-d; that is the task that did it.*

**`CanvasCardMetrics.itemLabelOnlyHeight` is what stayed, and it is now the FLOOR rather than the answer.** It is what a card with no picture measures to, and what a card whose photograph has not decoded yet measures to — one line at `itemLabelFontSize`, which is why the size is a shared constant rather than an `11` in each place. A node with no `cachedHeight` has no `frame`, which `nodes(intersecting:)` and `topmostNode(at:)` both drop: **not drawn, not clickable.** It was called `itemPlaceholderHeight` until 1C-d, when the placeholder it was named for stopped existing.

**What holds that height is the MEASUREMENT, and the two guards that used to hold it are gone** *(1C-c3 whole-branch Critical; re-opened deliberately by 1C-d Task 6)*. This paragraph used to say the height "has to be set by whoever creates it" and to concede that a hand-edited sidecar left a card silently absent. Creation was never the whole analysis: `CanvasScene.setWidth` CLEARS `cachedHeight` by design, and until 1C-c3 nothing in production made an item node, so no gesture could reach it. `CanvasClaudePlacement` makes one on every `add_canvas_scraps` naming a source — and the resize corner had no kind test, while `drawCard` drew the triangle inviting the writer to grab it. One drag took the photographed page off the canvas permanently, `cachedHeight: nil` and all, through a save.

**The fix at the time was three things, and two of them were a stand-in for the third.** (1) `CanvasInteraction.begin` took the corner for `.scrap` only; (2) `CanvasRenderer.drawCard` drew the resize mark on a `.scrap` only — the mark and the target are ONE decision, like the promoted stripe; (3) `rebuildLayouts` HEALED a missing item height rather than conceding the sidecar route. **(1) and (2) were a fix for a missing measurement pass, not a ruling that item nodes should not resize**, and 1C-d retired them by supplying the measurement: (3) is now a real function of the card's WIDTH with the floor underneath it, and `CanvasView.remeasure` re-derives BOTH kinds per drag frame. So the corner and the mark are unconditional again, which is the rule the surface had before the Critical.

**The per-frame arm is the half that is easy to miss.** `rebuildLayouts` runs at `.ended`; a card healed only there is off the surface for the whole length of the gesture — no `frame`, so dropped by `nodes(intersecting:)` and by `topmostNode(at:)`, neither drawn nor clickable while the writer holds the mouse down. That is exactly how the writer meets it, and it is why every test here reads the scene **mid-drag, through the real event view**. Pinned by `CanvasViewMountingTests.test_aCornerDragOnClaudesSourcePageResizesItAndStaysOnTheCanvas` (mid-drag; frame, `nodes(intersecting:)`, `topmostNode(at:)`, then flushed to disk), `test_aCornerDragOnAPicturedItemCardKeepsThePhotographsAspectRatio` (§7A.3: width authoritative, height derived, so a corner drag scales and never distorts), `test_anItemNodeThatArrivesWithNoHeightIsHealedOnLoad`, `CanvasInteractionTests.test_theCornerOfAnItemNodeResizesItLikeAnyOtherCard` + `test_anItemNodeIsClampedToTheSameMinimumWidthAsAScrap`, and the mark assertion in `CanvasRendererTests.test_anItemNodeTakesTheSameBorderAndResizeMarkAsAScrap`. **Disable experiment, run 2026-07-31:** delete the `.item` arm of `remeasure` and the mid-drag assertions go red while every post-release one stays green — the original Critical, reproducing on demand, invisible to a test that only looks after the mouse comes up.

**One rule, not two, for the width floor.** `CanvasInteraction.minimumCardWidth` (120, renamed from `minimumScrapWidth`) clamps every kind; the clamp is a single line in `update`'s `.resizing` arm and never had a kind test on it. A second minimum for the second kind would be a rule invented for a card that does not need one.

## What an item node says it is — `CanvasItemFacts` (1C-d Task 4)

**Three facts, resolved from a reference and an index that is handed in: a title, a kind glyph, and a project-relative thumbnail path when there is a picture.** This is `Promotion`/`ArtifactIndex`'s shape for `ArtifactIndex`'s reason — pure, testable, and one walk of the manifest rather than one per query. It is **not** `ArtifactIndex`: that index answers a promotion's question (may this mark be overwritten, and by what), its vocabulary has no photograph, and a *path* on its `Entry` would invite resolving a promotion target by filename. `CanvasItemIndex` is the second index over one manifest, and both are built at the same site.

**A referenced item the writer deleted gets a sentence, not an id.** `CanvasItemFacts.missingTitle` is "No longer in the project." — deliberately `PromotedArtifactSection.contributionArtifactMissing`'s own predicate with the subject dropped, because the string is drawn as a one-line card label as well as read in the pane, and the card the writer is looking at supplies the subject. The two are asserted *against each other* rather than against literals, so a rewording of either that leaves them speaking differently goes red.

**An owned image's title is the fixed noun "Image", and the reason is that the filename carries nothing.** `ImagePasteHandler.destination` mints `image-yyyyMMdd-HHmmss.<ext>` and the writer's own filename is discarded at ingest — so a filename title is the clock reading at the moment they dropped the picture, dressed as a name. What tells one owned card from another is the picture on it. *If ingest ever preserves the source filename, that decision is void.*

**What the surface actually reads is `CanvasItemPresentation`, and it is ONE value with three consumers** *(Task 5)*. The draw pass, the measurement pass (`CanvasView.rebuildLayouts`) and the accessibility tree are all handed the same resolved facts, so the card a writer looks at, the rect a click lands in and the sentence VoiceOver reads cannot come to different conclusions about what a card is. `layouts` is the shape being copied: built once per structural change and handed down, never resolved per node inside a `Canvas` closure. **It is resolved at the head of `rebuildLayouts` rather than behind an invalidation key of its own**, because the measurement *needs* it — an item card's height is its picture's aspect ratio — and two schedules would leave one of them a frame behind the other. A manifest change with no structural change is the one case that misses, and `.onChange(of: itemIndex.fingerprint)` is the line that catches it: the writer renames the research note a card points at, nothing on the canvas moves, and without it the card shows the old title for the rest of the session.

**What an item card SHOWS, and the placeholder that is gone.** The picture at the top when one has arrived, and under it a line of kind glyph and title. The dashed border went with `placeholderLabel` — it said "unfinished", which was honest while the only content was `Item · res-3f2a`, and would contradict a card that shows a photograph. **This is what pays §8A.2's reproduction corollary**, which ADR 0026 §10 had recorded as "structural here, visible at 1C-d": a confident fabrication in a reproduction channel wears the writer's own voice, so the page and the scraps read off it have to be comparable **by looking**, not by clicking through. `CanvasRendererTests.test_anItemNodeWithAPictureDrawsIt` is the fixture that says so — two renders of one scene differing only in whether the thumbnail has decoded, counted inside the picture's own rect.

**A glyph is FITTED into its box, never stretched to it**, and the title is drawn in `secondaryLabelColor` because it is a caption for something that exists elsewhere rather than the writer's own words — `drawChip`'s reading of the same question. An SF Symbol is not square (`doc.text` is taller than wide, `waveform` wider than tall), so a symbol drawn straight into a square box would make one kind glyph disagree with the next about what a glyph looks like.

**Nothing decodes on the frame path, and the view is what schedules the decoding.** `CanvasThumbnails.resolved` is a dictionary lookup that records a miss; `servicePending()` does the work off the frame path. `CanvasView` runs it from `.task(id: thumbnailServiceTicket)` — a **monotonic ticket** bumped at the end of `rebuildLayouts` whenever a resolve missed something, deliberately not `revision` (tripwire 30: a decode has nothing to do with a frame, and keying it on the redraw counter restarts the task on every drag frame). **It was a pending COUNT for one commit and that stalled**: a decode that FAILS drains the queue, reports false and leaves the count where it stood, so the next miss that happened to produce the same count changed no id and scheduled nothing — and the next photograph the writer dropped never decoded, for the rest of the session, silently. A ticket says the honest thing, which is *that there is work* rather than how much. **What it does when something lands is `rebuildLayouts()` and not a redraw**, because a picture changes the card's *height* and not only its pixels; `test_anOwnedItemNodesCardGrowsWhenItsPhotographHasDecoded` drives that through the real view, and the redraw-only version of the line leaves the card at the floor with every renderer test green.

**A card's SHAPE outlives its pixels, and the reason is that a bounded cache met a derived height** *(Task 5's re-review, N1)*. `CanvasThumbnails` evicts LRU over a byte budget — roughly 85 resident thumbnails at the size an item card asks for — and an evicted path is a *miss*, which `resolved` re-queues. So on a canvas above that line every resolve misses on whatever the last service evicted. Two things follow, and each needed its own answer:

- **The height must not move.** `CanvasThumbnails.aspect(_:in:)` is a second, permanent memo — path → aspect ratio, recorded on the first successful decode, never evicted, 16 bytes an image — and the measurement reads *that*, never the resident `CGImage`. Without it a card whose thumbnail was evicted re-measures to the floor and the writer's layout moves every time the cache rotates. **Pixels are a cache; a shape is a fact.** Keyed without the bucket and first-decode-wins, because a thumbnail's own dimensions differ from its source's by up to a pixel of rounding at each rung (256×171 is 1.4971 where 512×341 is 1.5015) — which is also the second half of why a request does not follow the zoom. **First-decode-wins is stale the moment the FILE changes, and the writer does not have to wait for an eviction to meet it** *(found by Task 5's re-review, contained by Task 6, completed by review I1)*: a bucket change alone is a different cache key and therefore a genuinely fresh decode of the new file, meeting a memo that describes the old one, and `GraphicsContext.draw(_:in:)` stretches to the rect it is given — so a portrait photograph replaced in place is drawn **squashed into a landscape box**, which is the one thing an image on this surface may not do (§8A.2). The resize is what made crossing a bucket an ordinary gesture. So `CanvasThumbnails.store` records the shape when it is absent **or** when a fresh decode differs from the memo by more than `aspectReshapeTolerance` (**1%**): the ladder's measured spread is ≤0.3% and a reshoot in the other orientation is ~50× larger, so every case first-decode-wins was written for survives intact.

**And the reshape branch drops the PIXELS at every other bucket, which the first version of it did not** — the half that turns relocating the squash into removing it. `entries` is keyed by bucket and nothing else in the file invalidates it on a file change (the only removal is LRU), so moving the memo alone leaves the other buckets holding the old photograph, ready to be served into a box measured from the new shape. **One gesture reaches it**: drag the card out — fresh decode at the bigger bucket, memo moves, drawn correctly — then drag it back in, and the smaller bucket's stale pixels arrive in the new shape's box. `dropOtherBuckets(of:)` is deliberately narrow (one path, one project) because a cache dropping more than it has evidence about is a decode per frame wearing a correctness fix's name, and `test_aReshootMovesTheShapeMemoAndTheStalePixelsWithIt`'s untouched second photograph is the control that says so. It asserts four things — memo moves, rung does not move it, the old bucket now serves the new picture, `residentBytes` follows the eviction — because each of the three plausible half-fixes passes a different subset. **The 1% margin is also coupled to which rungs are REACHABLE**: at 128 the ladder's own rounding exceeds it at 2.54:1, and 128 is out of reach only because `CanvasInteraction.minimumCardWidth` asks for ~200 px. That coupling is held by an assertion in `test_theReshootToleranceSeparatesRoundingFromAReshape`, not by luck.
- **The schedule must not feed itself.** `rebuildLayouts(bumpsThumbnailTicket: false)` is the one caller that does not re-arm the servicing ticket, and it is the one the ticket schedules: resolve → miss → ticket → service → evict → resolve is a loop with no exit, re-decoding the same photographs and rebuilding the accessibility tree on every turn. **A pending-set signature does not close it** — above the budget the evicted tail rotates, so each pending set differs from the last and a signature bumps on all of them; it settles only by eventually repeating a set, which is the stall it was meant to avoid arriving a lap later.

**What is left is bounded and stated:** above the budget some cards draw an empty picture box until the next structural change services them, and each structural change on such a canvas costs a batch of re-decodes. Both are off the frame path and neither moves a height. If that ever bites, the fix is to resolve only what the draw pass would not cull — which needs a viewport this view deliberately does not have (`Canvas`'s own `size`, and no `GeometryReader`) — or a bigger budget, not a return of the loop.

**`CanvasView.thumbnailCache` is the seam that made N1 testable**, and it is the whole cache rather than a budget: the honest instrument is `decodeCount` (six photographs, six decodes), because the obvious one — watching `model.sceneRevision` fail to move — passes for the wrong reason. Measured while writing the test: this harness stops delivering SwiftUI updates once the mount burst is over, so the pre-fix loop ran three turns and went quiet, green over a live defect. `CanvasViewMountingTests.test_aCanvasOverTheThumbnailBudgetSettlesInsteadOfLooping` asserts six decodes for six photographs and every card at its picture's height, and both halves of the fix falsify it independently — re-arm the ticket and the decode count climbs past six; bypass the shape memo and cards drop to the floor. **How MANY it climbs to, and WHICH cards fall, differ from run to run and are not written down here**: `unorderedNodes` is `Array(byID.values)`, so the eviction order is a `Dictionary`'s and the same fix reported 14-against-6 and "cards 2, 3 and 4" to one reader and a different pair of figures to the next. The assertion messages are correctly general about it; a figure quoted from one run would read as a calibration.

**Nothing about that height is written to the sidecar, and that is deliberate.** A height is derived (§7A.3) and this pass runs on every load, so `canvas.json`'s copy is an optimisation rather than the record: a decode landing does not queue a save — opening a canvas would then dirty its own file — and the next launch measures from the floor up to the same number. What must never persist is a *nil* height, and the heal is what closes that.

**The camera's zoom is deliberately not part of a thumbnail request**, and the reason is on `CanvasThumbnails.assumedPixelScale` rather than here: a request that followed the zoom would re-measure every pictured card as the writer zoomed, because a thumbnail's aspect ratio differs from its source's by up to a pixel of rounding at each rung of the ladder. So a photograph inspected at 6× is softer than the display could show. That is the smaller cost of the two and it is bounded by the resize, which asks for more pixels because the request follows the card's WIDTH.

**`CanvasRenderer.connectHandleRect`'s subtraction is now CORRECT rather than merely harmless, and the note it carried is closed** *(the 1C-c3 finding, verified 2026-07-31)*. It subtracts `resizeHandleSize` on every card, and between 1C-c3 and 1C-d an item node's connect dot was therefore placed as though a triangle it did not draw sat below it. Task 6 draws that triangle on every card, so the reservation is for a mark that exists. The arithmetic behind the earlier decision to leave it was re-done rather than quoted: the clamp bites only below `2 * (resizeHandleSize + connectHandleSize / 2)` = **42 pt**, a 4:3 photograph on a default-width card measures **205 pt** (34 label-only + 165 picture + 6 gap) and is untouched, and a label-only card at the **34 pt** floor takes the clamp — leaving the dot inside the card and clear of the resize square, which is what has to hold now that the square is a live target with ink on it. Pinned by `CanvasLineRenderTests.test_theConnectDotsReservationIsForAMarkEveryCardNowDraws`.

**The cache key is `(sceneRevision, CanvasItemIndex.fingerprint)`, and each half is load-bearing.** `sceneRevision` alone is the subtle one: the writer renames the research note a card points at, nothing on the canvas moves, and the card shows the old title for the rest of the session. The fingerprint alone misses a newly added item node. The fingerprint is **content-derived** (`StableHash.fnv1a64Hex` over the entries sorted by id, once, at build) rather than a counter bumped per rebuild — the index is rebuilt on the window's body path, so a mint-per-build counter would move on every pass and the cache would hold nothing with nothing red. Content-derived is also *narrower* than a manifest-save counter: a tag or a caption changing leaves it alone. **Never `.onChange(of: index)`** — a dictionary comparison per frame is tripwire 30's shape arriving through the invalidation check. It was `entriesByID.hashValue` for one commit, which is process-seeded (SE-0206) — the same fact that made `CanvasMembership.homeRegion` answer differently between runs of one binary — and it needed a hole widened in `TripwireGrepTests.test_noHashValueInPersistedIdConstruction` to compile. `StableHash` is the same O(n) walk with no seeding and no caveat.

### The item node's own inspector arm — `ItemInspector` *(1C-d Task 7)*

**Selecting a page card used to draw the selected stroke and the connect dot over a pane reading *"Select something on the canvas."*** — ADR 0026 §10's recorded decision-not-bug, because every sentence in the card arm is wrong for a reference. This is the arm it was waiting for, and it is small: the reference's **title and kind glyph**, the **provenance row**, and **Open in Research**.

**`RegionInspectorPane` routes by KIND now, in a `switch` rather than a `case .scrap` guard with a fall-through.** The ruling is unchanged — an item node still never reaches `ScrapInspector` — but the shape is: the fall-through was silent, which is exactly how a selected page card came to be told to select something once 1C-c3 started minting item nodes. A `switch` makes the next node kind a compile error instead of an empty state.

**Open in Research is a VALUE, not an `if` in the body** (`OpenAffordance`, `RegionInspector.CiteAffordance`'s shape for its reason), and **the missing state is asked of `CanvasItemIndex`, never read back out of the title.** Task 4 left `CanvasItemFacts` at three facts and no fourth and named this exact caller in its doc comment; a `title == missingTitle` comparison would tie a control to a sentence somebody may reword *and* would withhold the button from a real note the writer happened to title "No longer in the project." — which `test_aNoteTitledLikeTheDeletedSentenceStillOpens` is. **So no fourth field was owed after all**: the question was asked of the thing that already answers it.

**An owned picture gets a sentence and not a Reveal in Finder.** There is no research item behind it, and the file it points at was ingested under a minted name (`ImagePasteHandler.destination`, `image-yyyyMMdd-HHmmss.<ext>`) with the writer's own filename discarded — so revealing it answers a question about content with a clock reading in a folder they never chose, which is the failure `CanvasItemFacts.ownedTitle` already names for the *title* arriving the same way. *If an owned image ever gains a home the writer can navigate to, that is a link rather than a sentence.* **A dangling reference is likewise not an error state**: the writer deleted the note, the title above already says so, and the sentence carries **no id** — `PromotedArtifactSection.contributionArtifactMissing`'s precedent.

**The arm mutates nothing**, so tripwire 32's verb is absent rather than applied: with no mutation there is no bracket to close. Anything added here that writes to the scene needs `mutateFromInspector` deliberately, and `test_theArmMutatesNothing` is what makes that a decision rather than an inheritance. **That survives Task 8's Promote… button**, and the distinction is the one worth keeping: the button *posts a command* and writes nothing — the scene change is `PromotionPerformer`'s, which is why the tripwire-32 census names that file and not this one. **No Delete button** — ⌫ remains the only route to deleting a node.

**The Promote… button is on ONE of the two provenances, and since Task 12a the SECTION is on both** *(Task 8, amended)*. An owned picture can produce a research asset and can be added to a palette card, so it gets the button and the whole section, with `PromotedArtifactSection.Subject.picture`; a referenced item still gets no button, and `Promotion.itemNodeReason` is still its sentence. What it does get is the **contribution** half, with `Subject.referencedPicture`, once a region's palette promotion has copied its picture onto a card — see "A region that holds a picture promotes with it" below. **`ItemInspector.promotes(_:)` is a third spelling of one rule** — the other two are `Promotion.targets` and `CanvasPromotionModifier.isPromotable` — so it is `static` and asserted *against them* rather than trusted (`test_theArmsGateAgreesWithTheTargetsAndTheCommand`): drift is a dead sheet in one direction and a greyed-out command in the other.

**Its heading is `CanvasAccessibility.itemKind`** — the same word VoiceOver says, now a constant for `regionKind`'s reason — asserted against the spoken label rather than against the constant, since `header == itemKind` is true by assignment and would survive the label going back to a literal.

## Focus straightens the card (§7A.5)

The whole card carries a seeded angle — FNV-1a over `id.raw` through a SplitMix64 finaliser, stable, never random per frame, chrome and text together. **The focused card animates to level over ~120 ms and settles back to its angle on blur.** That is the focus affordance: you pick the paper up and square it to write on, and the card being edited is the only square one on the canvas.

**The band is `minimumTiltDegrees` (0.4°) to `maximumTiltDegrees` (1.0°) in either direction, and the gap around zero is provenance rather than looks** *(1C-c3)*. The surface already says "a hand put this here" by leaning, so **straight means Claude**: everything the writer made leans by at least the minimum, and Claude's cards and Claude's regions are drawn at exactly 0°. Without the dead band a seed landing mid-range drew a writer's own card essentially level, which made the reading usually-right — and the failure a usually-right provenance signal invites is the writer trusting a card is theirs when it is not. **Regions lean too since 1C-c3** (they did not before), because a region is the primitive `CanvasClaudePlacement` creates on every call, and it carries no tint at all — the angle is the whole of a region's provenance. `CanvasRenderer.seededRotation` takes the NODE or the REGION, never a bare id: the author is read in the one function the draw pass and the caret's inverse transform both descend from, so an id-only overload would be a second place deciding whether a thing tilts. Pinned by `CanvasRendererTests.test_theTiltBandLeavesTrueZeroToClaude` and `test_claudesCardsAndRegionsAreDrawnExactlyStraight`.

**A Claude card is already level, so the focus affordance is invisible on one.** Accepted, not worked around: §7A.5's promise is that the card being edited is the only *square* one, and inventing a lean to take away would undo the provenance signal for the duration of every visit.

It also makes §7A.2 *easier*: at the moment the editor becomes the visible text, both layouts are unrotated, so the glyph-origin pin compares two axis-aligned layouts and `.rotationEffect` never enters the picture. `rotationEffect(`/`rotate(by:)` are grep-banned across this directory.

**Do not write down the old claim that an `NSTextView` cannot be rotated.** `NSView.frameRotation` rotates a real view and renders it crisply. The editor is level because that is the design, not because AppKit forces it.

Four parts are load-bearing, and each has failed in draft:

- **The editor MOUNTS on the click; it becomes VISIBLE on `isLevel`.** Two properties, and merging them back into one is how this has failed twice, in opposite directions.
  - `CanvasView.mountedEditorNodeID` is just `editingNodeID`: the editor exists, is first responder and takes keystrokes from frame one. Gate the *mount* on the straighten instead and there is no first responder for ~120 ms — double-click empty canvas, type immediately, and **the first character or two reach nothing at all.** §7A.5 allows a beat between click and caret; it does not allow discarded keystrokes.
  - `CanvasView.visibleEditorNodeID` adds `straighten.isLevel(_:)`, and **the same property feeds both `ScrapEditorHost.isEditorVisible` and `CanvasRenderer.draw`'s `visibleEditorNodeID:`** — one value, read once per body pass. So the card keeps drawing its own text (live, off the shared `NSTextStorage`, rotating as it goes) right up to the one frame the editor takes over, and the swap reveals nothing that was not already on screen. Make the editor visible on the click instead and axis-aligned glyphs land at the unrotated text origin over a card still up to `maximumTiltDegrees` off level with the drawn text already suppressed: they snap straight and the card catches up behind them — **§7A.2's failure by §7A.5's own route.**
- **While it is invisible the editor is also transparent to the pointer.** `ScrapEditorContainer.isEditorVisible` drives `alphaValue` *and* `hitTest(_:)`, so through the straighten a click or a pinch reaches `CanvasEventNSView` — whose space is canvas space — rather than being resolved against the editor's unrotated box under a tilted card. That is what keeps `ScrapEditorGeometry.viewPoint`'s "no rotation term" honest: the function is only ever reached from an event the container received, and it receives none while invisible. **Never `isHidden` / `.hidden()`** — AppKit moves first responder off a hidden view, which loses the keystrokes the early mount exists to keep, and (measured) a hidden view leaves the accessibility tree while `alphaValue = 0` does not.
- **`CanvasFocusStraighten.isSettled` means "every card is at ITS target", not "every progress value is 1".** The naive `allSatisfy { $0.value >= 1 }` reports settled the instant focus leaves — the departing entry is still at 1 while its target is now 0 — so `TimelineView` pauses, `step` is never called again, and **the card stays level for the rest of the session** (and nothing else on the canvas animates either). `step(elapsed:)` returns `!isSettled` for a non-positive delta on purpose; do not "fix" that to `false`.
- **The caret index is resolved in the card's unrotated space BEFORE the animation starts**, or the click point moves out from under the cursor. `CanvasRenderer.cardTransform` is the one definition of the card rotation; `localPoint` inverts *it*, never a second hand-written `R(−θ)`. A flipped sign convention doubles the caret error instead of removing it, and a round-trip test passes either way — which is why the grep ban above exists.

## Hit testing, and the resize corner

**Hit testing is on the unrotated rect.** The mismatch band is `r·θ` — about **2.2 pt at the corner of a default 240×80 card** (r = 126.5 pt, θ = `maximumTiltDegrees` (1.0°)), growing with the diagonal and with the tilt. It is not somewhere a writer never aims: it sits exactly where `CanvasRenderer.resizeHandle` draws and `CanvasInteraction.begin` tests. Accepted because 2.2 pt is inside pointer slop and the 14 pt target absorbs it — **but this is the reason `CanvasMaterial.maximumTiltDegrees` has a ceiling.** A calibration round that keeps raising the tilt eventually pushes this band past the target that absorbs it. `CanvasRenderer.cullingBleed` carries the same `r·θ` term for a different reason (a card culled while a corner is still on screen) and `CanvasRendererTests.test_theCullingBleedCoversTheRotationOverhangAtTheCalibratedTilt` re-does that arithmetic on every run.

**A region's ink and its grab diverge further than a card's, and that is the accepted cost of 1C-c3's tilt rather than a bug.** Only a region's *drawing* is rotated — the wash, the chrome bar, the label, the promoted stripe and the resize mark, on a copy of the context inside `drawRegion`. **The grab is the unrotated frame, exactly as before**: `CanvasInteraction` and `CanvasScene.hitTest` still test `CanvasRegionMetrics`' plain rects, unchanged, and no new geometry entered the file. The same `r·θ` band therefore applies, and a region is bigger than a card: ~6 pt at the corner of a 500 × 500 region against a card's 2.2 pt. It stays inside the affordances that absorb it — the chrome bar is 24 pt tall, the resize target 14 pt — and rotating the hit test instead would put a second geometry in the file and grow, rather than bound, the band `maximumTiltDegrees` has a ceiling to keep small. **A tether and a chip are not rotated either**, and for a different reason: one end of each lands on a card *outside* the region, so there is no single transform they belong in. Because a region has no size ceiling and a card effectively does, the flat `cullingBleed` cannot serve both — `CanvasRenderer.rotationOverhang(of:)` widens the region cull per region (`test_aBigTiltedRegionIsNotCulledWithACornerStillOnScreen`).

**The resize TARGET is the whole 14 pt corner square; the MARK is the triangle below its hypotenuse. Both are on EVERY card, of either kind** (the `.scrap` condition on each was 1C-c3's stand-in for a missing measurement pass and 1C-d removed it — see "Card metrics"). One constant (`CanvasRenderer.resizeHandleSize`) fixes the size of both so they cannot drift, and the shapes differ deliberately: a target slightly larger than its mark forgives a near miss, where the reverse would swallow drags the writer aimed at the card. **Do not shrink the target to the ink** — `CanvasInteractionTests.test_theUnmarkedHalfOfTheCornerSquareStillResizes` exists to stop a tidy-up doing exactly that. **And do not put a kind test on one without the other**: the mark and the target are one decision, and the two ways they can drift are a card that resizes with nothing to say so, and a mark inviting a gesture the state machine refuses.

## Regions, and why no *transition* is ever geometry

**A region is a labelled rectangle with two member sets, and CREATION ABSORBS while TRANSITIONS DO NOT.** Moving or resizing a region never adds or removes a member; sweeping a region around cards takes in every card whose **centre** is inside the swept rect, and a scrap made inside a region joins it (Denver, 2026-07-28 — spec §4.2 amendment, ADR 0026 §8). The transition half is the one with three shipped bugs behind it: every tool cited there was bitten deciding whether an *existing* relationship survives a geometry change, which creation cannot be, having no prior state to contradict.

`CanvasMembership` is still the whole mutation surface and **no function in it takes a point, a rect or an overlap** — deciding is the gesture's job (`CanvasInteraction.joinTarget` for a drop or an inside-creation, `CanvasInteraction.absorbedNodes(by:in:)` for a sweep), recording it is this file's, and keeping the two apart is the design rather than a coding style. Absorption skips **unmeasured** cards (no frame, so nothing was drawn to sweep around) and **hidden** ones (residents of a collapsed region, not drawn at all); a card that already lives elsewhere *is* taken in, and `join` moves the home.

**If you are about to write `region.frame.contains(…)` in a MOVE or a RESIZE path, you are reintroducing the bug class this design exists to eliminate.** The two legitimate geometric readings are both at CREATION — `absorbedNodes(by:in:)` for a sweep, `joinTarget(for:in:)` for a drop or an inside-creation — and both read the node's **centre**, never its origin. A `contains(node.origin)` anywhere is wrong on its own terms: it is the corner test §4.2 cites against Obsidian, whichever path it is on.

Three shipping tools get it wrong, three different ways, and the third is the one that matters:

- **Obsidian Canvas** leaves a card poking one pixel outside a group, and out of it.
- **Scapple** recomputes membership from live geometry, so a note inside two overlapping shapes moves with whichever shape you happen to grab. Unfixed.
- **tldraw ejects children when a frame is resized — *despite* storing membership explicitly** ([#6017](https://github.com/tldraw/tldraw/issues/6017)). That is the load-bearing one: **storing membership is not the fix.** The hazard is the geometry→membership *transition rule*, independent of storage, and tldraw has explicit storage and the bug.

`CanvasMembershipTests` is the firewall and it was falsified by introducing tldraw's own defect: make `setRegionFrame` drop members that fall outside the new frame and `test_resizingARegionNeverEjectsMembers` goes red; make `move(_:to:)` join whatever region now contains the node and `test_movingANodeIntoARegionsRectDoesNotJoinIt` goes red.

**One home, many appearances — and copies were rejected outright.** `homeMembers` are the cards that *live* here; `appearances` are cards *cited* here. The two sets are disjoint by construction (`addHome` drops the node from `appearances`; `addAppearance` declines a node that already lives here), because a node in both would draw as a card and as a reference chip at once — §4.3's "you cannot tell which is real". Home uniqueness is enforced in **two** places and they must agree: `CanvasMembership.join` on the mutation path, and `CanvasSceneCodec`'s loader at the disk boundary, where a hand-edited sidecar can hand us a node that is home in two. Both use **first in id order keeps it**, and the loser is *demoted to an appearance* rather than dropped — that region really did cite the card, and inventing or discarding a relationship are both worse than recording the weaker true one. `CanvasMembership.homeRegion` walks `scene.regions` (id-ordered) for the same reason: over `unorderedRegions` it answered differently between runs of *the same binary*, because Swift seeds `Hashable` per process. Measured on the pre-fix code — five runs red, one green. **That test is a deterministic guard against a probabilistic bug; if it is ever seen flaking, somebody reintroduced `unorderedRegions`.**

**Three of 1C-d's routes onto the canvas are a creation and a drop at once** *(spec §8A.1 and §8A.4)* — a research row dragged out of the binder, a photograph dropped from outside, and a capture dragged from the Inbox; see their three sections below. Each reads `joinTarget` and nothing else, so none of them adds a geometric reading to the two this section licenses. **The Inbox's *command* route joins nothing at all**, deliberately, and that is §8A.4's own ruling rather than an omission.

**Every gesture that changes membership is a drop or a creation, and `CanvasInteraction.joinTarget`'s production callers are the list** — `grep -rn joinTarget Maugham/`, plus `absorbedNodes` for the sweep. A card dropped after a move joins; a sweep takes in what it was drawn around; a scrap made inside a region joins it; and each of the three 1C-d drop routes above joins where it landed. At `.ended` of a node **move** — and only a move — the node joins the region whose frame contains **the node's centre**. Centre is predictable and sayable ("drop it so its middle is inside"); a corner is the one-pixel absurdity §4.2 cites against Obsidian. The `kind == .movingNode` filter is not decoration: without it, *narrowing* a card until its centre crosses into a region silently changes its owner — the same failure arriving through the resize path. Ties resolve on one total order: greatest overlap area, then **the smaller region**, then the smaller id. Smaller-region is not arbitrary — it is the rule `regionHit` already uses for the grab, and the grab is the one the writer can see. **Collapsed regions are skipped**: a card dropped inside a collapsed region's frame would join and vanish into `hiddenNodes` in the same gesture, and a card disappearing on drop is the worst failure available on a spatial surface. Refusing leaves it where the writer put it, which is legible.

**Dropping outside every region removes nothing.** Removal is always its own act — ⌫, or the inspector's minus button. The state that leaves behind — a resident sitting visually outside the region that owns it — is **the accepted cost of the whole design, and it is paid in the renderer as a tether**, not paid back by moving the membership.

**Region drags carry residents, never visitors.** `CanvasMembership.residents` also filters against the live scene, so a stale id from a hand-edited sidecar (or from a snapshot the undo has not caught up with) cannot reach the drag loop.

**Collapse hides residents in the SCENE, not in the renderer.** `CanvasScene.hiddenNodes` is recomputed on every structural change and both `topmostNode(at:)` and `nodes(intersecting:)` filter on it, so drawing, culling, hit testing and the accessibility tree agree by construction rather than by a parameter threaded to four callers. `unorderedNodes` deliberately still returns hidden nodes — `CanvasView.rebuildLayouts()` must keep measuring a hidden scrap, or expanding the region shows unmeasured, unclickable cards.

**Citing is an inspector act, not a gesture.** "Appears here" in the region inspector carries a **Cite a Card** menu offering every card not already in the region; `RegionInspector.cite` is the only production caller of `CanvasMembership.addAppearance` **that a writer's hand reaches**, and `RegionBindingTests.test_makingAnAppearanceHasAProductionCaller` is a caller census that keeps it that way. **The census names `RegionInspector.swift` and `CanvasClaudePlacement.swift`** — the second joined in 1C-c3, because a second Claude batch read off a page the canvas already holds cites that page rather than moving it, moving it being the geometry→membership transition §4.2 refuses. **Count the array, not this sentence**, and each name in it carries its reason in the test's own doc comment. Inspector-side rather than a modifier-held drop, deliberately: a modifier flag would have to be threaded through `CanvasEventNSView`'s callbacks — the signature this slice kept stable throughout — for a gesture nobody would discover. **The offer is behind the same `(sceneRevision, regionID)` gate as the member lists and needs it more**: it walks every node in the scene rather than one region's members, so it grows with the canvas rather than with the region. `citeControl` is *handed* the gated snapshot and never reads the `candidates` property; a source scan (`test_theOfferReachesTheViewOnlyThroughTheGatedSnapshot`, with a planted-bypass companion) is what enforces that, because `ForEach(candidates)` compiles, reads correctly and puts a scene-wide walk on every drag frame. **A region with nothing left to offer presents a sentence, not a live menu** — a `Menu` that opens on nothing says a thing is possible and then shows a blank rectangle the writer cannot tell from a bug; the decision lives in `citeAffordance(from:)` rather than in an `if` inside the view, so it is reachable from a test that does not host SwiftUI.

### Dragging research onto the canvas — `CanvasDrop` *(1C-d Task 10, spec §8A.1)*

**The binder's research tree sits beside the canvas in the Plan persona** — `BinderPaneToggle` gives `.research` and `.canvas` one left pane on purpose — **and a row dragged out of it becomes an item node where it is dropped.** The file is untouched; the canvas holds a position (§3.1). Until this, the only producer of an item node in production was `CanvasClaudePlacement`, on Claude's behalf.

**A pure decision plus a model verb, and the split is forced rather than stylistic.** SwiftUI's drop delivery has **no seam a test can post into** — `CanvasEventNSView.applyMouseDown` gives the mouse one, and there is no equivalent for a drag session — so what a drop *means* is `CanvasDrop.decide`, a pure function over the payload, the scene and the item index, tested over the whole product; and *that the modifier is mounted at all* is pinned by the wiring census in `PromotionCommandTests` instead. **Measured, not argued: delete the `.dropDestination` line from `CanvasView.body` and all nine `CanvasDropTests` stay green** while the gesture does nothing. That is this directory's fifth candidate unreachable half, closed by a token rather than discovered by a caller count.

**Everything is refused by not being in `CanvasItemIndex`, and that is the whole validation rule.** The binder drags manuscript pieces and tasks with the same raw-id payload, and a node made for one of those would draw `CanvasItemFacts.missingTitle` — "No longer in the project." — **from birth**, a card the writer can neither fix nor explain. **The corollary is a contract for the next drop source**: a second id space must arrive **prefixed**, because an inbox entry's ULID and a research id are not tellable apart and a bare one would be silently refused here rather than routed.

**A research GROUP is accepted, where `AddCanvasScrapsTool` refuses one.** Not a disagreement: Claude's refusal is about a *source page* a batch was read off, which a folder cannot be, and `item:<groupId>` from that route would be a node nothing else mints. Here the writer is pointing at a folder sitting in their own binder, and Task 4 already ruled that resolving a group is right because *"'no longer in the project' said over a folder the writer can see in the binder is a lie"*.

**A second drop of the same item REVEALS; it never re-creates and never moves.** `CanvasNodeID.item(_:)` derives the id from the reference and `CanvasScene.insert` is keyed by id, so the naive route **overwrites** — falsified by removing the branch: origin, width, z, `promotedItemID` and `author` all lost, and it looks exactly like nothing happening. (Membership survives an overwrite by accident, `insert` writing only `byID`; the test says so rather than claiming a catch it does not make.) Moving the card to the new drop point is not the alternative either — that is the geometry-driven transition §4.2 refuses, and it is why `CanvasClaudePlacement` cites an existing page rather than relocating it. Reveal means **select and bring on screen**, and the camera moves even when the card was already visible: this view has no viewport outside the `Canvas` closure, so "is it visible already" is not a question it can ask.

**The card is born MEASURED at `CanvasCardMetrics.itemLabelOnlyHeight`, and that is a class of bug removed rather than an ordering got right.** `joinTarget` reads the node's CENTRE and returns nil for a node with no `frame`, so a join asked of an unmeasured node silently joins nothing — on every drop, for ever, with nothing red — and an unmeasured node is also dropped by `nodes(intersecting:)` and `topmostNode(at:)`, which is 1C-c3's whole-branch Critical arriving through a new door. `CanvasClaudePlacement` writes the same constant at creation for the same reason. The consequence is stated rather than avoided: the centre the join reads is the label-only card's, so a photograph that has not decoded yet joins by a centre 17 pt below its origin. Awaiting the decode is not available — unbounded, with the writer holding a mouse button. `CanvasView.rebuildLayouts` refines the height on the next pass, **inside the same undo bracket**.

**It is `mutateFromInspector`, and the delivery site is not what tripwire 32 turns on.** A drop *is* delivered to `CanvasView`, which is the argument for the inside verbs — and it is wrong, because what the tripwire turns on is whether the arriving mutation has a bracket **of its own** to protect. **The rule was already written down, in the Undo section**: *"`CanvasView` keeps using `mutate` — everything it does is already inside its own bracket **by construction**."* That qualifier is the discriminator, and a drag beginning in the left column is precisely what does not satisfy it. It is recorded here because a 1C-d task brief read that sentence as being about the *view* rather than about the bracket, and reached the opposite ruling. A drag that starts in the binder never reaches `CanvasView.handleClick`, the only caller of `commitActiveEdit`, so **double-click bare canvas, type, then drag a research row in without touching the canvas again** and the drop lands at depth 1 with nothing on either side able to close "Edit Scrap". **Measured with `mutate`:** the Edit menu still reads "Undo New Scrap" and one ⌘Z takes back the card *and* the writer's sentence together. `CanvasView.deleteSelection`'s refusal is the other answer and is wrong here — mid-gesture a ⌫ is genuinely ambiguous and a drop is not, so refusing would make dragging a note in do nothing at all whenever a scrap was open. The accepted cost is the one every user of this verb pays: the visit is closed into its own step and reopened, so the writer gets one extra ⌘Z boundary mid-sentence. `CanvasDrop.swift` is therefore a tripwire 32 census entry, and `CanvasCapture.swift` (Task 12) joined it for the same reason — **the array is a sorted dictionary literal, so an ordinal here is false on the day it is written**; read the names.

**The first payload only, which is the house pattern.** `ResearchRow`'s own `.dropDestination` takes `ids.first`, and every `.draggable(id)` source sends exactly one — the research tree's multi-selection is expanded by the *receiver* (`ResearchSelectionSync.expandedDragIds`) rather than travelling in the payload. This receiver deliberately does not expand it: the canvas cannot see the binder's selection, and a rule derived from it would be a second answer to "what did the writer drag".

### A photograph dropped from outside — `CanvasExternalDrop` *(1C-d Task 11, spec §8A.1)*

**The second half of the same target, and the node it makes is OWNED rather than referenced.** A research row has a home in the project already and the canvas holds only its position; a photograph from Pictures or from a web page has no home here at all, so it is ingested into `canvas_assets/` through `ProjectStore.ingestCanvasAsset` and the node holds `CanvasItemReference.owned(path:)`. Its id is **minted** — there is nothing to deduplicate, and a path in an identity would put tripwire 22's rename hazard in the one field nothing may rewrite.

**Never `.dropDestination(for: URL.self)`, and this is the rule the whole route exists for.** A browser image drag carries a *rendered bitmap* and no file URL, so that modifier rejects it with CoreTransferable error 0: nothing logged, nothing red, nothing on screen. `[.fileURL, .image]` providers plus `DropClassification` is the only route, the canvas is that type's **fifth** adopter (palette well, three Research zones), and it adds **no classification logic of its own** — it routes the two answers to the two halves of the ingestion pair, which is the palette editor's shape one surface over. `TripwireGrepTests.test_theCanvasExternalDropUsesTheProviderRouteAndNeverAUrlDestination` censuses the required token and bans the forbidden one, with a planted-offender companion, because no runtime test in this repo can see a drop modifier at all.

**The two modifiers are ordered, and the order is load-bearing.** The external `.onDrop` is applied **first** (innermost) and Task 10's `.dropDestination(for: String.self)` after it. A Finder drag also carries a text representation of its path and a browser drag also carries the page URL as text, so both satisfy `String.self` too; innermost, the typed external target claims them. The other way round, the internal router takes the payload, finds no research id in `CanvasItemIndex`, refuses — and the photograph springs back with nothing said. A research row's `.draggable(item.id)` carries neither a file URL nor an image and falls through to the router either way.

**A non-image file is refused before anything is copied, and the hole under it is closed at the saver now** *(M1A Task 12)*. `ImagePasteHandler.saveAndReferenceFile` used to take `sourceURL.pathExtension` as given and validate nothing, so an unchecked `.txt` drop copied into the well, minted an owned node, drew the photograph glyph and queued a decode that could only fail — and `CanvasThumbnails` **memoises failures** and has no `invalidate`, so it was one permanent dead cache entry per mistake. The predicate is now `ImagePasteHandler.isIngestableImage`, **moved there rather than copied** when visual language became a well of its own, so every caller of the shared saver gets it.

**The canvas still calls it before ingesting, and that is defence in depth rather than redundancy.** `ingestCanvasAsset(fileURL:)` would now throw for a `.txt` with nothing upstream — but a throw lands in `Outcome.failed`, whose sentence is *"Couldn't add “notes.txt” to the canvas."*: the sentence for a picture that should have worked and didn't, which tells a writer their surface is broken when in fact they dropped the wrong file. `refused` and `failed` are separate because they are separate facts, and only a check ahead of the call can tell them apart. `CanvasDropTests.test_theCanvasDropStillRefusesItInItsOwnWords` is the assertion, with a throwing seam as its control.

**What the predicate takes is measured, and the comment it replaced was wrong about macOS.** That comment claimed to read *"the file's real type first, its extension second: a file with no extension still has a type on disk"*. Neither half holds (2026-08-01, macOS 26.5): `URLResourceValues.contentType` is derived from the NAME, so a text file called `liar.png` reports `public.png` and a real PNG with **no** extension reports `public.data` — the extensionless drag the ordering was written to serve was the one case it refused. The shape now is a chain of questions, any yes being enough: the OS's type for the name, the extension alone (a URL whose file has not materialised), and — only when the OS said `public.data` — whether an image decoder reads the header. The last is gated rather than asked of everything because ImageIO reads a PDF's first page happily. Every row is pinned by `ImagePasteHandlerTests.test_whatTheSaverWillAndWillNotTakeIntoAWell`.

**Several files cascade at `CanvasClaudePlacement.cardGap`, they do not stack and they do not column.** Two cards at one point read as one card and the writer concludes the others were lost. A column is not available: laying one out needs the cards' heights, and an item card's height is its photograph's, which has not decoded and cannot be waited for while the writer holds a mouse button.

**Await first, touch the scene second** — Swift rather than style, since an `inout CanvasScene` cannot cross a suspension point. `ingest` returns paths and `apply` makes the cards, exactly as `CanvasClaudeWrite` and `PromotionPerformer` split for the same reason. `apply` uses **`mutateFromInspector`** for Task 10's reason, one step sharper: a drag beginning in the Finder has no bracket of its own to protect and nothing on either side of it can close the writer's open "Edit Scrap".

**A failure is an alert, not a fourth transient banner.** Three `.overlay(alignment: .top)` banners already share this window and two on screen at once draw over each other; one banner host for the window is the honest fix and is its own slice. `InboxPane`'s "Couldn't promote" alert over a `@State` `String?` is the nearest precedent, and `ResearchView`'s import alert is the same shape on the same kind of failure. The message **names the files**, because a writer who dragged four photographs in needs to know which one did not arrive.

**`ProjectStore` does not reach this view; two closures do.** `CanvasView.assetIngest` is a `CanvasAssetIngest` whose default `.unavailable` throws — ~70 test hosts have no store, and the routing (which drag reaches which twin) is the only part of this task that `CanvasAssetIngestionTests` cannot already see, so it has to be drivable without a project on disk. The production wiring is the `assetIngest:` token on `ProjectWindow`'s row of `PromotionCommandTests`' wiring census, for the same reason `itemIndex:` is there beside it: a defaulted argument that compiles, runs and silently refuses every photograph. (**Named, not numbered** — that row has grown four times in this slice alone, and every ordinal written into this file about it was stale within a task.) **The closure that reaches this file is censused too** — `CanvasExternalDrop.ingest(` and `CanvasExternalDrop.apply(` on `CanvasView.swift` — because the modifier can be mounted with a body that reaches nothing, which neither the drop tests (they call the router directly) nor the drop-route tripwire (it sees only the modifier) can observe.

### A capture sent from the Inbox — `CanvasCapture` *(1C-d Task 12, spec §8A.4)*

**The pipeline is `inbox → canvas → research`, and only the first arrow was missing** — promotion has been the second since 1C-c2. The store half is `InboxStore.sendToCanvas`, the **third sibling** of `promoteToResearch` and `promoteToPaletteCard`; its ordering, the refusals and which assets it removes are **`Maugham/Stores/AREA.md`'s**, under "Inbox → canvas seam", and are deliberately not restated here.

**Two routes, one path, and they differ in exactly one value.** `CanvasCapture.Placement` is `.dropped(at:)` for the drag — the **third** caller of the drop target, landing the capture where the writer let go, joining by centre — and `.loose` for the command, which has no drop point and takes `CanvasClaudePlacement.looseOrigin`. **`.loose` never joins a region**, and that asymmetry with §8A.2 is the amendment's own ruling rather than an inconsistency: Claude's batches take a region because a derived scrap must stay tied to its source, and a writer sending one capture has already decided what it is. Adding a `joinTarget` there for symmetry is that ruling broken, not a tidy-up. Both facts are on `Landing.joinsARegion` as a *value*, so the rule is driven over the whole product rather than asserted about one path.

**The drag payload is PREFIXED (`inbox:<id>`), which breaks the house's raw-id pattern deliberately.** `ResearchRow` sends `item.id` bare and every existing drop target reads one id space; the canvas is the first that reads two, and an inbox ULID is not tellable apart from a research id — bare, `CanvasDrop.decide` would miss it in `CanvasItemIndex` and refuse the drag *silently*, which is the target built one task earlier refusing the source built by the next. `CanvasDrop.payload` destructures it before anything else runs, and `CanvasDrop.inboxPayload(for:)` is the one place the prefix is spelled.

**`CanvasCapture` is a sibling of `CanvasClaudeWrite`, not a generalisation of it** — that file's name is about Claude, and this is the writer's own act, drawn tilted rather than straight and never announced as Claude's. What they share is `liveModel`, the one place `store.liveCanvas` and `isAttached` are written together, now that there are two writers that must choose between the attached model and the sidecar. `CanvasLiveSeamTests.test_theLiveCanvasDiscriminatorIsSpelledOnce` counts that pair across production, with a planted-offender companion built from this file's own source.

**The command's landing place is off-screen by construction**, exactly as Claude's region is, so §8A.4's amendment rules that it may not be silent. It is told two ways and **neither is a fourth transient banner** — three `.overlay(alignment: .top)` banners already share this window, two on screen draw over each other, and Task 11 declined to add one for the same reason. `CanvasCapture.show` selects the card and asks the camera for it (parked past `attach()` when no canvas is mounted, which is the case the command exists for), and `InboxPane` shows a line naming the capture and the persona, in the pane the writer is already looking at.

## Lines

**A line is an untyped edge between two nodes with an optional free-text name, and it must not gain a `kind`.** Kinopio shipped author-typed connections for years and removed them in April 2026 because "connection types were confusing for people I observed using the tool for the first time" (spec §5, §9). A line costs nothing to draw and nothing to be wrong about, which is what thinking needs; `[[wiki-links]]` remain the durable relationship layer, reached deliberately through promotion.

**Two routes draw one, and they resolve to one `Bool` before `CanvasInteraction` sees anything.** ⇧-drag from any card is the fast route; a drag out of the connect mark on the **selected** card is the findable one. `CanvasView` decides — the mark test needs the selection, and handing that to the gesture layer would give it a second opinion about what is selected. Keeping the routes joined at `connecting:` is what stops the discoverable one and the fast one drifting into behaving differently. `begin(at:in:connecting:)` has **no default** on that argument, deliberately: a default hides the fact that there is exactly one caller. Only `.began` reads it, so a modifier released mid-drag does not abandon the line in progress.

**The modifier is threaded through `CanvasEventNSView.applyMouseDown`, never read off `NSEvent.modifierFlags` at the point of use.** A static global read cannot be driven by a test that sends a real event, and this area has shipped a whole feature nothing could reach.

**The connect mark is drawn only on the SELECTED card**, inside `drawCard`'s `isSelected` block — *not* beside the unconditional `resizeHandle` fill two lines below it, which the two adjacent calls make very easy to get wrong. Hover-revealed handles (the tldraw/Miro/FigJam/Obsidian consensus) were rejected because they need `NSTrackingArea`, `mouseMoved`, a hovered-node state and a redraw per pointer move — new per-frame state on a surface whose architecture is keeping per-frame work viewport-proportional — and because handles blooming on every card the pointer crosses is the diagramming look §5 positions against. Scapple's border-drag was rejected for stealing an eighth of the card from *move*, inside the `r·θ` band, along the edge where the text starts. A **permanent** mark was the closest call, since `drawCard` already inks the resize triangle unconditionally; it lost only because a second always-on mark overstates a thing §5 calls costless to be wrong about.

**The connect target is on the right edge, vertically centred, clamped above the resize square**, both sized from one constant so the mark and the target cannot drift. On a card too short for both, **the corner belongs to resize** — it is the permanent mark, and a target that moved with the card's height would be worse than one sometimes absent. Target ≥ mark, for the reason `test_theUnmarkedHalfOfTheCornerSquareStillResizes` exists.

**The first press on an unselected card's handle position must NOT connect**, and the mechanism is `CanvasView.selectionWhenPressed` rather than a live read. The mark was not drawn there, so the writer aimed at nothing. `applyMouseDown` fires `onClick` before `onDrag(.began)` — a written contract, not an accident — so click-then-press is correctly two gestures. **But the same contract is what makes the obvious spelling wrong:** `handleClick` reassigns `model.selection` to whatever is under the pointer and runs *first*, so a `.began` arm reading `model.selection` live sees the card the press just selected, and every unselected card gets a 14 pt patch on its right edge that draws a line instead of moving it. The selection is captured at the head of `handleClick`, before anything can move it, and read once. **A tidy-up that replaces it with `model.selection` reintroduces exactly that** — which is why it is written here and not only in the property's doc comment.

**The route also requires `topmostNode(at:)` to BE the selected card**, not merely to exist: a card drawn in front hides the mark, and `begin` resolves the source with `topmostNode`, so without that term the writer presses a card they can see and gets a line out of one they cannot.

**`endLine` is called ABOVE `interaction.end()`, never inside the `.ended` chain.** A swept rect survives being read into a `let` because it is a value; a source card does not — `endLine` resolves its source off the live mode, and `end()` has already set that to `.idle` by the time the chain runs, so an `endLine` inside it returns nil on every drag and no line is ever created, silently. The chain carries only the selection assignment. It also means `end()` runs on an idle mode, so a line drag reports no flick by construction.

**Endpoints are node CENTRES** — the same reading `joinTarget` takes for a drop, so the canvas has one answer to "where is this card". A line to an unmeasured node is neither drawn nor clickable: drawing to a guessed position twitches the moment the measurement arrives.

**A line to a resident of a COLLAPSED region is neither drawn nor clickable either**, and the reason it is easy to miss is that the geometry says nothing: a hidden node keeps its frame, and `endpoints(of:)` reads the frame. `CanvasScene.drawnLines` filters on `!isHidden(_:)`, joining `tethers` and `appearanceChips`, which guard the same thing for the same reason. Drawing and hit testing take the identical rule because they read the identical function — a line the writer can click and cannot see is worse than one they can see and cannot click. **The 1C-c1 plan shipped without this and Task 3's review caught it**; it is written here because nothing in the geometry will remind the next author.

**`CanvasLineHit.distance` clamps at BOTH ends.** Without it a click far past either card lands on the infinite line the segment sits on. `line(at:)` takes the NEAREST rather than the first found — two lines leaving one card run within a few points of each other for their whole first stretch.

### One rule: cards over lines over regions

**In drawing and in hit-testing alike.** The click order is the draw order read backwards, and `test_theClickOrderIsTheDrawOrderReadBackwards` asserts the two against each other rather than each against a literal — so a change to one that is not made to the other goes red. Cards beat everything unconditionally, matching `CanvasInteraction.begin`, so a click and a drag never disagree about which thing it was.

**This is not a new rule** — cards already beat region chrome *and* draw above it — which is exactly why lines slot in rather than becoming an exception, and why there is no second rule here to defend.

**Two consequences fall out, and both are the rule rather than exceptions to it.** A press on a line over *bare canvas* no longer starts a region sweep either — the same disagreement in its other location, fixed the same way rather than by special-casing the chrome bar, which would have been the special case wearing the rule's clothes.

**The cost of that, measured and then ruled on.** `CanvasLineHit.tolerance` is 6 pt against 1.5 pt of ink, so every drawn line sits in a **~12 pt corridor where a press does nothing**, and a region sweep must start outside one. **Denver smoked it on 2026-07-28 and called it fine in practice** — the question is closed, not open. Three things make it cheap: only the *press point* is affected (a sweep already under way drags across lines freely), the press still *selects* the line, so the surface says what it grabbed rather than failing silently, and recovery is pressing a few points over. **If it ever does bite, the answer is a smaller `tolerance`** — with the trade named, since 1.5 pt of ink is not aimable without it. **Not** a rule that lets a press on a line start a sweep over bare canvas while selecting the line over a region's bar: that is two meanings for one press, and eliminating exactly that is why the branch above exists. And **no pointer sequence now ends with a line selected and a gesture open**, so a test needing that state opens the bracket directly; the one that does must also probe `begin` at the line, or the hand-driven setup outlives its reason.

**The DRAG takes the same order, and that is not free — it had to be added.** *(Task 5's review, 2026-07-28.)* `CanvasInteraction.begin` originally had no line branch, so a press on a line/chrome crossing selected the line and **dragged the region**: a trackpad click drifts a point, `CanvasUndo.endGesture` registers whenever the state moved, and the writer got the region and every resident nudged plus a "Move Region" step on the stack, while the inspector and ⌫ pointed at the line. A reversal that creates a click/drag disagreement is not "one rule" — it is a special case wearing its opposite's clothes. **A press resolving to a line leaves `mode = .idle`**, which is the honest answer for a thing that cannot be dragged, and the precedent is one case down in the same function (a press inside a region's interior does exactly that).

**An earlier draft of the 1C-c1 plan had it the other way**, giving the chrome bar priority because it is a region's only grab handle. That is true and it is still the wrong answer: it left the line drawn *over* the bar while the bar took the click, which is hit-testing disagreeing with what is visibly frontmost. Splitting `drawRegion` so lines slide under the label does not rescue it either — the bar's tint stays under the visible line. Measured, both losses are negligible: a near-perpendicular crossing costs the line ~24 pt of a length in the hundreds and costs the bar ~12 pt of a width in the hundreds. **If anyone re-opens this, the question is which package they propose *whole*; draw order and click order move together or the defect returns.**

*Open, for a smoke session and not for a unit test:* a hairline at `lineOpacity` crossing a region's 11 pt label, which only happens for a line entering a region from directly above. If it reads as damage, nudge the label.

**A double-click on a line mints nothing.** It fell to `.emptyCanvas` in the first draft and made a scrap under the line the `clickCount: 1` click had just selected — tripwire 32's repro shape arriving through a new door.

### Drawing, culling, and the inset that is defensive

**Lines draw as a pass INSIDE `CanvasRenderer.draw`, between regions and cards** — above the *whole* region pass, which inks the wash, the bar, the label, the collapsed count and the region's resize triangle in one go. The order is the sequence of calls, exactly as it is for the other four passes. **There are no layer-depth constants and adding some would enforce nothing.**

**The draw pass culls** — `visibleLines`, never `CanvasScene.drawnLines`. Lines are the one collection on this surface that nothing bounds.

**The hairline inset in `visibleLines` is DEFENSIVE, and the obvious story about it is false.** Measured 2026-07-28 on macOS 26.5: `CGRect.intersects` is false only for a **null** rect, not an empty one — `CGRect(x: 100, y: 20, width: 440, height: 0).intersects(CGRect(x: 0, y: 0, width: 800, height: 600))` is **true**, so a horizontal line's zero-height box survives culling unaided. What the inset defends against is one spelling over: `!box.intersection(viewport).isEmpty` reads as an obvious equivalent, is false for that same box, and culls every horizontal and vertical line on the canvas — silently, totally, and two cards side by side is the ordinary case. **Do not write the version of this note that says `intersects` fails on an empty rect.** The 1C-c1 plan said exactly that, an assertion written from it was green with the inset deleted, and a rule whose stated reason is false is worse than no rule: the next author tests the reason, finds it holds without the code, and deletes the code.

### The scene owns the projections

**`CanvasScene.drawnLines` and `CanvasScene.selectionTarget(at:)` are one spelling each, and every caller is above them.** Both were scene knowledge parked somewhere else, and each cost a layering inversion the moment a second caller needed it:

- `drawnLines` was `CanvasRenderer.lineGeometry`. It reads node frames and the scene's hidden set and applies no camera, no culling and no appearance, so it is not a drawing concern — and pointing `CanvasLineHit` (a pure geometry enum one layer *below* drawing) at the renderer left a `public` type resting on an `internal` nested one. Sharing it was right; the direction was not. What is genuinely the renderer's stayed there: `visibleLines`, which is about a viewport.
- `selectionTarget` was a `static func` on `CanvasView` that `CanvasInteraction.begin` reached up into, so the press and the click could not disagree about what was under the pointer. Same shape: the gesture state machine asking a SwiftUI `View` for a fact about the scene.

**One is still parked, and it is recorded rather than fixed:** `selectionTarget` calls `CanvasInteraction.regionHit`, which is a pure query over `(point, scene)` living in the gesture file — the same shape a third time. **The cost is the one that justified the lift in the first place:** `begin` calls `scene.selectionTarget` and `selectionTarget` calls `regionHit`, so the two files now reference each other, and `CanvasScene` — the most primitive type here — cannot move to MaughamCore until `regionHit` does. That is exactly the argument for taking `CanvasDrawnLine` off the renderer, so it applies here or it applied nowhere. It is still a strict improvement on pointing at a SwiftUI `View`, and moving it is its own reason rather than that one's — `regionHit` is ~15 lines plus a two-case enum, and `CanvasRegionMetrics` is already `public` and already in `CanvasRegion.swift`, so nothing else has to move with it.

### The rest

**`CanvasScene.lines` sorts on every access and that is currently safe**, unlike `nodes`. If a canvas ever makes it measurable the fix is an `unorderedLines` peer, exactly as the `nodes`/`unorderedNodes` split — not a cache. **`lineCount` is the peer of `count`** and exists for the same reason: `CanvasAccessibility.summary` is read from `body`.

**Deleting a node deletes its lines** (`CanvasScene.remove`), and self-lines are rejected in `insertLine` — the codec goes through `insertLine`, so that rule has one definition.

**The label is edited in the RIGHT-HAND COLUMN through `mutateFromInspector`**, never in a sheet and never through `mutate`. The census in `TripwireGrepTests` names `LineInspector.swift` for that reason.

**Whitespace is no name, and as of 1C-c3 there is ONE trim in three places.** `LineInspector.normalise`, `CanvasAccessibility.connectionPhrase` and `CanvasRenderer.lineLabelBox` all trim `.whitespacesAndNewlines`. The renderer trimmed `.whitespaces` — space and tab only — until 1C-c3 widened it, so a label of `"\n"` drew an **empty pill** on the line while the inspector called the same string no name and the accessibility layer said nothing about it. The agreement was reached by **fixing the drawing, not by announcing it**: "announce what is drawn" was the tempting precedent and the wrong one, because what was drawn in that case is the defect `normalise`'s own doc comment describes — visible, unreadable, and removable only by finding the field and clearing it twice.

**Two claims this file made about that widening were wrong, and both are worth keeping as corrected rather than deleted.** It said the widening *"wants a raster fixture rather than a one-word edit"* — it does not: `lineLabelBox` is a `static func` returning a `CGRect` from a value, so a unit assertion over `"\n"`, `"\t"` and `" "` covers it exactly, which is what `CanvasLineRenderTests.test_aWhitespaceOnlyLabelDrawsNoPill` is (with the disable experiment run: restore `.whitespaces` and the newline cases go red while the others and the control stay green). And it said **1C-c3 is the slice that makes the case reachable** — it is not. `add_canvas_scraps`' `connect` carries no label at all (ruling 2, an untyped edge with no name), so nothing in that slice writes a line label, and **the only route in is still a hand-edited `.maugham/canvas.json`**, since `CanvasSceneCodec` does not normalise labels on load. The widening was taken anyway, on that narrower ground: the route is real, and it is a one-word fix. *A prediction about a future slice is not evidence about it; the shape to copy is checking what the slice actually wrote.*

**Clearing a label is its own undo step, `"Clear Line Label"`** — not "Label Line" again. `CanvasLine.label` is `String?` and the renderer skips the pill entirely on nil, so clearing reaches a genuinely different state. This is `commitBinding`'s Bind/Unbind precedent, and it does **not** transfer from `RegionInspector.commitLabel`, which says "Rename Region" both ways because `CanvasRegion.label` is non-optional with a placeholder and has no clear-state to name.

**The pane names no endpoints, and the reason is scope, not impossibility.** `chipTitle` × 2 ungated would put a whole-scrap-text read on a body that re-evaluates every drag and coast frame — tripwire 30's shape. **A gated route does exist** and is used one file over: `RegionInspector` caches its scene-proportional work in `@State` behind `(sceneRevision, regionID)`, and a caption keyed on `(sceneRevision, lineID)` would be the same shape. Do not record this as "there is no gated way"; the next author will believe it.

**Which arm the pane renders cannot be asserted** — `_ConditionalContent`'s type is branch-invariant — so it is pinned by a caller census plus each resolver's behaviour. Arm *order* cannot be wrong: `selection` is one enum, so `selectedRegion`, `selectedLine` and (since 1C-c2) `selectedNode` are mutually exclusive by construction. **The pane has three arms and an empty state as of 1C-c2** — a selected card used to fall into the empty state, which was right while a scrap had nothing to say about itself; the promoted mark is what changed that. The one stronger instrument available is a hosted render diff through `ImageRenderer`, which this area already does for the canvas; slow and appearance-sensitive, and not worth it here.

**A line drag that minted nothing is not a structural change**, and it shares the sweep's guard rather than growing a second one.

## Promotion

**A promotion is a SNAPSHOT, and the mark it leaves is provenance rather than a link.** `CanvasNode.promotedItemID` and `CanvasRegion.promotedItemID` record what a thing produced; nothing syncs afterwards in either direction, and there is no reconciler because none is promised. **The region row is the one-sentence reason**: promoting a region joins six cards' text into one palette card while all six stay on the canvas, so a copy at a moment is already what §6 describes — a scrap that moved its words *out* would give one verb two rules. A promoted card and its note drifting apart is the design (spec §6.1's 2026-07-28 amendment), not a defect. See [ADR 0026 §9](../../docs/adr/0026-planning-canvas-rendering.md).

**The pure half and the impure half are two files, and the line between them is load-bearing.** `Promotion` never mutates anything: it reads the scene and returns a `PromotionPlan`, which is what makes the preview honest (`test_planningNeverMutatesTheScene`). `PromotionPerformer` is `@MainActor async throws` **with no `inout` anywhere on the path**, and that is a fact about Swift 6 rather than a style choice — `ProjectStore`'s creation APIs are all `async throws`, and an `inout CanvasScene` cannot cross an `await`. Scene changes therefore happen through `CanvasModel`, synchronously, after the awaits. **Validate first, write second**: a refused promotion leaves nothing behind, and a half-created artifact on a surface whose whole promise is predictability is worse than a refusal.

**Tripwire 32's census names `PromotionPerformer.swift` because of this section, and `ScrapInspector.swift` because of 1C-c2a.** Every scene change `PromotionPerformer` makes is `mutateFromInspector`, never `mutate` — this is not `CanvasView`, and **the sheet can be opened, and its mark written, while a focused scrap still holds "Edit Scrap" open** — the Inspector's Promote button is reachable mid-visit, which is tripwire 32's own repro shape: double-click a region's chrome bar so a scrap is minted and focused, then promote from the pane. Nested, the mark would register no undo step of its own and would ride into the writer's next sentence, so a ⌘Z aimed at a sentence takes the mark with it. `ScrapInspector`'s Piece picker joined for the same reason and is reached by a **different** gesture, which is worth stating because this file spends four paragraphs elsewhere warning that a double-click on a card is *not* the region arm's repro. It is exactly this arm's. `handleClick` assigns `model.selection` **inside** the `guard clickCount >= 2 else` branch, so click 1 selects the card and puts this pane on screen, and click 2 opens "Edit Scrap" without touching the selection — the pane stays, its Picker live over a bracket nothing on that side of the window closes. The chrome-bar repro cannot reach it at all: with a region selected, `RegionInspectorPane` renders the region arm and `selectedNode` is nil by construction. **Each arm has its own repro, and neither one's is the other's.** `TripwireGrepTests` names the files — **count that array rather than this sentence**: the last slice shipped a "four files" claim over a five-entry array one directory over, and it survived three passes because no test guards an `AREA.md`.

**The mark cannot be validated where it is stored, and never could** — the scene has never seen the manifest. `ArtifactIndex` (item id → title **and kind**, built once when the sheet opens) is what resolves one, and it is *passed in* rather than reached for: that is what keeps `Promotion` pure and testable, and it walks the manifest once rather than per query. The dangling case — the writer deleted the note — is not an error state: the inspector says what the thing produced is no longer in the project, and a line whose end has gone stops offering a wiki-link at all and **says which of the two it is**, because "Promote both cards first" told a writer who had already promoted one to do the thing they did.

**The KIND on that index is what stops one promotion destroying another's artifact.** A mark records an item id and nothing else, and palette cards and craft-intent docs are ordinary `.document` research items — so with titles alone *every mark resolved for every updatable target*. Promote a card to a palette card, promote the same card again as a research note, and the sheet offered "Rewrite “Act II fog”" over a preview reading "Goes to: the existing “Act II fog”", both sentences true; committing renamed the palette card's backing file and wrote raw scrap text over it, and the swatches, the kind, the sensory notes and the `<slug>_assets/` image references were gone with only the mark on the ⌘Z stack. The craft-intent variant replaced the writer's whole accumulated intent statement, which is exactly what excluding `.intentStatement` from `updatableTargets` exists to prevent, arriving through the other door. **The kind is read from the manifest rather than stored beside the mark** — deliberately: it answers what the artifact *is now*, so a card whose artifact was later moved or converted is covered too, and it needs no sidecar bump. `PromotionTarget.producedArtifactKind` is the other side of the comparison, and a mismatch **declines the update** rather than refusing the promotion — a duplicate note is a cost the writer can undo and their palette is not. `performResearchNote`'s `.update` branch re-checks it against the live manifest as well, because the plan is a snapshot and the artifact can change under it.

**Only two of the six targets ask the writer for a name** (four until 1C-c2a took the piece binding off the row and 1C-d added the two picture rows — count the enum, not this clause), and `PromotionTarget.namesItsArtifact` is the one place that is decided. `performWikiLink` never reads `plan.title` and neither does `performCraftIntent` — the intent doc is find-or-create at a fixed title with the body appended — so for a whole slice the sheet showed a `Name` field seeded from *someone else's* title that changed nothing, and clearing it disabled Promote with "This needs a name." for an act that names nothing. The performer's `validate` and the sheet's `canCommit`/`refusal` read the same flag, or the two disagree about what a valid plan is. **It is deliberately not spelled `updatableTargets.contains(_:)`**, which happens to be the same set today.

**Four selections can reach the sheet with nothing to offer, and `Promotion.blockedReason` answers all four.** It used to answer only the line. An **empty scrap** is offered all three targets — emptiness is not a targets question — and then `plan` returns nil, so `preview`, `resolvedPlan` and `refusal` were nil together and the writer met an empty Name field, no destination, no message and a dead button; it now returns `PromotionFailure.emptyBody`'s own sentence rather than a second wording of it. An **item node** offered `[]` and said nothing; `Promotion.itemNodeReason` is the sentence, held as a constant because three places now reason about an item node's mark (the renderer, the accessibility label, this). That is also why `blockedReason` takes `scraps`: emptiness is the one fact about a card the scene does not hold.

**The fourth is an EMPTY REGION, and it is the scrap's own defect arriving one row down** *(fixed 2026-07-29, whole-branch review)*. The region arm returned nil unconditionally and `plan` had no emptiness guard, so a region with no residents — or residents that are all empty scraps — previewed an empty body with Promote enabled and threw `emptyBody` at Commit, in the **card's** noun. It is pre-existing and 1C-c2a is what made it matter: `.researchNote` is the headline verb on that row now, so "a cluster of scraps is a note" is the thing a writer tries on a region they have only just drawn. `Promotion.regionBodies` is one spelling that `plan` and `blockedReason` both read, or the two agree about "empty" by coincidence rather than by construction; and **`PromotionFailure.emptyBody` carries its `PromotionSource`** so the noun comes from `PromotionSource.noun` rather than from a literal. (That is the second spelling of card/region here — `PromotedArtifactSection.Subject.noun` is the view's, and what `Subject` enumerates is **who renders that SECTION**, not who has a pane. *Every* arm has a pane, including the line's; this sentence said "the two subjects that have a pane" for two slices, which is the false-**reason** class rather than a stale count, and a reason is what the next implementer acts on. `Promotion.swift`'s own comment on `PromotionSource.noun` carries the correction; count `Subject`'s cases.)

### The fourth row — an owned picture promotes *(1C-d Task 8, spec §6's 2026-07-30 amendment)*

**A REFERENCED item node is still refused and an OWNED one is not, and the refusal below is what that amends.** Until 1C-d every item node was a reference — already in the project, already the artifact, with nothing left to produce — so refusing all of them was right. A capture is the case it was never about: the photograph exists nowhere but the canvas, and the inbox entry it came from is `.promoted` and gone, so the pane's own **Promote to Research** is not a fallback either. That is what made §8A.4's *"promotion is already the second arrow"* true of two capture kinds out of three.

**The two destinations are the inbox's own, because it is the same object one hop later**: `ProjectStore.createResearchAsset(scope:fromURL:)` and `addImage(toPaletteCard:fileURL:)` — the ingestion pair §3.1's amendment names, with the canvas as one more *caller*. **There is no intent-statement row**: an intent is prose about how a piece is written, and a photograph is not a sentence.

**It is a SNAPSHOT and the file is COPIED, and the node stays owned.** Handing the file to research and turning the card into a reference was considered and rejected in the spec: it leaves no duplicate on disk, and it makes promotion the one verb on this surface that MOVES — a writer who promotes and then presses ⌘Z would be relying on a file move to reverse. `test_theCanvasKeepsItsPictureAndItsFile` asserts the original in `canvas_assets/` and the node's kind after.

**The research row writes the MARK; the palette row writes a CONTRIBUTION and never the mark.** This is §6.3's distinction arriving on a new row rather than a new idea, and it is the guard against the failure the amendment names by name: promote a photograph onto a palette card, promote it again, and be offered **Rewrite** over a card holding swatches, sensory notes and other images. `promotedItemID` means *"I am this artifact"* and `Promotion.existingArtifact` reads it — only it — to offer that. What is true of an appended picture is that it is *in* the card, alongside whatever else is. Three guards, and each is independent: neither picture row is in `updatableTargets`, `PromotionTarget.producedArtifactKind` answers `.researchAsset` on one row and nil on the other so no updatable target's kind can match a picture's mark, and the palette row leaves no id in that field at all.

**`ArtifactKind` gained `.researchAsset` for the middle of those three**, and it is not decoration: every `.png`, `.pdf` and recording in `research/` answered `.researchNote` until 1C-d, which was harmless only while no target produced a file. With one that does, a mark naming a picture matching the research-note row is `performResearchNote`'s update branch **renaming that file and writing a card's prose into it** — the 1C-c2 Critical in a new extension. `refuseIfNotAResearchNote` reads it too.

**`PromotionPerformer.recordPicture` stamps WITHOUT clearing, and `record` clears before it stamps.** A region's note is rewritten from its current members, so a departed card must stop claiming it; an image well is *appended to*, and the card's earlier pictures are still in it. Routed through `record`, the second picture promoted onto a card would erase the first one's record — a false sentence in a pane, which is the bug §6.3 exists to remove. `PromotionPlan.contributors` is therefore `[]` on both picture rows, and the record is written from the plan's `PromotedPicture` instead.

**A palette card now has TWO producers with different semantics, and `record`'s clear had to learn the difference** *(fix round 1, M1)*. A region rewrites its card from its current members, so it clears every record naming that artifact before it stamps; a picture is *appended*, so `recordPicture` stamps and never clears. Scoped to the artifact id alone the two collide, reachably and silently: promote region R to card P, promote a picture onto P, re-promote R with **Update**, and the picture's record is cleared while its image is still in P's well. The clear skips item nodes. `CanvasNodeKind.isScrap` is that predicate and is deliberately **not** `carriesAMark`: one asks what a node IS, the other whether a *mark* on it means anything, and they differ on exactly the kind this task added.

**Task 12a changed that skip's REASON without changing the predicate, and the reason is what the next author will act on.** It was "this path can never have stamped one" — `contributors` came from `regionBodies`, which reads the scrap table and cannot see a picture. That is now false: a region's palette promotion carries the pictures in it and records every one. The skip is nevertheless still right, for a stronger reason — **a picture's contribution is never undone by a rewrite, because the image well is append-only for every producer.** `performPaletteCard`'s update branch replaces the title, the kind and the BODY and carries `current.imagePaths` across untouched, and `addImage` appends; so a picture that has since left the region still has its image on that card, while a card whose words have left really has had them written out of the body. The record follows what the artifact still CONTAINS, which is the rule `record` is an expression of — the id scope is the same rule on the axis the kind cannot see. **The STAMP is deliberately unfiltered**: an item node in `contributors` is a picture this promotion just copied, and it must record that.

### What a promotion records on the cards it consumed *(1C-c2b, spec §6.3)*

**A card whose words a region's promotion folded into a note carries a record of that, and the record is NOT the mark.** The writer's report after the 1C-c2a smoke: *"not all the scraps know they were promoted, some think they weren't — all did turn up in the research note though."* Their text was in the note; their inspectors said **"Not promoted yet."** The ones that did say promoted were the ones promoted individually earlier, carrying marks of their own. Two truths on one screen, and the screen was lying.

**Why the record is not the mark, which is the sentence that stops the next author merging them.** `CanvasNode.promotedItemID` means *"I am this artifact"* and `Promotion.existingArtifact` reads it — **only** it — to offer **Rewrite**. Stamp a contributor with the same field and promoting one member afterwards offers to rewrite the whole six-card note with that one card's text: the 1C-c2 Critical (a mark that did not record its artifact's *kind*) returning as a mark that does not record its *cardinality*. So `contributedToItemID` is a second field with its own type at the surface (`PromotedArtifactSection.ContributionState`), its own sentences, and **no route into `existingArtifact`** — pinned by `PromotionContributionTests`, which was falsified by adding exactly that fallback and watching the no-Update assertion report `update(itemID: res-fog)`. **If you are about to make `existingArtifact` fall back to this field when the mark is nil, that is the defect.**

**Who is recorded, and when.** `PromotionPlan.contributors` — the home members whose CONTENT actually went in, in one reading order. A member with an empty scrap contributes nothing and is not recorded. **Recorded at promotion time and never derived from live membership**: a card added to the region afterwards has no words in that note and must not claim to. A scrap plan and a line plan have no contributors at all.

**"Content" is words *or* picture, and that is spec §6.3's 2026-07-31 amendment rather than a widening somebody chose** *(1C-d Task 12a)*. Denver's words: *"they should report their promotion in the same way as the text scraps."* It was read from `Promotion.regionBodies` alone — which reads the scrap table and **structurally cannot see a picture** — so once a region's palette promotion started carrying the pictures in it, a photograph went into the card and its own node recorded **nothing**, with its pane saying *"Not promoted yet"* while its picture sat in the artifact. Word for word the failure this section was written to answer, arriving through the one door the original ruling did not cover. Everything else here is unchanged and binding: `contributedToItemID` and never the mark, no route into `existingArtifact`, at promotion time, neither drawn nor announced.

**One gesture, one undo step.** `PromotionPerformer.mark` writes the source's own mark and every contribution record inside a **single** `mutateFromInspector` bracket, so one ⌘Z takes back the whole promotion's canvas-side effect. Proven by opening a second bracket: the one-⌘Z test goes red with the region still naming the artifact after the undo and a second "Promote Region" left on the stack.

**An update re-records, clear-then-stamp.** `PromotionPerformer.record` clears `contributedToItemID` on every node naming *this* artifact and then stamps the current contributors, because the note is rewritten from the region's current members. A card that has left stops claiming it; one that joined starts. The clear is **scoped to the artifact**, so this promotion cannot wipe a record naming somebody else's note; on a `.new` promotion the id is freshly minted and the clear matches nothing. **The record is single-valued, though, and the most recent contribution wins — the scope limits the CLEAR, not the STAMP.** Card X lives in region A, A is promoted so X records A's note; X is dragged into B (`join` moves the home, and there is only ever one), B is promoted, and the stamp overwrites X's record. A's note was never rewritten, so X's words really are in both and the pane names only the later one. That is intended and it is the honest cost of one field: a *set* would put a growing, never-collected list of danglable ids on every node to describe a snapshot taken once, and provenance reads most usefully as the most recent act. **Do not read the scoped clear as a promise that another region's promotion cannot cost you the record** — it was written that way for a slice, and `PromotionContributionPerformerTests` now pins the overwrite so the next author meets a decision rather than a bug. Stamping alone would leave the departed card pointing at a note its words are no longer in — §6.3's own false sentence, one promotion later.

**The pane shows both records and never chooses.** A card may have produced its own note *and* have its words in a region's, and they say different things. `PromotedArtifactSection.Provenance` carries both; the body is **two straight-line `switch`es rather than an `if`/`else`**, so "show both" is structural and not a convention a tidy-up can invert. The one place the two genuinely interact is `Provenance.saysNotPromotedYet`, which is true only when *neither* record exists — that boolean **is** the reported bug, and it is a value rather than an `if` inside `body` because `_ConditionalContent` is branch-invariant and a `Form`'s contents are not inspectable. A disable experiment on it (`artifact == .notPromoted` alone) turns `test_aContributingCardStopsSayingNotPromotedYet` red.

**The record dangles the same way the mark does, and it is resolved through the same index.** Records persist through the codec and are **never rebuilt on load**, so a card really can name a note the writer has since deleted. `ScrapInspector` resolves both ids through the deferred `artifactTitle` lookup it already held, so a deleted note produces a sentence rather than a raw id — `PromotedArtifactSection.contributionArtifactMissing`, which carries no id, because an id is not something the writer can read.

**The sentences are deliberately different, and neither names a count.** `Subject.became` says *Became “…”*; `wordsAreIn` says *Its words are in “…”*. One sentence for both would be the pane inviting the rewrite §6.3 forbids. Neither says "along with the others": a region's contributors are whoever had text at promotion time, sometimes one card, so that clause would be false exactly when the region was smallest. The caption under the contribution line is where the writer learns that Promote… from here makes something **new** and never rewrites what they are looking at — the fact they need at the moment they are looking at it.

**A contribution gets no drawn mark and no VoiceOver term, and that is the design.** The stripe (`CanvasMaterial.promotedMarkWidth`) means *this produced that*; a second identical stripe for *this went into that* would say on the canvas exactly the thing §6.3 spends its length separating, and the writer would have no way to tell which one they were looking at. The record is an inspector fact. **`CanvasRenderer` and `CanvasAccessibility` do not read `contributedToItemID`** — if a future slice wants it drawn, it needs its own visual language and its own announcement, not the mark's. **Spec §6.3 now carries this ruling**, which it did not at first: a smoke session cannot overturn what a smoke session cannot perceive, so the authority had to say the record is inspector-only and unannounced before anyone could amend it. **This is one of the surface's two deliberate drawn/spoken divergences and the other runs the opposite way** — a Claude item node is announced and never tinted; see "What is SPOKEN" under the material, which states the single rule both follow.

### The piece association — where a promotion lands *(1C-c2a, spec §6.2)*

**One optional field on both primitives, and it is the region's own binding.** `CanvasRegion.boundPieceID` shipped in 1C-b as §4.4's bridge; `CanvasNode.boundPieceID` joined it in 1C-c2a, and the field now has a **second reader that exists today**: where a promotion lands. **The reference rail is M2's, not 1A's** (umbrella §10 — the intent strip, pinned references and the assistant column), and the sentences in this area saying otherwise were written in 1C-b and corrected in M1A Task 10. ## `⌘\` gives the canvas the window *(1C-d Task 13, spec §8A.3)*

None of this lives in this directory — it is all `ProjectWindow.swift` — but it is the one keystroke that changes what the canvas looks like from outside it, so it is recorded here.

**`.doubleColumn`, and the value that reads as obviously right is the one that breaks it.** The canvas is the **content (middle) column** — `CanvasView` is inside `contentColumn` — so `NavigationSplitViewVisibility.detailOnly`, the case that sounds like "everything else away", hides the canvas itself. What leaves the canvas the whole window is `.doubleColumn` (sidebar hidden) *together with* `showInspector == false`, which already renders the detail column empty. `ProjectWindow.canvasCollapse` is the decision as a pure function and it carries the visibility **in its payload**, so a test has to name the case it expects: "not `.all`" is satisfied by the trap.

- **The same key, the same event, no second subscription.** `⌘\` posts `.maughamToggleNoChrome` once and `FocusPostureModifier` receives it; `CanvasCollapseModifier` watches the *flag* that handler flips. A second `.onKeyWindowCommand` for one name in one window is tripwire 21's territory.
- **`⌘⇧F` is a second entry and it is INTENDED.** `toggleFullScreen` sets `isNoChromeOn = true` on the way in, so full-screen focus collapses the canvas too. Watching the flag rather than the keystroke is what makes both entries one behaviour; a guard would have to name `⌘\` specifically and would then owe a second exit rule for the leaving-full-screen path. Documented in `docs/guide/editor-and-focus.md` and the shortcut table.
- **The wall and the canvas share an update pass, and the collapse TAKES OVER the wall's memory.** Plan's binder offers Palette and Canvas side by side (`Persona.plan.binderSegments`), so palette → canvas in focus mode is **one click** — no persona switch, no synchronous handler to sequence them — and `PaletteSegmentModifier`'s exit arm and `CanvasCollapseModifier`'s collapse both fire on that one `binderSegment` change in an order SwiftUI picks. Left alone the orders disagree: wall-first remembers the writer's real pre-wall visibility, collapse-first remembers the wall's forced `false` and then the exit arm **reopens the pane against the collapse**. So `canvasCollapse` takes `paletteStash` and stashes `paletteStash ?? showInspector`, declaring `takesOverPaletteStash`, which makes the exit arm's conditional restore a no-op. **Both orders now end in the same state**, and `CanvasCollapseTests.test_thePickerFromTheWallToTheCanvasEndsTheSameWayInEitherOrder` runs the pair both ways round and compares the window state *whole*. The wall's rule is extracted to `ProjectWindow.applyPaletteSegmentChange` for exactly that reason — an order test needs both halves callable.
- **Never automatic on entering the persona**, in §8A.3's own words. The collapse is a function of *(the centre is the canvas)* AND *(the writer asked for focus mode)* — you need the binder open to drag research and captures onto the canvas, and only then do you want it gone. `PaletteSegmentModifier` does auto-hide on entry and the canvas deliberately does not follow it.
- **Off the canvas nothing is written at all** — not even `.all` over a sidebar the writer dragged shut by hand. `⌘\` in the editor still touches only the titlebar and the three standard buttons, exactly as it did.
- **The stash is `PersonaModifier`'s business too, and for the palette's exact reason.** `CanvasCollapseModifier`'s `.onChange(of: binderSegment)` fires in a *later* update pass than the persona handler, so its release arm would restore the stashed visibility *over* that handler's unconditional `showInspector = true` — a writer who had closed the inspector before collapsing would land in the new persona with it closed, unlike every other persona switch. `PersonaModifier.releasesCanvasCollapse(from:to:stash:)` drops the stash **and hands the sidebar back** in the same synchronous pass, beside `clearsPaletteStash`. Dropping the stash alone would leave the binder hidden in a persona with no canvas to justify it. **Extend that predicate; never defer a pass** (tripwire 2).
- **The stash is also the "already collapsed" flag**, which is what makes the fold idempotent: both triggers fire together on a reopen that restores focus mode *and* the canvas from `UIState`, and a second collapse would stash the `false` the first one wrote and hide the inspector for good.
- **Known interaction, deliberately not special-cased, and it is true of ONE of the two cases.** A writer in focus mode who clicks **Show** on Claude's arrival banner: *arriving from another segment*, `binderSegment` changes, the collapse runs a pass later and the force-open closes again (camera move and selection still happen; `⌘\` brings the naming back). *Already on the collapsed canvas*, the segment does not change, neither trigger fires, and the pane stays open beside the collapse. The case that loses the naming pane is the jump, not the local reveal. **Slice 2 moved one case across that line rather than adding a third**: a writer on `.tree` is now left on `.tree` (see the arrival section below), so their segment does not change either and they get the local-reveal behaviour.

## Composition and layering

**Layer order: ground → drawn nodes → events → editor (frontmost).** With the event view in front of the editor, click-to-place-caret, drag-select and double-click-word all die and the surface reads as "typing does nothing". `CanvasCompositionTests` pins the order.

**The ground is a sibling BENEATH the content, never an overlay.** A shader applied *over* a subtree containing an `NSViewRepresentable` logs a console warning and renders a grey placeholder (§7A.4) — and this view has two of them. So `ZStack { CanvasGround(...); content }`, never `content.background { CanvasGround(...) }` and never `.colorEffect` on anything containing `CanvasEventView` or the editor. Nothing is thrown and nothing goes red; you get a placeholder and a warning.

Pass the ground the **same live camera `@State`** the draw pass uses. A stale or default camera makes the grain crawl under the cards as you pan, and `CanvasGroundTests` constructs its own cameras so it cannot see that.

**`CanvasScene.nodes` sorts on every access.** Per-frame and per-`body` callers use `count` or `unorderedNodes`; `topmostNode(at:)` and `nodes(intersecting:)` filter first and order the survivors — and both drop residents of a collapsed region, which is why collapse is one rule in the scene rather than one per consumer (see Membership above). `CanvasAccessibility.summary` reading `scene.nodes.count` from `body` was a 2,000-element sort per body evaluation.

## The material — `CanvasMaterial`

**Every number the canvas's *look* is calibrated with lives in `CanvasMaterial.swift`, and nowhere else.** The look is tuned by eye against the running app by the writer, so a constant he wants to move must be findable without reading a Metal file: `CanvasGround.metal` holds no material literals at all and takes grain amplitude, lamp depth, lamp floor and lamp reach as uniforms. **Do not inline a number back into the shader** — a shader constant is a constant nobody can find, and this whole seam exists because the first calibration round could not.

**Quoting one of those numbers in THIS file has a required notation, and a test.** Write the constant as an inline code span immediately followed by its value in parentheses — `` `maximumTiltDegrees` (1.0°) ``, `` `lightBase` (0.930, 0.915, 0.880) `` — and `DocSyncTests.test_theCalibrationFiguresInTheCanvasAreaFileMatchTheirConstants` parses those pairs out and compares each against the literal in `CanvasMaterial.swift` / `CanvasNode.swift`. **The incident is the reason**: a stale `1.2°` in this file and four others was read as current, went into the option preview Denver chose the tilt band from, and gave him a wrong upper bound for a decision he then made. It was found by accident, by an implementer sweeping unrelated drift. Prose counts here have a mitigation that works for the next *author* ("count the array, not this sentence"); a wrong number reaches the *writer*, because this file is where he comes to find a knob. **The guard asserts nothing about the prose** — that is where this file's value is and where no test belongs — and it only sees figures written in the notation, which is also what makes a stale one findable by eye.

| lighter / darker ground | `lightBase`, `darkBase` |
| more / less card separation | `darkCardPaper` up, or `darkBase` down |
| more / less tilt | `maximumTiltDegrees` |
| how far a human thing must lean before **straight can mean Claude** | `minimumTiltDegrees` (the dead band around zero; bounded above by `maximumTiltDegrees`) |
| how strongly a card says **Claude put me here** | `lightClaudeCardPaper`, `darkClaudeCardPaper` (cooler than the writer's paper, and darker in dark) |
| how strongly a line says it | `lightClaudeLineStroke`, `darkClaudeLineStroke` |

**Light and dark are two materials, not one texture inverted** (§7.1: "Dark: slate under a lamp… Paper is a light-mode idea"). Every knob that differs is a *pair*, and `CanvasGroundTests.test_theTwoAppearancesAreCalibratedSeparately` stops a tidy-up collapsing them — light was signed off on 2026-07-26 and dark was recalibrated on 2026-07-27; putting dark's hotter grain on light undoes an approval.

Three constraints bind any further calibration:

- **The card must stay lighter than the ground in both appearances, including at peak grain.** A card darker than its surface reads as a hole cut out, not an object resting on it (§7.2) — as fatal as unreadable text. Pinned by `test_theCardIsLighterThanTheGroundInBothAppearances`, which reads the grain amplitude, so raising the texture has a ceiling and the test names it.
- **Author colours in sRGB.** `NSColor(calibratedRed:)` with the same digits resolves ~30% lighter and the lift is invisible in the source. `test_theGroundResolvesToTheValuesItIsAuthoredWith` and `test_theCardPaperIsTextBackgroundInLightAndItsOwnValueInDark` pin the resolved components against the literals.
- **`cardPaper` is `textBackgroundColor` in LIGHT ONLY.** Dark takes a dedicated `darkCardPaper` (0.235, 0.232, 0.226): the system colour is 0.118 in dark, which is *below* the raised ground and turns every scrap into a hole. `cardInk` is still `labelColor` (white in dark) and is the ceiling on how light the dark paper may go — `test_theCardsInkContrastsWithItsPaperInBothAppearances` says when.

**Whose hand made a thing is said with the MATERIAL and with the TILT, not with a fourth mark** *(1C-c3, spec §8A.2 constraint 1)*. A card Claude put down takes a cooler paper and a line Claude drew strokes in a correspondingly cooler value — same ink, same shape, same hairline weight, so "Claude's" is one thing to learn rather than three. `drawCard`'s adjacency warning already covers three marks and a fourth of the same family is what §6.3 spends its length arguing against. The tilt carries the rest (see "Focus straightens the card"), and it costs no colour budget at all, which matters because light mode has very little.

**The two signals answer different questions, and the item node is where they diverge.** The tint says *whose words these are*; the tilt says *who put this here*. For the page a batch of scraps was read off, those differ — Claude minted the node and chose its place, and the words on the page are the writer's own photograph. So a source item node carries `author == .claude` (`CanvasClaudePlacement`) and is drawn **straight and untinted**: `CanvasRenderer.seededRotation(for:)` reads the author, and `CanvasRenderer.paper(for:)` refuses to tint an item node *whatever* its author says, on the same already-exists-as-itself terms as the promoted stripe. **`author = nil` is not the place to record "do not tint this"** — that was the shape until 2026-07-30 and it wrote a falsehood into a field whose meaning is "who made this card" in order to obtain a colour decision. `CanvasAuthorRenderTests.test_anItemNodeIsNeverTinted` holds both halves. **Note this is the ORDINARY path, not a guard against hand-editing**: the source page is the only item node the product creates, and it always carries `.claude` now.

**What is SPOKEN is one phrase on all three primitives, and it does not mirror the tint's refusal** *(Denver, 2026-07-30)*. `CanvasAccessibility` says `claudeTerm` on a Claude scrap, a Claude region **and** a Claude item node — the last of which `paper(for:)` refuses to tint. That is deliberate, not drift: the label is the only channel a VoiceOver user has for *either* signal, and a lean is inaudible, so passing `false` on the item arm left a page that is visually marked as placed-by-Claude and silent. A region's case is stronger still — it carries no colour at all, so the 1° lean is the whole of its provenance. Two consequences were weighed and accepted rather than worked around. **The repetition**: walking a Claude region of six cards says the phrase seven times, and a default-labelled region says it twice running ("Region, From Claude, from Claude, 6 cards"). The alternative was saying it once on the region, and **this tree is flat** — `rowOrdered` interleaves regions and cards by position, a card is not a child of its region — so a user walking card to card can reach a Claude card without passing the region that would have said it; repetitive and never wrong beats quiet and sometimes wrong, and for the same reason the term is not suppressed when the region's *name* already contains it. **The looseness**: on the item node the shared term covers a placement rather than an authorship, so it says slightly more than is true of the words on the page. A second wording for that distinction was rejected — a listener keeping two phrases apart, and remembering which primitive takes which, is the worse cost. The ordering lives in exactly one place, `CanvasAccessibility.label`, which every arm now goes through.

**There are TWO deliberate drawn/spoken divergences on this surface and they go in opposite directions.** This one is *drawn on fewer primitives than it is spoken on* — the item node is announced and not tinted. The other is under Promotion, ~90 lines up: **a contribution record is neither drawn nor announced** (see "What a promotion records on the cards it consumed"), because the stripe means *this produced that* and a second identical stripe for *this went into that* would erase the distinction §6.3 spends its length making, so the record is an inspector fact only. Read together they are one rule rather than two exceptions: **a signal is added to a channel when that channel would otherwise be silent about a fact the writer needs, and withheld when it would be ambiguous with a fact already there.** Neither is drift; each is recorded where its own decision was taken, and this paragraph is the cross-reference so the pair cannot be read as inconsistency.

**A chip carries neither signal, deliberately** — it is already the one thing drawn as "same paper, less of it", and its hairline ends outside the region it sits in, so there is no transform it belongs in. `CanvasRenderer.drawChip` records the reasoning; the card itself is tinted where it lives, which is where the writer goes to read it.

**The two appearances are not equally free here, and the light one is bounded in RED and only in red.** The writer's light paper is white over a `lightBase` (0.930, 0.915, 0.880) ground whose grain peaks at 0.9575 in red, so red may fall at most 0.0225 below white — 5.7 levels of 255, of which the shipped pair spends 5. **Green and blue are not bound by red's ceiling**, and the first draft of the light pair wrongly assumed they were: it dropped all three by similar amounts, which is a *dim*, and a 1.5% dim on white is near-invisible. The cool cast is bought by lowering red, so **blue sits at 1.000** and green comes down to meet red — that turns the dim into a *tint*, and hue is the discriminable axis at near-neutral. Measured against white: the uniform draft was ΔE2000 **1.36**, the shipped `lightClaudeCardPaper` (0.980, 0.980, 1.000) is **2.79**, with red's headroom byte-identical. Measured through `CanvasRenderer.draw` on 2026-07-30, the hairline is the comfortable one and the card is the marginal one: line 14/255 light and 21/255 dark, card 5/255 in its nearest channel light and 10/255 dark. Light still reaches only ~71% of dark's ΔE (3.91, where a warm→cool hue flip and a real value drop are both affordable) and it cannot do better without moving red — i.e. without `lightGrainAmplitude` or `lightBase` coming down, which is a recalibration of a material signed off on 2026-07-26 and is his call, not a tidy-up. **Never a second mark** — and the tilt is the other half of the answer.

**Test the dark ground by rendering it.** Every fixture here was pinned to `.light` through Task 8, so a dark canvas that was flat black shipped with the suite entirely green — the shader ran, the grain was anchored, the zoom faded, and it was still unusable. `renderGround(pan:zoom:size:scheme:)` takes a scheme; `test_theDarkGroundIsAMaterialRatherThanABlackFill` measures brightness, grain energy and the lamp's sweep in 8-bit levels.

## The mounted editor

- **Its focus is requested, not taken.** `makeNSView` has no window yet, so `makeFirstResponder` there is a silent no-op. `updateNSView` re-claims it.
- **`mount` rebinds on layout IDENTITY, not on `textView == nil`.** Otherwise clicking from scrap A to scrap B keeps editing A — and a subview count cannot tell the difference. The mounted layout is held as a `weak var ScrapLayout?` compared with `!==`, not an `ObjectIdentifier`: an identifier held past its object's lifetime can be matched by a *different* layout allocated at the same address, skipping the rebuild and leaving the writer typing into the scrap they just left.
- **Zoom is BOUNDS SCALING** — the frame grows, the bounds are held, and AppKit rasterises the glyphs at the new scale. Never `.scaleEffect` (blurry, wrong geometry, breaks `NSCursor` tracking) and never a re-layout (a re-layout at zoom changes line breaking, which is §7A.2 again). **Do not "harden" this against external frame writes.** Measured 2026-07-26 across 11 cases: `NSView` stores a per-axis frame→bounds *scale*, not a bounds size, and a frame write on its own moves `bounds.size` to preserve that scale — hosted, unhosted, through a layout pass and via superview autoresize alike. Forcing `bounds.size = unscaledSize` after every `setFrameSize` makes the scale `frame.size / unscaledSize` *by definition*, i.e. whatever the external writer chose (measured (2.083, 2.1) and (2.833, 3.0) under exactly that prescription) — which manufactures the 7A.2 failure this file exists to prevent. `mount` writes both rectangles on every call, so the invariant is *established*, not merely maintained, and the frame-then-bounds order is load-bearing.
- **`allowsUndo = false`** — see the undo section below. `NSTextView` gates `undoManager` on `allowsUndo` and returns nil before consulting the delegate or the responder chain (measured all three paths on macOS 26.5), so ⌘Z reaches the canvas stack through `undo:`/`redo:` forwarding on `ScrapEditorContainer`. **Do not delete that forwarding expecting the responder chain or `undoManagerForTextView:` to take over. Neither does.**
- **Caret clamping is in UTF-16 units** (`(tv.string as NSString).length`), not `Character` count — `NSRange` and `ScrapLayout.characterIndex(at:)` are UTF-16, and a scrap containing an emoji otherwise drags a valid caret backwards into the wrong word.

## State, counters, and the writer's words

**`layouts` holds reference types in `@State`.** Typing mutates a `ScrapLayout` in place, so the `Canvas` will not redraw without the `revision` counter — and `revision` must be read in `body`, not inside the draw closure, or SwiftUI never sees the dependency.

**Two counters, and they are not interchangeable:**

| | ticks on | keyed to it |
|---|---|---|
| `revision` | every animation frame — every drag frame, straighten frame, coast frame, keystroke | the `Canvas` redraw, and nothing else |
| `sceneRevision` | load, create, delete, undo, the end of a drag or resize, a coast ending (at rest, or truncated by a press), leaving a scrap, **drawing a line and deleting one**, **a promotion writing its mark**, **an inspector setting or clearing a piece association**, **a batch of Claude's scraps arriving over MCP** | the accessibility tree, **and the region inspector's member lists and Cite-a-Card offer** |

**Delete joined that list in 1C-b**, and this file twice said it never would. **Lines joined it in 1C-c1**, on the same terms as the sweep: a drag that minted nothing is not a structural change, and it shares the sweep's guard rather than growing a second one. **Promotion joined it in 1C-c2** — `PromotionPerformer.mark` bumps on the model's counter right after its `mutateFromInspector`, because a mark is a structural change made from the *other column*, and the accessibility label and the inspector's own arm both have to follow it. **One bump covers 1C-c2b's contribution records too**, and that is not an oversight: the mark and every record are written inside that one bracket (§6.3's one-gesture-one-step rule), so a second bump would be a second structural change where the writer made one. **The piece association joined it in 1C-c2a**, on the same terms and from the same column: `RegionInspector.commitBinding` and `ScrapInspector.commitPiece` both bump, which is what makes the `(sceneRevision, regionID)` gate the correct key for anything that ever caches the resolved destination. **A Claude batch joined it in 1C-c3** — `CanvasClaudeWrite.apply` bumps on its own line after the bracket, exactly as `PromotionPerformer.mark` does, and it is the first writer on this list that **needs no writer's hand at all**: an `add_canvas_scraps` call is a structural change arriving from outside the app, so a region inspector sitting open beside the canvas would otherwise go on showing the member list it cached before the cards landed. **One bump per arrival, not per card** — the whole batch is one bracket (§8A.2 places every add in one region), and the sidecar route bumps nothing because there is no tree and no inspector to follow.

**Every one of those bumps is `model.bumpSceneRevision()`** — see "Who owns what" for why the view no longer has a bump of its own, and what shipped for one commit while it did. A caller passes `rebuildLayouts(bumpsStructuralCounter: false)` when it bumps on its own line — the undo apply and `deleteSelection()` from the start, and since 1C-c3 the `onSceneChangedExternally` binding, which every `mutateFromInspector` writer reaches. **Count the callers, not this sentence**: `grep "bumpsStructuralCounter: false"`.

**A sweep that minted nothing is not a structural change.** `interaction.hasMoved` asks whether the pointer left the press point, and on a trackpad a click routinely drifts a point — so on bare canvas that is most clicks, since `applyMouseDown` opens a drag session on every mouse-down and `createRegion` refuses anything under `CanvasRegionMetrics.minimumSide`. The `.ended` bump is guarded on a region having actually been minted, or every click on nothing sorts the scene, copies every scrap's string, and rebuilds two cached lists in the other column. `test_aBareCanvasPressThatDriftedIsNotAStructuralChange` asserts both directions.

`commitActiveEdit`'s bump is guarded on a scrap having been focused. It runs at the head of `handleClick`, and `onClick` precedes `onDrag(.began)`, so unguarded it rebuilt the whole tree on **every mouse-down** — including the press that starts a drag. The coast's second bump (`handleDrag(.began)`) exists because of that guard: a press truncates a coast, so the timeline's rest branch never runs, and without it the tree keeps the frame the card had when the writer let go.

Keying anything scene-proportional on `revision` runs it at 60–120 Hz through every drag, coast and straighten. That is tripwire 30, and it is how the accessibility tree came to sort the scene and copy every scrap's string per frame.

**`revision` cannot be covered by a test whose redraw is driven by a `@State` mutation, and that is a fact about SwiftUI rather than about the test.** Measured twice: delete `revision += 1` from `handleDrag(.changed)` and all 36 mounting tests stay green, because `.changed` mutates `interaction`, which is `@State`, and **the mutation is itself an invalidation**; delete the bump after a selection assignment and the same thing happens, because the next line writes three more pieces of `@State`. The counter earns its keep only where there is no such mutation — `layouts` (reference types mutated in place) and values read off `model` inside the draw closure — which are exactly the two cases its own doc comment names. **Do not write an assertion to cover the ones that cannot be covered.** Keep the line, put the measurement in the comment beside it, and report the gap rather than papering over it.

**The writer's words leave the editor on every keystroke.** `ScrapEditorContainer` is the text view's delegate; `textDidChange` → `onTextChanged` → `CanvasView.syncActiveEdit()` → fold into `scraps`, re-measure the card, bump `revision`, schedule the debounced save. *Named symptom if this is ever removed:* type into a new scrap, quit without clicking away, and **the scrap comes back empty** — plus the card never grows while you type, because nothing is re-measuring. Three commit points, all required:

1. on change (`onTextChanged`),
2. `.onDisappear` (persona switch, window close),
3. `CanvasStore.beforeFlush` (app quit).

`syncActiveEdit` folds the string and nothing else. `onTextChanged` fires synchronously inside `textDidChange`, so rebuilding layouts from there would replace an `NSTextStorage` from inside its own text view's change notification.

## Persistence

**`canvas.json` is derived and deletable; `canvas.md` is content and is not.** Positions, geometry, seeds, z-order, **every region — label, frame, both member sets, bound piece and collapsed flag — every card's own bound piece, and every line, with its two ends and its name** live in `.maugham/canvas.json`; scrap *text* lives in `canvas.md` at the project root. Delete the sidecar by hand and the layout, the regions and the lines are gone, the words are not. That split is the point (§8), and it is why the region inspector takes a `CanvasModel` and not a `ProjectStore`: none of this is manifest state.

**As of 1C-d there is a THIRD thing on disk and it is content too: `canvas_assets/`**, at the project root beside `canvas.md`, holding every photograph an *owned* item node points at. It is deliberately not under `.maugham/` — deleting the derived sidecar costs the arrangement and must never cost the pictures — and the well's name is never spelled in production code, being derived from `canvas.md`'s own path by the saver research notes already share. `Maugham/Stores/AREA.md`'s "The canvas's asset well" is where that lives, with its two tripwires; the rule here is only that **a node's `ownedPath` is project-relative**, so the pair moves with the project.

**The sidecar is at schema 8** (`CanvasSceneDTO.currentSchemaVersion`; 1 = 1C-a, 2 added `regions`, 3 added `lines`, 4 added `promotedItemID` to nodes and regions, 5 added `boundPieceID` to nodes, 6 added `contributedToItemID` to nodes, 7 = 1C-c3's `author`, on `NodeDTO`, `LineDTO` and `RegionDTO` together, **8 = 1C-d's `ownedPath` on `NodeDTO`**). **Every bump so far is additive-optional, and that is the pattern to keep**: `regions` and `lines` are optional keys and `promotedItemID`/`boundPieceID`/`contributedToItemID`/`author`/`ownedPath` are optional fields, so every older file decodes unchanged rather than throwing on a missing one. In the other direction `CanvasStore.load` refuses a `schemaVersion` above its own and returns an **empty layout with the scraps intact** — so a newer canvas opened by an older build costs the arrangement and never the words. **4 through 8 are the shape to copy, not 2 and 3**: the mark belongs to the thing it marks, so does the association, so does the record of whose note a card's words are in, so does whose hand made a thing, and so does which of an item node's two provenances it has — all five went on `NodeDTO`/`LineDTO`/`RegionDTO` rather than arriving as top-level collections.

**An item node has TWO provenances and `referenceId`/`ownedPath` are their two disjoint fields** *(1C-d, spec §3.1's amendment)*. `CanvasItemReference.project(id:)` is a research item / palette card the canvas never writes to; `.owned(path:)` is a file under `canvas_assets/` the canvas ingested, held as a **project-relative path** — never absolute, never a `file://` URL, never a Markdown ref, each of which breaks the moment the project moves. The encoder writes one field or the other and never both, so **a node carrying both can only come from a hand edit — and the loader keeps it, reading it as OWNED**: an owned path is a claim about a file this project owns and so cannot dangle outside it, it is the same demote-don't-drop answer the one-home repair gives eight lines down, and dropping loses a card the writer can see. An item carrying **neither** is still dropped — nothing to draw, nothing to point at. Pinned in `CanvasSceneCodecTests`, whose schema-7 fixture is the control that says the rest is not vacuous. **All three primitives' `author` rides ONE bump, and that is deliberate**: node, line and region author are one slice's one concept, so minting three versions for it would have said the canvas changed three times when it changed once (the region's arrived last, in Task 8's round 2, and joined 7 rather than opening 8 — the slice was unreleased, and 7 *is* "c3's author field"). `author` on disk is a raw `String?` and `CanvasSceneCodec.authorKind` is the one place it becomes a `SourceKind`, with an unknown value decoding as nil rather than throwing — a **floor, not a place to add author kinds**: a genuine third author wants its own `AnnotationAuthor.SourceKind` case. Membership arrays are written `.sorted()`, which is what makes saving an unchanged canvas byte-identical **across processes**: `Set` iteration is deterministic within one run, so a test that saves twice from one value cannot see this — `test_regionMembershipArraysAreWrittenSortedRegardlessOfSetIterationOrder` decodes the DTO and asserts the order instead.

**`CanvasStore.load()` reads both files directly, and both lines carry `// adr-0018-ok:`.** The reason matters and is not the obvious one — see [ADR 0026 §"The ADR 0018 exception"](../../docs/adr/0026-planning-canvas-rendering.md). Short version: **`canvas.md` has no second source of truth to disagree with.** ADR 0018 exists to stop two representations of the same content drifting; scrap text has exactly one persisted representation, so there is no drift to reintroduce. "It is outside the op log" is *not* the exemption — plenty of things are and still get flagged.

**`CanvasStore.flush()` takes no arguments** (it writes whatever `scheduleSave` last queued) and covers app quit via `NSApplication.willTerminateNotification`, because `.onDisappear` does not fire on ⌘Q. **The observer takes `queue: nil`** — that is required, not a default: with a queue the block is enqueued rather than run on the posting thread, and during termination the hop may never run before the process exits.

**The crash floor: a force-quit loses up to 750 ms of typing since the writer last paused.** That is the same number `DocumentStore`'s autosave debounce gives, so the canvas and the manuscript settle on one rhythm — **but it is not the same guarantee.** A manuscript has the op log behind it: the ops are appended as they happen and a crash costs at most the debounce window of a *rendered* file. The canvas has no op log. `canvas.md` and `canvas.json` are the only records, and 750 ms of scrap text is genuinely gone. Do not let "matches `DocumentStore`" imply parity.

## Undo

**Snapshot-based and canvas-scoped.** `CanvasUndo` registers whole-scene snapshots on a `UndoManager` owned by this surface, not on the window's — `@Environment(\.undoManager)` would give a window-lifetime manager shared with the manuscript editor's op-log stack (ADR 0023), and a persona switch mid-drag would leave a half-open group on it. Canvas state is derived (§8); op-logging it would stop it being derived.

`CanvasUndo` owns no state of its own — it reads and writes its owner's through two closures. That is what let 1C-b Task 3 move the same class onto `CanvasModel` by rebinding them; the class itself did not change. **`CanvasModel` is the owner now**: it holds the scene, the scrap text, the selection, the sidecar store and the recorder, because the region inspector in the right-hand column reads and mutates the same scene the canvas draws. `CanvasView` keeps everything that is a property of one *view* of that state — camera, layouts, editing focus, the straighten, momentum, `revision` and `sceneRevision`.

- **`beginGesture` opens no `UndoManager` group.** A group cannot span an event boundary, and an "Edit Scrap" gesture spans as many events as the writer types keystrokes; a closed *empty* group still pushes a step. **Tests must set `groupsByEvent = false`; production must not.** (With `groupsByEvent` off, an out-of-group registration raises rather than being absorbed — `register` guards for it explicitly.)
- **Only a real keystroke may move an undo boundary.** `syncActiveEdit(fromKeystroke:)` defaults to `false`, and the two housekeeping callers — `.onDisappear` and `CanvasStore.beforeFlush` — take that default. Without it, a writer who pauses and then quits trips the idle break inside `beforeFlush`, which closes a step and **reopens a gesture on a view that is going away**: a half-open bracket arriving from the save path instead of the focus path.
- **An undo serviced while a gesture is open re-baselines that gesture.** The baseline is captured at focus-in and nothing is registered until focus-out, so a ⌘Z mid-visit leaves it stale: close the gesture against it and you register a step whose *undo* re-applies what the writer just undid. The one line that fixes it lives at the end of `CanvasUndo.register`'s closure. Closing the gesture *before* servicing the undo instead needs `NSUndoManagerWillUndoChange`, and registering while the manager is mid-undo makes the registration a **redo**.
- **The mounted editor has `allowsUndo = false`.** Snapshots own scrap text. If the text view registered too, one change would land on the stack twice, and its copy would target an `NSTextStorage` that `rebuildLayouts()` has replaced — so the second ⌘Z would appear to do nothing.
- **⌘Z is reachable by exactly TWO routes, and BOTH are an explicit `undo:`/`redo:` pair with a `validateUserInterfaceItem` beside it.** `ScrapEditorContainer` (a scrap focused, through `CanvasUndo` so the open gesture closes first) and `CanvasEventNSView` (nothing focused, through the bare manager because no gesture can be open). **An `undoManager` override is NOT a third route and does not enable a menu item.** Measured 2026-07-27 on macOS 26.5: with `CanvasEventNSView` genuinely first responder in the real hosting hierarchy, `window.undoManager` was a *different* manager — `NSWindow` asks its delegate for `windowWillReturnUndoManager:` and otherwise vends its own; it does not ask the first responder. Believing otherwise shipped the 1C-a smoke defect: undo built and twenty-two tests deep, greyed out on a bare canvas, because every one of those tests drove the recorder or the manager directly. `CanvasViewMountingTests.test_undoIsReachableFromTheEditMenuWithNoScrapFocused` is the one that goes through resolve → validate → send.
- **Granularity inside a scrap is the SENTENCE.** The outer bracket is focus; `breakGesture()` splits the visit on a finished sentence or `ScrapUndoBeat.idleSeconds` (1.5 s) of stillness. The idle beat is asked BEFORE the keystroke is folded in (so the closing step ends where the writer stopped) and the sentence rule AFTER (so the full stop belongs to the step it closes). Swapping either is invisible in the code and obvious to a writer. **Per-word is only reachable through `allowsUndo`, which is the defect above**, and would also mean a whole-scene snapshot per word. This cost is writer-facing: the guide's Personas topic says what ⌘Z does inside a scrap, and that sentence is part of the contract, not a nicety.

**A change made from the OTHER COLUMN goes through `CanvasModel.mutateFromInspector`, never `mutate`.** A visit to a scrap holds "Edit Scrap" open for as long as focus is in it, and *nothing on the inspector's side of the window closes it*: `commitActiveEdit` runs from `handleClick`, which only fires for clicks on the canvas. A double-click never reassigns `selection`, so a region picked a moment ago is still what the right-hand column is showing. **The repro is a double-click on the region's own CHROME BAR** — click 1 selects the region, click 2 finds no node under the point, takes the `.emptyCanvas` branch, mints a scrap and opens "Edit Scrap", and the `guard clickCount >= 2` returns before the selection is reassigned. It is *not* a double-click on a card: AppKit delivers `clickCount: 1` on the first mouse-down of a double-click, so that first click selects the card and the inspector is already showing its empty state. Rename the region there — and through `mutate` the rename NESTS: depth 2 takes no snapshot, depth 1 registers nothing, and **the rename is not on the undo stack at all.** Worse than absent: the open gesture's baseline predates it, so the writer's next keystroke carries the rename into a step called "Edit Scrap" and a ⌘Z aimed at a sentence takes the region's name with it; quit without returning to the canvas and `release()` drops the lot. `CanvasUndo.mutateFromOutsideTheCanvas` is the same close-run-reopen `undo()` performs — the run of typing in progress becomes its own step first, under its own name, and the visit resumes so the rest of the sentence is still bracketed. **The reopen is load-bearing and is invisible to the obvious test**: drop it and the first ⌘Z after the edit lands on the rename's snapshot, which happens to carry the same scrap text, so every assertion but "the label is still the new one" is satisfied by coincidence. `CanvasView` keeps using `mutate` — everything it does is already inside its own bracket by construction.

**A caller from that column that writes scrap TEXT as well as scene shape hands the words to `mutateFromInspector(_:scrapTexts:)`, INSIDE the bracket — and both other orderings are MEASURED failures, not hazards** *(1C-c3, `CanvasClaudeWrite`, which adds a batch of cards *and* their words in one act)*. A snapshot is `(scene, scraps)` and `endGesture` diffs the whole of it, so:

- **`setScrapText` BEFORE the call registers the words under the WRITER's name.** `mutateFromOutsideTheCanvas` closes the open "Edit Scrap" gesture first, and by then the words are already in `scraps` — so the step the writer takes back with the ⌘Z they aimed at their sentence carries Claude's text as well.
- **`setScrapText` AFTER the call lands them in the gesture that has just been REOPENED**, i.e. in the writer's *next* sentence. Measured symptom, and it is the memorable one: **"the card is there and its words are not"** — the ⌘Z removes the text and leaves the cards, and at depth 0 the snapshot was captured *after* the fold, so the undo removes the card and leaves `scraps[id]` behind. `ScrapText.render` then writes that orphan into `canvas.md` for good.

Both are the ride-along this verb exists to prevent, arriving through the **scraps** half of the snapshot instead of the scene half, and **invisible to any assertion about the scene alone**. Inside the bracket the words also go in *first*, because `onSceneChangedExternally` re-measures each card from the text it finds and a card measured before its words arrive is measured empty. The tripwire-32 census catches the wrong version independently — `setScrapText` is an *inside* verb, so calling it from a file in the census fails it — but only for production: **the census scans `Maugham/` only**, so a test-side `setScrapText` is invisible to it, which is how the tree's only worked example of "a card added from outside the canvas" came to demonstrate the forbidden ordering, green, for a task.

The undo apply replaces the scene and then calls `CanvasModel.onSceneReplacedByUndo` — synchronously, inside the apply and a whole timeline tick before the coast could take another step. `CanvasView` binds that to `momentum.stop()` FIRST (a ⌘Z within ~1 s of a flick otherwise puts the card back at the pick-up point and lets the coast skate it away from there) and reconciles focus AFTER (an undo can remove the scrap the writer is standing in — double-click bare canvas, type, ⌘Z, ⌘Z is three keystrokes to it, and without the reconcile every drag is ignored until the writer happens to click somewhere).

**A replaced `ScrapLayout` under a live editor is SAFE, and the file says so with the measurement.** Measured 2026-07-26 on macOS 26.5: an `NSTextView` built through `NSTextView(frame:textContainer:)` **owns its TextKit 2 stack** — with the `ScrapLayout` released, the layout manager, content storage and text storage are all still alive and the view lays out and draws. Safe is not the same as correct: through that window the view still shows the text the undo discarded, and showing the restored words is the *rebind's* job. Which is why the replacement must be a **new** `ScrapLayout` rather than the old one mutated in place.

## Delete

**⌫ is `CanvasEventNSView.keyDown` → `CanvasView.deleteSelection()`, and it is deliberately a `keyDown` rather than `deleteBackward(_:)`** — the event view is not a text responder, never calls `interpretKeyEvents`, and so would never receive the action message.

**The two keys are `NSDeleteCharacter` (0x007F, ⌫) and `NSDeleteFunctionKey` (0xF728, ⌦), and they live in `CanvasEventNSView.backwardDelete`/`.forwardDelete` so the tests read the same values production switches on.** `NSBackspaceCharacter` is **0x0008 — Ctrl-H, not forward delete**; the first draft paired it with 0x7F on the stated belief that it *was* forward delete, so ⌦ over a selected card beeped. It is also unreachable here at all: **`charactersIgnoringModifiers` ignores every modifier except Shift** and never applies the Control transformation, so a physical Ctrl-H arrives at this switch as `"h"` and 0x08 could not have matched any keystroke.

**The obvious spelling of that switch is wrong, and the compiler's warning is easy to walk past in a file this size.** `case "\u{7F}", "\u{8}" where onDeleteKey?() == true:` **binds the `where` clause to the LAST pattern only** — so `\u{7F}` matches unconditionally and the condition is never asked, reintroducing the swallowed-key bug. Measured with a standalone probe: the condition ran **zero** times. It is written as an `if` inside the case body for that reason; the comment names both the warning and the test, because a warning is not a defence and the tests are what stop a tidy-up.

**A ⌫ pressed while any gesture is open deletes nothing** — `deleteSelection()` guards on `CanvasModel.isInGesture`. `CanvasUndo.beginGesture` takes no snapshot when it nests and `endGesture` registers nothing above depth 0, so a delete inside somebody else's bracket collapses into it under the wrong name, and if that bracket never closes it is not recoverable at all. Both are reachable: press and hold on a card (which opens "Move Card") and press ⌫; or press ⌫ in the runloop turn after a double-click, before the editor has claimed first responder, while "Edit Scrap" is open — then quit, and `detach()` drops the stack with the card already written away. Measured: the card *and its words* gone from disk with nothing that could have brought them back. **The comment at the `undoManager:` argument in `CanvasView` — "there is never an open gesture here" — is the belief this broke.** Anything new hung off a key on this view inherits the same problem.

- **Selection is a region → the region goes and its cards stay.** The canvas owns arrangement, not existence (§3.1, generalised); the membership records die with it, which is all `CanvasScene.removeRegion` touches.
- **Selection is a node → the node goes, its text goes from `canvas.md`, and `CanvasScene.remove` scrubs it from every region.** One gesture, so one ⌘Z brings back the card *and* its words — a snapshot carries the scene and the scrap text together and they cannot be restored out of step.
- **Selection is a line → the line goes and both cards stay.** The two directions are not symmetric and the section has to say both: deleting a CARD takes its lines with it (`CanvasScene.remove` scrubs every line touching it), because a line to a node that is gone would draw into nowhere — but deleting a line touches neither card, exactly as deleting a region leaves its cards. One ⌘Z brings back a deleted card, its words *and* its lines together, because the snapshot carries the whole scene.
- **Nothing selected is a no-op, not a guess** — and the key travels on rather than being swallowed. `deleteSelection()` returns whether anything actually went, and `keyDown` sends a `false` to `super`, where AppKit's `noResponder` beeps. **A key that does nothing *and* suppresses the beep is indistinguishable from a broken app**: the beep is the platform saying "that key means nothing here", and it is the one signal separating "nothing was selected" from "delete is broken". The canvas does not claim a key it did not use. The first draft returned `Void` and swallowed every ⌫ — it read identically at the call site, which is why the callback reports back.

**⌫ never fights the editor**, and that is a fact about first responder rather than about z-order: while a scrap is focused the mounted `NSTextView` holds it, so the window delivers the key there and the writer deletes a character. Measured 2026-07-28 by mutation — putting the event view in FRONT of the editor does not change it, while stopping the editor claiming focus deletes the whole card mid-sentence. `CanvasViewMountingTests.test_backspaceInsideAScrapDeletesACharacterAndNotTheCard` is the one that says so, and it clicks the card once before entering it so there is a live selection for the event view to destroy if it ever wins.

**1C-a shipped this feature's other half with no caller at all** — `CanvasScene.remove`, its inverse and the `"Delete Scrap"` undo step, all built, all exercised, no key and no menu item — which is the same shape as its ⌘Z defect. So `test_backspaceDeletesTheSelectedScrapThroughTheRealResponderChain` sends a real `NSEvent` through `window.sendEvent(_:)` and reads what reached DISK. **Do not replace it with a test that calls `deleteSelection()`**; that is precisely the test that would have passed throughout 1C-a.

## Accessibility — we own the tree (§7A.6)

Drawn content has no accessibility tree, and §7A.6 calls one "not optional in a writing tool". `CanvasAccessibility` mirrors the scene graph: **every node, all of them, in rows-then-columns reading order** (a proximity walk over the y-sorted list, starting a new band when the gap to the previous card exceeds `rowBand` — deliberately not a `(y / rowBand).rounded(.down)` grid, which reads two cards 2 pt apart as different rows whenever they straddle a multiple of 60). Never `.accessibilityElement(children: .ignore)` on the stack.

**Whose hand made it is spoken, because neither drawn signal can be heard** *(1C-c3)*. A cooler paper, a cooler hairline and a card drawn perfectly straight all say "Claude put this here" and none of them reaches an assistive client — so the label says it too, on **every** primitive that can carry the fact: `CanvasAccessibility.claudeTerm` after the kind (and after a region's name) and before the durable facts — "Scrap, from Claude, promoted, 1 line: …", "Region, Act II fog, from Claude, 3 cards", "Reference, from Claude" — and inside `connectionPhrase` for a line, which is the only place anything about a line can be said at all. **One wording for one fact**: the term agrees with `CanvasClaudePlacement.defaultRegionLabel` ("From Claude") and `CanvasClaudeWrite.undoStepName` ("Add Scraps from Claude"), and `test_theSpokenTermAgreesWithTheRegionLabelAndTheUndoStep` holds the three against each other. A line phrase keeps the WHOLE count and adds provenance as a share of it — "3 lines, 2 from Claude", or "2 lines from Claude" when they all are. The item node is the one place the spoken term is looser than the drawn one, and the accepted repetition is the region's; both rulings are recorded with the material, under "What is SPOKEN".

**`CanvasAXRole` never reaches an assistive client, so the kind goes in the LABEL.** `CanvasAXChildren` publishes `accessibilityLabel` and `accessibilityValue` and nothing else; the role enum is computed, tested and read by nothing downstream. A scrap opens with "Scrap" and an item node with "Reference" — and for one slice a region opened with its own name, so it said what it was called and never what it was. `CanvasAccessibility.regionKind` is the word, held as a constant so the tests assert against what ships. `test_everyRoleNamesItselfInTheLabelBecauseTheRoleItselfIsNeverPublished` asserts it for all three roles together, which is what stops the next primitive shipping with the same hole.

Two rules keep it off the frame path, and both are needed:

- **Elements carry content-space frames and are rebuilt from `.onChange(of: sceneRevision)`** — never inside `body`, and **never from `revision`**. `revision` is bumped by `handleDrag(.changed)` and by the timeline's per-frame `.onChange(of: context.date)`; keying the tree on it sorts the scene and copies every scrap's string at 60–120 Hz through any drag, coast or straighten.
- **The synthetic children live in `CanvasAXChildren`, used with `.equatable()`.** Inline in `body`, the `ForEach` reads `camera` and so rebuilds N views per frame. Extracted, SwiftUI skips it unless the elements or the camera actually moved — which covers every animation path here. A pan or a zoom does still rebuild it; that is accepted, bounded by the 2,000-node number, and the thing to verify before reaching for `.scaleEffect` over the AX layer is whether SwiftUI resolves accessibility frames through it at all.

**The frame half of this tree is covered in two places, and the split is about the ENVIRONMENT rather than the code** *(2026-07-31)*. A published frame is the end of six hops — the scene, the content-space rect, the camera, SwiftUI's publication of it, the accessibility runtime, and the screen coordinates that come back — and only the first two are ours. Those two are asked in `CanvasAccessibilityTests`, with no window and nothing to be unavailable: `test_aCardsContentFrameIsTheCardsOwnRectangle`, `test_theCameraMapsThatRectangleToWhereTheCardIsDrawn`, and a camera-at-rest control. The fixture card sits at (20, 90) **off the origin on purpose** — (0, 0) is the one point a flipped y leaves exactly where it was, which is why the older `test_elementsAreCameraIndependentAndResolveThroughTheCamera` beside them reads `s1` at the origin and cannot fail for the flip its own message names. Verified by patching, not by reading, and the shape decides it: negating the *content* y on its way into the camera leaves that test green, negating the *view* y the camera produced fails all of them.

**The round trip stays in `CanvasViewMountingTests`, and it is the half no developer machine agrees with CI about.** Both frame tests there passed locally on macOS 26 and failed on CI (macOS 15, Xcode 26.3) for five days. Two test-side defects came out of that, and both could not fail. The attach probe discarded its `AXError` and returned `true` unconditionally; it now **reports what happened**, so "no assistive client" is a named `XCTSkip` rather than a wall of assertions failing for a reason none of them mentions. The card lookup took the first element carrying the right *value* — and `axElements` walks from the content view *down*, so an ancestor that proxies a descendant's value is reached **first** and answers with the container's rect, which is indistinguishable from the defect the test exists to catch. It now **names the published role** (`AXStaticText`, which is the reason `.isStaticText` is on `CanvasAXChildren` at all). Every failure carries the **whole observed tree**, the attach result and the OS version, because a message nobody can reproduce has to bring its own evidence — that is what turned two blind runs into one answer.

**A LONE card's published frame is the canvas's on macOS 15, and that is Apple's publication rather than ours** *(measured 2026-07-31, on the runner)*. When `.accessibilityChildren` produces exactly ONE synthetic child, **macOS 15.7.7 (build 24G720)** gives that child its container's rectangle: the hosting view, the canvas group and the card all report `(0, 0, 800, 600)` for a card drawn at (20, 20) 240 wide. The element itself is entirely correct — role `AXStaticText`, label "Scrap", the writer's sentence in `accessibilityValue` — so the card is reachable and reads out right; **only the rectangle is wrong**. **macOS 26.5 (build 25F84)** publishes `(20, 20, 240, 38)` for the same canvas, and 15.7.7 publishes distinct, correct frames the moment there are three cards. So it is a fold of a lone child, not a broken frame path.

The consequence to state plainly: on **macOS 14/15 — and the deployment target is 14, so these are real users** — a VoiceOver cursor over a canvas holding exactly one card gets the whole canvas instead of the card. It corrects itself the moment the writer makes a second card, which is the interaction immediately after the one that produces it.

**Nothing in production works around it, and that is a decision rather than an omission** (Denver, 2026-07-31). A synthetic second element, or a frame override on a one-child tree, would be a product change made to satisfy an older OS — inventing a thing for an assistive client to meet that the writer's canvas does not contain. The tree is correct on the platform that reports it correctly. `test_aLoneCardOnTheCanvasIsAnElementOfItsOwn` therefore **skips on the OBSERVATION** — the card's published frame equals its container's — and never on `#available`: a version check asserts a belief about which systems are affected and exactly one has been measured, whereas this predicate is the fold itself, so the test resumes on its own if Apple fixes it. It is not unfalsifiable either: the multi-card round trip beside it is **ungated** and asserts a published width of 240 against a container 800 wide, which is the same predicate exercised in its false direction on every run.

**A line is not an element of its own, and it is named at both its ends instead.** An element carries a content-space frame and an assistive client navigates by it; a line's frame is the bounding box of two *other* cards — mostly bare ground, and a sliver when the line is near axis-aligned — so a VoiceOver user would walk into a rectangle and find nothing in it. A relationship is legible at its ends, so each connected node's label carries the count and the names (`CanvasAccessibility.connectionPhrase`), and `summary` reports the total, or a canvas carrying twenty relationships announces itself identically to one carrying none. The whole layer therefore rides the `sceneRevision` rebuild `elements` already uses, and adds no new frame path.

Two rules inside that, and they differ on purpose:

- **A line with a HIDDEN end is not named.** The filter is `scene.isHidden` — the same predicate the node loop reads — so the tree keeps ONE rule about a collapsed region rather than two. A resident of a collapsed region has left the tree entirely, so naming a line to it announces a relationship to a card nothing can navigate to.
- **A line with an UNMEASURED end IS named, and that is deliberately not the renderer's rule.** `CanvasScene.drawnLines` drops both cases and is the tempting single source; but the node loop keeps an unmeasured card in the tree on purpose (`unmeasuredHeight` exists for exactly that — a scrap the writer just made must not be unreachable until a layout pass happens to run), so a line to a card that IS in the tree belongs in the tree with it.

**`CanvasScene.lines(touching:)` was the obvious way to build that and it was deleted instead.** It shipped 1C-c1 with zero production callers, censused three times, and this was its last chance. It loses on complexity, measured rather than asserted — Debug, 2,000 nodes × 2,000 lines (a line per card, the case nothing bounds): the whole shipped rebuild **11.6 ms**, a per-node filter **398 ms**. That rebuild is not on the frame path but it *is* on the gesture path, since `sceneRevision` bumps at the end of every drag — 398 ms when the writer lets go of a card is not a hitch. `CanvasAccessibility.connections` builds one index in a single pass instead, and `CanvasPerformanceProbeTests.test_theAccessibilityRebuildDoesNotGoQuadraticInTheLines` fails at 150 ms. **This is the FOURTH built-and-unreachable half in this area and the fourth found by counting callers rather than by a test**; it is the first to be resolved by deletion, which is the other legitimate answer.

The names inside a phrase are in `scene.lines`' id order, and that is load-bearing: `Dictionary` iteration is seeded per process, so a phrase built from an unordered walk reads differently between two runs of the same binary — `CanvasMembership.homeRegion`'s measured flake, in a third id space.

**Known divergence, open for smoke:** the focused scrap is announced **twice**. The synthetic twin stays in the tree beside the real `NSTextView`, and because the rebuild is gated on `sceneRevision` its value is the text as of *entry* — so a VoiceOver user walking the canvas mid-visit meets the same card twice with two different strings. It refreshes on leaving. Whether `elements` needs to know the focused id is a question for a VoiceOver walk, not for a unit test.

**Second known divergence, open for the same walk:** whether a line should be independently navigable after all. The argument above is that its frame is not a place — but a VoiceOver user who wants to *select* a line, to name it or delete it, currently has no route to one at all, and hearing "1 line: because" on a card is not the same as being able to reach it. The answer is a real session with a screen reader, not a unit test; the two candidate shapes are a frame at the label pill (small, aimable, and absent on an unlabelled line) or a custom action on each connected card.

**Third known divergence, and 1C-d created it: two owned photographs are told apart by LOOKING and not by listening** *(Task 5's review, M7; open for Denver at the smoke).* An owned item card now carries the one thing that distinguishes it — its picture — while its element says `"Reference"` and its value is `CanvasItemFacts.ownedTitle`, the fixed noun "Image", for every owned card on the canvas. It is not a regression (the placeholder said "Image" too) and there may be **nothing to say instead**: the title is fixed precisely because ingest discards the writer's filename, so the only honest alternative is alt text, which nothing in the app collects. Recorded rather than inherited, because this directory's rule is that a drawn/spoken divergence is *decided*. **The heading is `itemKind` on BOTH provenances for the same reason** — that is what VoiceOver already says for both, and one constant changes it — and it reads slightly oddly over a photograph; that is the second half of the same question and wants the same answer.

**`summary` counts the WHOLE line set**, collapsed regions included, matching `scene.count` right beside it — that has always counted a collapsed region's residents too. The summary says what is on the canvas; a collapsed region says separately what it is holding.

## What tells the writer Claude was here *(1C-c3)*

Every other signal in this file — the cooler paper, the 0° lean, `claudeTerm` — reaches a writer **who is already looking at the canvas**, in the Plan persona, with the batch's region on screen. Two surfaces exist because that is not where a writer is when Claude adds something.

**`CanvasClaudeArrivalModifier` is the arrival**, and it is `MCPNoteBanner`'s third caller. It receives `.maughamCanvasNodesAdded` — **project-scoped**, so a window on another project announces nothing and a closed one nothing at all — names the count and the region's `displayLabel`, and its **Show** puts the window in the Plan persona with the region selected. The persona is not decoration: `Persona.binderSegments(for:)` gives `.canvas` to Plan and to nobody else, so setting the segment alone lands the binder on a surface its own picker does not offer. **The SEGMENT it lands on is conditional as of slice 2 of the persona shell**: a writer already on a segment that centres the canvas — `.canvas` or `.tree`, which differ only in the left column — is left where they are, because the reveal and the selection already keep Show's whole promise there and forcing `.canvas` would swap their manuscript tree out for the research tree and cost them their place in the structure they were arranging. From anywhere else it is `.canvas`. The rule is `BinderSegment.centresTheCanvas`, the one predicate every canvas-centre check asks. **It says "added to the canvas" and never "saved"** — `CanvasStore.writeNow` swallows every I/O error with `try?`, area-wide and pre-existing, so nothing on this path can promise the disk; `AddCanvasScrapsTool`'s own return comment says the same about its response. **`MCPBannerModel` is deliberately not reused** though the banner view is: `bump(title:latestId:)` increments the count by one per call, so a batch of six would announce one, and its `latestId` is a research id `handleShowLatestMCPNote` takes to the Research segment. `Arrival` and `Destination` are values rather than expressions in `body` for this directory's standing reason — `_ConditionalContent` is branch-invariant and a rendered `Text` is not inspectable.

**The jump does not go through `PersonaModifier`**, and that is a decision. Reaching it would mean posting `.maughamSetPersona` and then writing `binderSegment` over the segment it restores from `PersonaMemory` — a correctness argument resting on SwiftUI delivery order, which is tripwire 2's shape. So the remembered position is left alone (it records where the writer *chose* to be) and the persona is persisted directly, or the next launch reopens in the old persona with the binder already on the canvas.

**`CanvasAuthorLine` is the pane's own half, and EVERY arm that can carry the fact renders it** *(three since 1C-d Task 7; the census discovers the file list rather than holding one, so count nothing here).* It is a THIRD fact on the card arm — beside the promoted mark and the contribution record — and a SECOND on the region arm, never a fresh wording of any of them. It sits beside the thing's own name (the card's words, the region's label) and **not under "Promotion"**: being Claude's is an *attribute* and not an event, and under that heading the sentence starts reading as a mark, which would tell the writer a thing has already been promoted — re-promoting a Claude card or region is exactly as available as re-promoting their own. `nil` author is the writer and the pane **says nothing at all**, which is what keeps "made by you" off every card and every region made before this slice.

**It lived in `ScrapInspector` as `Origin` for one round and only cards had it** — the same field, the same 0° lean, the same spoken `claudeTerm`, and a surface for one half of it. That is `PromotedArtifactSection`'s history repeating on the neighbouring field (Task 10's docs sweep found it, no reviewer did), and the fix is the same one: **one implementation every arm is handed** — `CanvasAuthorLine.forCard` / `.forRegion` / (1C-d) `.forItem` for the decision, `CanvasAuthorLineRow` for the rendering, so neither the wording nor the one-source rule can drift between them. `RegionBindingTests.test_everyInspectorArmRendersTheOneAuthorLine` is the census, and it also forbids any arm spelling the sentence itself. **Its file list is DISCOVERED from the directory** — every `*Inspector.swift` under `Maugham/Canvas/` is either in its resolver table or on its exemption list (a LINE, whose author is spoken inside `connectionPhrase` and which has no name of its own for a row to sit beside) — so a *new* arm that does not render the row goes red, which a written-down array cannot see. That is the failure this census is actually for; the plant that proves it reads the disk is a fifth `Inspector.swift` file.

**`forItem` is a third resolver and not a call to `forCard`, and the reason is a real sentence it would otherwise produce.** `read(from:)` finds the source page by asking which of the home region's members is an item node — and for the page card itself, that member *is* the page. Through `forCard` the source page's own pane reads *From Claude. Read from “The falls at night”.* over the card that IS "The falls at night". So `forItem` answers `.claude` and stops, and it takes no `title` closure because with no page to name there is nothing to look up. `test_aSourcePageDoesNotSayItWasReadFromItself` asserts both halves — the arm's answer *and*, as its control, that `forCard` really would name the page — so a quiet forwarding goes red.

**The source page is read from the region's item member** — created, adopted or cited, all three of `CanvasClaudePlacement`'s cases — because there is no page-to-cards field and inventing one for a caption would be a schema change. **Exactly one item member, or the line names none**: that is the shape the planner produces (one `source_item_id` per call), and it means a writer who later drops a second research item into Claude's region gets silence rather than a caption naming whichever sorted first. The title comes through the same deferred `artifactTitle` closure the two promotion records use — which has no default on either arm, deliberately — so a page the writer has since deleted falls back to the plain sentence instead of printing an id. **A `store` is never read from a `body` or from anything a `body` calls.** It costs nothing at all for the writer's own things: the author check is one dictionary lookup and returns before any walk.

**For a CLAUDE subject it is scene-proportional work on a frame path, on the two resolvers that WALK, and the disclosure lives in `CanvasAuthorLine`'s class doc** — "What this costs on the frame path, honestly". (`forItem` is one dictionary lookup and is outside that disclosure deliberately, not missing from it: an item node has no source page to resolve.) `forCard` pays a `homeRegion` walk (which sorts `scene.regions`) plus a member union, a lookup per member and a `TreeWalk`; `forRegion` pays all of that but the walk. It is the same accepted-unmeasured cost as `ScrapInspector.association`'s two `Promotion.piece` walks below, with the same gate available and unused (`RegionInspector`'s `(sceneRevision, id)` cache). **The paragraph was deleted by the extraction that made the resolver shared** and `ScrapInspector` was left pointing at it for one commit — the whole-branch review's finding, and the reason it is now named in two places: a disclosure a review relied on can stop holding with nothing going red.

**Show moves the CAMERA too, and it has to** — `CanvasClaudePlacement.looseOrigin` is `occupied.maxX + gutter` over the union of everything, so Claude's region is *by construction* outside the bounding box of the writer's own work, and a Show that only selected it showed them nothing. The route is a third `@ObservationIgnored` hook — `CanvasModel.onRevealRequested`, bound in `CanvasView.load()` beside its two siblings, where `momentum.stop()` already writes `@State` from a model callback — and **not** a `MaughamEvent`: a camera nudge is not an app-wide fact, and a name would cost a scope, a receive helper, a liveness note and a tripwire-21 entry while making delivery order the correctness argument. `CanvasCamera.bring(_:toViewPoint:)` is `viewPoint(fromContent:)` solved for `pan`, one line and pure; it puts the region's top-left at `revealViewPoint` and **never touches zoom** (fitting a rect needs the viewport size, and this view deliberately has no `GeometryReader` — the size exists only inside the `Canvas` closure). **The hook carries the REGION and not a point, and the model PARKS the request when nothing is mounted**, which is the part that is easy to get wrong: Show switches persona *and* asks in one act, so at the moment it asks there is no `CanvasView` yet and no hook to call — a version that only called the closure did nothing at all whenever the banner was the thing that told the writer. And a caller in another column may be holding a scene written before the batch landed (`add_canvas_scraps` writes the sidecar when `isAttached` is false), so the point is resolved on the far side of `attach()`. `CanvasLiveSeamTests`' assigner census carries `onRevealRequested` for the reason it carries the other two: a hook that is declared, parked into, cleared on detach and bound to nothing is `addAppearance`'s exact shape.

**Show also forces the inspector column open**, as every other navigation-to-a-pane in `ProjectWindow` does (`PersonaModifier` on *every* persona switch, and the detail-segment command's "ensure pane is visible"). Without it a writer who had closed the column with ⌘⌥I got a camera move and a selection and nothing naming what had arrived. It is a field on `Destination` rather than a bare assignment in `show`, so a test pins it.

**Not built, and worth knowing before the next slice:** the overlapping banners. Three `.overlay(alignment: .top)` transient banners now share the window — the research-note sentence on `ProjectWindow` itself, the promotion confirmation on `CanvasPromotionModifier`, and this arrival — and two on screen at once draw over each other. It needs a promotion within 8 s of a batch landing, and fixing it properly means one banner host for the window rather than a fourth overlay, which is its own slice.

## The MCP surface — how Claude reads and adds *(1C-c3, spec §8A.2)*

**Two tools, and the write one cannot express where anything goes.** `list_canvas` returns the whole canvas; `add_canvas_scraps` adds cards. `AddCanvasScrapsTool.Params` has exactly five keys — `project_id`, `scraps`, `source_item_id`, `region_label`, `connect` — and **no position, no node id and no region id**, which is the constitution's reproduction corollary made *structural* rather than promised: where the cards land is `CanvasClaudePlacement`'s decision. `connect` indexes **this call's own `scraps` array** (`[[0, 2]]`), so Claude can draw the arrows it read off a page and can reach nothing the writer made. `test_theSignatureCannotExpressAPositionOrAnId` reads the schema and is what will still be there when someone adds an `x` for convenience. The **result** carries ids only, and for a second reason worth keeping: a caller that reads back coordinates starts reasoning about layout, and the next request is a parameter for it. `list_canvas` reports geometry, legitimately, being a read.

**Two routes, and the discriminator is `CanvasModel.isAttached` — never `liveCanvas != nil`.** `CanvasClaudeWrite` is the only place that test is written, so the read and the write cannot come to different conclusions about which canvas is real.

| | attached | otherwise |
|---|---|---|

**Why `isAttached` and not "is there a model".** The model is created eagerly with `ProjectWindow` and is attached only while the Plan persona is on screen, so a window whose writer has never opened the canvas holds a model that is real, addressable and **unusable** — and a *detached* one's scene is the snapshot from when the persona closed, which cannot see anything the sidecar route has written since. Either way a write into a detached model is accepted, reports real ids and **vanishes** the next time the writer opens Plan, with nothing red. Note the tempting shorter reason is false and was corrected during the slice: a detached model does **not** have `store == nil`, because `detach()` flushes and clears `store.beforeFlush` but deliberately keeps `store` — so `scheduleSave` would queue a real write. The conclusion is unchanged and the accurate reason is *the scene is a stale snapshot*.

**`flush()`, not `scheduleSave`, and `save`, not `scheduleSave`.** A tool that answers "added" with the words only in memory has lied if the app is quit inside the 750 ms debounce, and the canvas has no op log behind it (see "The crash floor"). On the sidecar route the store is transient and nothing would ever fire its debounce. **Neither route may say "saved" though** — `CanvasStore.writeNow` swallows every I/O error with `try?`, area-wide and pre-existing, so `apply` returning without throwing is not evidence the write reached disk. Both the tool's response and the arrival banner say **added**.

**`CanvasClaudeWrite.swift` is a tripwire 32 census entry, and its repro is the sharpest of them because it needs no gesture at all.** An `add_canvas_scraps` call can arrive while the writer is inside a scrap with "Edit Scrap" held open, and *nothing on this side of the window closes their bracket* — `commitActiveEdit` runs from `CanvasView.handleClick`, which fires only for clicks on the canvas. ## Scale

**Supported: 2,000 nodes** (`CanvasPerformanceProbeTests.supportedNodeCount`). Not a hard cap — nothing enforces it — but the number the culling probe defends. It is far above any real canvas (a Playlist-scale collection is tens of nodes); tldraw ships a hard 4,000-shape cap, Excalidraw degrades near 5,000. Above it, expect the *draw* pass rather than the culling to become the limit.

**Be precise about what defends that number**, because the probe's own comments were not, once:

- `test_culledSetDependsOnViewportNotSceneSize` is an **output-invariance** assertion, not a complexity one. It pins that culling happens and that its result is independent of scene size. It cannot see that `visibleNodes` does O(scene) internal work — `nodes(intersecting:)` filters `byID.values` before sorting, and that linear scan is unavoidable because **there is no spatial index.** (Its docstring used to claim to be a complexity assertion; corrected 2026-07-26.)
- **The three measured figures:** cull of 2,000 nodes **0.082 ms/frame** (~0.5% of a 60 Hz frame); hit test over 2,000 nodes **0.052 ms**; a zoomed-out cull returning 820 nodes in **1.429 ms**.
- **The 8 ms budgets are scoped to catching O(n²)**, and do that well — an O(n²) blowup at n = 2,000 is a ~2,000× regression, nowhere near survivable in ~100× headroom. They are **not** guaranteed to catch the specific regression `CanvasScene`'s own doc comment warns against: reverting `nodes(intersecting:)` from filter-then-sort to sort-then-filter is roughly a `log(2000) ≈ 11`× constant factor, plausibly inside that headroom. Read the doc comment, don't lean on the budget.
- **Nothing MEASURES `CanvasRenderer.draw`'s cost.** It *is* exercised — `CanvasRegionRenderTests` renders it through `ImageRenderer` and reads pixels back, and 1C-b added five more raster fixtures that each render two scenes differing in exactly one model fact and count changed pixels. (This section used to say `draw` was not exercised at all, on the belief that `GraphicsContext` is not practically constructible in XCTest; corrected 2026-07-28.) None of those is a timing probe, so the transitive argument still carries the scale claim and is stated rather than left implicit: `draw`'s only scene-size-dependent step *is* `visibleNodes`, and its per-node body is bounded by the same output count, which `test_aFullSceneStillCullsToAHandful` already bounds below 60. That is an inference, not a measurement.
- **Lines are unbounded per-frame work too, and the projection is sort-then-filter.** `CanvasScene.drawnLines` reads `lines` — a full sort with `String` comparisons — compactMaps it, and only then does `visibleLines` cull, all inside the `Canvas` draw closure. That is the exact shape `nodes(intersecting:)`'s own doc comment warns against, and this file calls lines the one collection nothing bounds. Both doc comments are honest about it and **nothing measures it**: 1C-c1 added a probe for the accessibility rebuild and none for the draw path, so the 2,000-node claim rests on the same unstated inference as `draw` itself. Not a bug at any canvas anyone has drawn. **If it ever measures, the fix is `unorderedLines` plus cull-then-order** — precisely the `nodes`/`unorderedNodes` split already shipping one type up, not a cache.
- **What an item card costs, stated because 1C-d is the slice that makes item cards numerous** *(Task 5's review, M8/M9/D2; unmeasured, and this file does not use "measured" without a figure and a date).* Three things, none on a frame path that the slice made worse: `drawItemContent` calls `card.resolve(Text(…))` and `card.resolve(Image(systemName:))` **per visible item card per frame**, where a scrap's equivalent is cached in its `ScrapLayout` — inherited from the placeholder arm, which resolved one `Text` the same way, and bounded by the viewport rather than by the scene. `ProjectWindow` builds `CanvasItemIndex` **twice per body pass** (the canvas's own argument and `canvasInspector`'s), each a `TreeWalk` plus a `PaletteLookup` walk plus a dictionary build; one `let` in `body` would halve it, and it is `pieceChoices`' shape rather than a new one — the third builder, in `begin()`, is the promotion sheet opening and is not on that path. And `RegionInspector.rows(_:)` resolves `CanvasItemPresentation.facts` once per call, three calls per refresh, each a walk of the scene — behind the `(sceneRevision, regionID)` gate, so it runs on a structural change and not on a frame.
- **The region passes are the known unbounded per-frame work.** Tethers and appearance chips are recomputed every frame, walk every region's member sets with a sort per region, and are **not viewport-culled**. That is reasoning rather than measurement, for the reason above. When it is fixed, **cull the SEGMENT, never the region** — culling on the region hides exactly the line whose job is to say where the card went, and a segment with both endpoints off screen can still cross the viewport. Caching them on `sceneRevision` is the wrong instrument and the comment in `draw` says why: a node dragged *out* of its region bumps `revision` per frame and not `sceneRevision`, so a cached tether would not grow while the writer drags the card away and would snap into place on release.

**Getting things onto the canvas was the whole of this list and 1C-d built it.** Four paragraphs, one per half:

- **An item node has TWO provenances now** (§3.1's 2026-07-30 amendment) — *referenced*, a research item or palette card the project already holds and the canvas never writes to, and *owned*, a file under `canvas_assets/` the canvas ingested because it exists nowhere else. The distinction is **storage, not drawing**: both draw the same way, and `referenceId`/`ownedPath` are their two disjoint sidecar fields at schema 8. See "An item node has TWO provenances" under Persistence, and `Maugham/Stores/AREA.md` for the well.
- **An item node draws as itself** — the real title, the kind glyph, the photograph, a height measured from that picture's shape, a corner that resizes it, and its own inspector arm. The dashed placeholder card carrying `Item · <refId>` is gone with the `CGImageSource` downsampling path, the path-keyed bounded cache (tripwire 22) and the permanent shape memo that arrived with it. See "What an item node says it is" and "The item node's own inspector arm".
- **Three routes put something there, and every one of them is a *caller*** of the same two primitives rather than a storage decision of its own: a research row dragged out of the binder (§8A.1, "Dragging research onto the canvas"), a Finder file or a browser bitmap dropped from outside (§8A.1, "A photograph dropped from outside"), and a capture sent from the Inbox by drag or by command, for all three capture kinds (§8A.4, "A capture sent from the Inbox"). Claude's `add_canvas_scraps` is the fourth and predates them.
- **An owned picture promotes and a referenced one still does not** (§6's 2026-07-30 amendment, "The fourth row"), a region carries the pictures in it onto the palette card it produces (§6's 2026-07-29 amendment, "A region that holds a picture"), and `⌘\` gives the canvas the window (§8A.3, its own section above).

**One thing §8A named is deliberately still open and it is Claude's**, recorded here rather than as a gap: **Claude cannot see an owned picture's pixels.** `list_canvas` reports a node's `provenance` and never its path, on the grounds that nothing in the catalogue reads a file by project-relative path, so a path would dangle in exactly the field a reader would feed to another tool. What is missing is an image response keyed on a canvas node id — a new read tool and a new response shape — which is a decision rather than an omission, the treatment spec §8A.4's last paragraph gives `read_inbox_entry`.

**The MCP canvas surface was on this list and 1C-c3 built it** — `list_canvas` reads the whole canvas, `add_canvas_scraps` adds cards to it in a labelled region beside the page they were read off, and the writer is told by a banner and by three signals on the drawing itself. See "The MCP surface — how Claude reads and adds" above. **What it did not build is the page's *appearance***: an item node drew as a dashed placeholder carrying its reference id, so the reproduction corollary was structural there and visible at 1C-d — which Task 5 then paid. That was a slice boundary and not a gap in what shipped.

**Lines were on this list and 1C-c1 built them** — draw one by ⇧-dragging from any card or by dragging out of the connect mark on the selected one, click it to select, name it in the right-hand column, ⌫ to remove it. See "Lines" above; they are recorded there rather than quietly deleted from here, the same treatment regions and delete got.

**Promotion was on this list and 1C-c2 built it** — `Promote…` on the current selection (⌘⇧↩, or File → Promote…, or the button in every inspector arm whose subject can be promoted; `PromotionCommandTests`' wiring census is the array that says which, and 1C-d added a fourth arm to it) turns a scrap into a research note, a palette card or a craft intent, a region into a research note or a palette card, and a line into a `[[wiki-link]]` once both its cards have been promoted. See "Promotion" above. **Amendment, 2026-07-29 (1C-c2a):** a region's binding to a piece was one of the five targets at ship; it produced no artifact — `RegionInspector`'s own Piece picker already set the same field — so it is spec §6.2's piece *association* rather than a promotion target, and `.researchNote` took its place on the row. `RegionBinding` and the inspector's picker are unchanged; only the promotion-sheet door onto the same field is gone. **Two gaps it did not close, and neither is a bug in what shipped:** the promoted `[[…]]` is not carried by `propagateWikiLinkRename` (manuscript documents only), and it is not rendered as a link in `ResearchNoteEditor` or `ResearchNotePreviewPane`. The link layer *reads* it — that is what `list_all_links` and `find_references` gained in the same slice, and it is what §6.1's line row actually required.

**Regions and `CanvasModel` were on this list and 1C-b built them** — see "Regions, and why membership is never geometry" and "Who owns what" above. **Deleting a scrap was on it too**: ⌫ with something selected is `CanvasView.deleteSelection()`, reached from `CanvasEventNSView.keyDown` — see "Delete" above.

**Creating an appearance was on this list for exactly one commit and 1C-b closed it too** — the "Cite a Card" menu under "Appears here", `RegionInspector.cite`, described under "Regions, and why membership is never geometry" above. It is recorded rather than quietly deleted because the *shape* is the thing worth remembering: `CanvasMembership.addAppearance` was persisted, drawn as a chip, listed in the inspector and removable from it, with every one of those parts under test — and no caller. That is `CanvasScene.remove`'s failure recurring inside the slice that closed it, and neither was findable by a test. `grep -rn addAppearance Maugham/` was.

**This is a slice boundary, not a milestone one.** Spec §8A.1 puts **images inside milestone M1C** — "not deferred past it" — and says in the same breath that no plan may cite it as authorising their omission. Nothing here may be quoted to that end either. What 1C-a defers is the order of work, not the work.
