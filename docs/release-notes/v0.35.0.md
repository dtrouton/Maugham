# Maugham 0.35.0

_Released 2026-09-04_

The editorial letter: every check now ends with a letter about the piece as
a whole, written by a reader with a name — Le Guin, the coach, who reads
anything you have not handed to a pass. You can ask her something before
the check and she answers it first. What you learn from her letters goes
into a ledger of your own that every later check is briefed on. And the
letter is dosed by what you are doing: short while you draft, full while
you revise, and full whenever you ask for Fresh Eyes. Three plans, one
milestone, shipped whole.

## What's new

- **The letter.** A sixth section of every check, drawn at the top of the
  Diagnostics pane in Author and inside a disclosure on the round cockpit in
  Review, from one view. In reading order: the answer to what you asked,
  a one-sentence say-back of what the piece seems to be about as read,
  the one thing to fix if you fix only one, **What's working** with the
  principle behind each strength, **Habits** — a pattern across what was
  read, with what it costs the reader and an exercise you can **Accept as
  task** — **Questions** (each also arrives in your queue as a query),
  a **Scenes** table whose form follows the kind of piece and your own
  intent, the lessons a cold read looked for and did not find, and one
  sentence from Maugham's own numbers. Asked last, so you read the
  line-level notes while the letter is still being written.
- **Le Guin, the coach.** A seat beside the ladder rather than a rung on
  it. She reads every piece you have not assigned a pass to, files
  numbered rounds in her own lane, and signs what she writes. She attends
  to the sound of the sentences, point of view, and what the reader is
  made to feel; her line-level output is questions only — never a
  rewrite, never a correction. Author's Diagnostics header says who reads
  the piece; **Vacate the seat** in Project Settings makes an unassigned
  piece plain "Claude" again, and nothing is lost by vacating.
- **Scenes, three ways.** For a screenplay, or a piece whose intent says
  scenes must turn, the table judges each scene's turn; a lyric or
  essayistic piece gets no table at all; everything else gets the
  observational form. When the strong form came from the kind of piece
  rather than from your own words, a turn-less scene draws **Add to
  intent** — one click files the clause as a dated ruling, at the scope
  your intent actually resolves to.
- **Keep this letter** writes it, with its date and lane, as a research
  note where that piece's research already goes. Keeping twice makes two
  notes on purpose.
- **Ask about…** A one-line field in both homes, per piece, kept until
  you clear it: whatever stands there when you press ⌘R is what the round
  is briefed with, and the letter answers it first, directly, in full
  whatever the stage. Committed on submit and on focus loss; refused over
  its limit with your words left in the field.
- **What I've Learned** (⌘⌥G, Author and Review). A project-scoped ledger
  whose rows are live lessons, settled choices and dated retirements, in
  your own prose, moved only by your own verbs: **Keep as lesson** on a
  letter's habit or an accepted craft note in the queue; **This is a
  choice** on a queue question raised under a habit — a stet that also
  files — offered again on a second stet of the same habit, anywhere in
  the project; **These are all choices** over a Fresh Eyes letter's habits
  in one press; and **Retire**, which dates an entry in place rather than
  deleting it, offered when a cold read looked for a lesson and found no
  instance. Every round is briefed on what stands there, cites a known
  habit by its heading instead of rediscovering it, and never raises a
  choice.
- **Short while you draft.** The stage is derived, never set: a check over
  a delta that is mostly new prose at a frontier that moved this session
  is drafting, and its letter is short — about, what's working, at most
  one question, a habit only when it is everywhere, no exercise, no
  scenes — with a line under the title saying so and how to get the full
  one. A revising piece gets the full letter; **Fresh Eyes** always does.
  The lane line ends in the stage after a round: *Le Guin · round 3 ·
  drafting*.
- **Practice.** A fifth section of the Statistics window (File → Show
  Project Statistics), from your own edit history and nothing else: the
  frontier (the last paragraph you typed new, and how many sessions ago),
  the three paragraphs you have rewritten most in the last five sessions,
  and how long you were away. Each row opens its chapter. The same numbers
  brief a round only when one crosses a plain threshold — three sessions
  without a new paragraph, one paragraph rewritten five times in five
  sessions, two weeks away — so a quiet session says nothing, and the
  letter's process line has real numbers behind it. Nothing about them
  appears in the footer, the tree or the editor.
- **A fourth depth.** Beside Fast, Standard and Deep, **Exhaustive** — the
  one that earns its cost on a Fresh Eyes over a long piece. The depth
  menu is in both homes.
- **One MCP tool** (58 → 59): `read_lessons`, the fourth spine reader —
  Claude reads the ledger and never writes it. `list_annotations` and
  `get_annotation` report the habit heading a question was raised under.

## Fixes

- In Review, a long letter in the round cockpit's disclosure could not be
  read: it pushed the queue off the pane and nothing scrolled. The
  disclosure now scrolls within a bounded height, and the queue stays
  reachable beneath it.

## Under the hood

- No manifest schema change — Mac-only release; MaughamPhone stays
  current. The coach's seat is a tolerated-missing manifest field (an
  older build re-saving the manifest forgets a vacated seat, which is
  recoverable); the ask is per-device derived state; the letter and its
  stage and process line are optional keys on the diagnostics sidecar,
  which older builds read unchanged.
- The compiler still reads and never writes: the letter's questions mint
  through the same annotation door as every other note, the ledger is
  written only by your verbs through one file, and a ruling's identity is
  the heading decided app-side, so a heading the model re-spelled writes
  nothing.
- Process signals are computed off the op log alone and never leave the
  document: sessions split on a 30-minute gap, the frontier moves only on
  your own typing (never a rewind, an import or an accepted suggestion),
  and the Statistics walk runs off the main actor.

## Known issues

- A letter question draws in both the letter and This check — one finding
  shown twice, by design; judge it on screen.
- At the smallest window, the queue keeps about one row beneath an open
  letter in the cockpit.
- On a large project with a cold cache, the first **Stet** press in
  document scope pays a walk of every chapter's history before it can
  offer *This is a choice*.
- A Practice row opens the chapter, not the paragraph; the row shows the
  paragraph's opening words instead.
- A drafting check keeps only the first question the letter raises; the
  others are not shown until a revising check or Fresh Eyes.
