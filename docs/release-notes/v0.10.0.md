# Maugham 0.10.0

_Released 2026-06-10_

Big manuscripts. This release is about Maugham staying fast and trustworthy
as a project grows — a feature-length screenplay in one file, a novel with a
long editing history — without ever giving up its promise that your full
history is kept.

## What's new

- **Typing stays fast in long documents.** Editing a 70-page (and beyond)
  single-file screenplay used to get slower with every page; keystrokes could
  visibly lag. Maugham now restyles only the part of the document your edit
  actually changed, stopped re-reading the whole manuscript several times per
  keystroke, and computes the Scenes sidebar's page numbers in one pass.
  Typing in long documents now feels like typing in short ones.
- **Your history takes far less space — and far less syncing.** Maugham keeps
  every edit you've ever made (that's what powers History and Rewind). It now
  writes that history much more compactly: ordering information is recorded
  only when it changes, and once a document's history file grows past a
  threshold it is sealed into a compressed, checksummed archive segment.
  In testing, a feature-length screenplay's history shrank by more than 80%,
  and the data re-uploaded to iCloud per editing burst dropped by ~90%.
  Rewind still scrubs back through every edit, including sealed history, and
  restoring to a point inside a sealed segment works exactly as before.
- **Sealed history is tamper-evident.** Each sealed segment carries a
  checksum. If a file is ever damaged (disk fault, bad sync), Maugham
  quarantines the damage, recovers everything readable, flags the project as
  needing attention, and pauses backups rather than propagating corruption.

## Fixes

- **Pasting large amounts of text could crash — or worse.** Paragraph
  identities are assigned randomly, and pasting hundreds of paragraphs into a
  long document could assign the same identity twice: at best a crash, at
  worst two paragraphs silently sharing one history. Identities are now
  guaranteed unique, and documents written by older versions that already
  carry a duplicate are handled gracefully instead of crashing.
- **Rewind preview went blank near the end of history in very long
  documents.** The history was always intact — the preview pane simply
  couldn't draw documents past a certain size. It now renders documents of
  any length.
- **Rapid edits could land in history out of order.** Two edits recorded in
  the same millisecond had a coin-flip chance of replaying in reverse, which
  could let the older text win. Edit identifiers are now strictly ordered.
- **The word-count footer could briefly show the previous document's count**
  after switching documents quickly.

## Known issues

- Very long single-file screenplays (roughly 150+ pages) can still show a
  slight keystroke lag and a brief catch-up moment after you pause typing.
  The remaining work is identified and scheduled; it does not affect novels
  split into chapters.
- Signing, notarization, in-place updates, backups, and the iPhone companion
  are unchanged from 0.9.0.
