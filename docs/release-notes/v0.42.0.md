# Maugham 0.42.0

_Released YYYY-MM-DD_

Your devices are you. A Mac or iPhone signed into your Apple ID now joins
your books as you, without asking, and a collaborator's new device joins as
her once you've let one of hers in. Pairs with MaughamPhone 0.15.0 —
**update your Mac and your iPhone together.**

## What's new

- **Your own devices are recognised.** Maugham keeps a key for you in iCloud
  Keychain, so every device signed into your Apple ID holds the same one. Each
  device uses it to say whose it is, and the Mac that holds the book lets it in
  as you, with no panel. It writes the whole book, as you do. History records
  it: *Denver's iPhone joined as you — recognised by your Apple ID.*
- **A collaborator's other devices join as her.** The first time one of Sam's
  devices turns up, you are asked as before. Once you've let it in, the book
  links her Apple ID to her, and her next device joins as Sam, with whatever
  you've said she may write here. This happens separately in each book.
- **The admission panel says more about a device you don't know.** It shows
  what the device says about itself (*It calls itself Sam's MacBook Air,
  belonging to Sam Ruiz.*), marked as the device's own word. In a shared
  folder it also shows who iCloud says last saved the device's record, and
  says so plainly when that isn't the name the device gives. Neither ever
  fills in the name for you.
- **People & Devices says who is recognised**, and on the Mac that holds the
  book, **Forget This Person Key** stops a person's Apple ID letting new
  devices in without a panel — for use if that Apple ID may be in somebody
  else's hands. The devices already let in stay. Forgetting lasts, and History
  records the day.
- **Revoking a device says it doesn't forget the Apple ID it carried**, wherever
  one is linked, and points you at Forget if that's what you meant.

What it needs: iCloud Keychain on, and the same Apple ID, on both devices.
Without it nothing changes: a device is asked about once, as before, and then
goes in silently.

## Fixes

- **A device you'd named in another book no longer skips the panel in a book
  where that name has limited permission.** It used to go in as an author of
  the whole book. It is now asked about, so you can say what it may write.

## Known issues

- An iPhone never makes the key itself; it waits for it to arrive from one of
  your Macs. An iPhone used before any of your Macs has run this version is
  asked about once.
- The iCloud line on the panel only appears where iCloud knows who saved the
  file, and never for a file you saved yourself. It names whoever saved the
  device's record last, which is usually, but not always, the person at that
  device. It can also differ harmlessly from the device's own name, for
  example when iCloud has somebody under a nickname.
- A Mac or iPhone still on an older version can rewrite its own entry without
  the line saying whose it is. It is recognised again once it updates.
