# Maugham 0.39.0

_Released 2026-XX-XX_ <!-- draft: Denver names the number and the date -->

People and admission. Your book now knows which machines may write in it,
and you are the one who says so. The first time your iPhone or a second Mac
writes into a project, Maugham asks you once who it is; you give it a name,
and from then on it goes in everywhere silently. Work from a machine you
haven't named yet is **held** — not in your draft, not deleted, not touched
— until you say the word. There is a **People & Devices** list in Project
Settings for the rest of it: revoke a person, retire a Mac you're finished
with, merge two Macs of your own, or claim a book you hold no key in.
Nothing here is a lock and nothing refuses to open: this is provenance, and
your own words on the Mac in front of you are never affected by any of it.
(ADR 0032's P2 addendum; spec
`2026-09-09-signed-op-log-p2-people-and-admission-design.md`. Pairs with
MaughamPhone 0.13.0.)

## What's new

- **One panel, one machine, once.** A device writing into a book that
  doesn't know it raises a small panel over that project's window: the
  machine's own name, its four-character code, and how much is waiting. You
  type a name — **the name is yours, not the machine's**, because a person
  is what you call a keyboard rather than what it calls itself. Type a name
  that's already in the book and that machine joins that person instead of
  minting a second one by the same name. **Not now** writes nothing and
  asks again at the next open; Escape does the same.
- **Then silently, everywhere.** After you've named a machine once, every
  later project it has written into admits it without asking. Maugham
  remembers the name on this Mac, not in the book, because what you decided
  to call a machine is a fact about your decision rather than about a
  folder.
- **The code is there to be compared.** The panel shows four characters;
  the phone shows the same four at the top of **Settings → This device**.
  If they match, it's that phone.
- **People & Devices**, in Project Settings. Everyone in the book, their
  machines nested under them, what each machine does, and when it was
  added. Beneath them: Macs you've merged, anything claiming the book, and
  machines you once named whose record has gone.
- **Revoke** stops Maugham applying what a person's machines write from
  here on. What they wrote before stays in your manuscript, untouched, and
  Maugham tells you so beside the button — along with the thing that is
  easy to assume and wrong: revoking doesn't stop them *writing*, it stops
  Maugham *applying*. Removing them from the iCloud share is what stops
  them writing. A revocation is confirmed first, and **Re-admit** is there
  if you change your mind.
- **Maugham can tell a change made after you revoked from one that was
  merely slow to arrive**, and says which is which rather than accusing a
  note that was already on its way.
- **Retire** is for a Mac you're finished with — sold, wiped, replaced —
  and you can only retire the one you're sitting at. Afterwards every other
  machine holds back what this one writes, while this one carries on quite
  normally in its own copy, so Maugham says plainly, in History and on the
  row, that what you write here now stays here. **A Mac can't be
  un-retired**; come back to it and it comes back as a new machine.
- **"Is this book yours?"** A Mac holding a book it has no key in — restored
  from a backup, handed to you, or your old Mac is gone — is asked this
  once per project per launch. **Claim** makes this Mac an owner and takes
  in everything already there. **Not mine** writes nothing at all.
- **Two Macs that both started as owners can merge**, and neither gives
  way. **Merge: this is also me** on the other Mac's row takes in its
  history; the same button pressed over there takes in yours. Nobody's book
  becomes a copy of anybody's.
- **History tells you trust in two shapes.** Banners at the top are things
  that are true now — *14 notes from iPhone are waiting for admission*,
  *this Mac is on Denver's chain*. Under a **Project** heading above the
  timeline are dated entries for things that happened — admitted on 3 June,
  revoked on 9 September, this book claimed, a record put back. The
  timeline below is one document's; that section is the whole book's.
- **The Inbox says when captures are waiting** — a banner with its own
  Admit… — so a phone syncing into a book that shows none of it is no
  longer silent. Each capture row also says who it came from.
- **Every record is signed, and a signature covers what a later version of
  Maugham might add to it.** A record written by a newer build carrying a
  field this one has never heard of still verifies, and a revocation
  written here doesn't quietly drop it.
- **A record can only change hands under the same key.** Another Mac can no
  longer write over your Mac's own record of this book and take the chain
  with it; the attempt is refused, your copy stands, and it shows up as a
  claimant instead.
- **If somebody deletes a record out of the folder, Maugham puts it back
  and says when** — in History, and as a mark on the row.

## Changed

- **History from a second device that had rotated its files is now held
  until that device is admitted**, where before it was applied as unsigned
  history. This is the one visible change to a book you already have: open
  it after updating and some history that was in your draft may read as
  *waiting for admission* instead. Nothing is deleted and nothing is
  rewritten — admit the device and all of it comes back at once. (A
  device rotating its own files is maintenance it performs on itself, and
  it should never have amounted to letting it in.)
- **The waiting counts count notes and captures, not bookkeeping lines.**
  Three ops under one signature used to read as four.
- The chain banner now reads *this Mac **is on** X's chain* — a fact that
  holds — with the joining itself moved to a dated entry below it.

## Known issues

- **A device that has written only into chapters you haven't opened isn't
  asked about until you open one.** The question is put on the load path,
  so no chapter can be opened without it, but a project-open sweep of the
  whole book is a later release.
- **A revoked device's pre-revocation work stays held, with no way to let
  just that part in.** The honest fix needs Maugham to remember a
  per-stretch exception, which is a later release. Re-admitting the device
  brings everything in.
- **Revoking and then re-admitting somebody reads as one admission rather
  than two dated events**, because the entries are derived from the records
  and re-admission clears the revocation. A durable log of trust events is
  a later release.
- **Admission is a Mac act.** The phone shows where it stands and has
  nothing to press.

## Before you smoke this build

**Delete the dev registries first.** P2a's signed records do not verify
under this release's canonical digest, which was deliberately changed while
nothing had shipped. In each dev project remove `.maugham/people` and
`.maugham/devices`, and remove the dev variant's `registry-cache.json` and
`admission-memory.json` from its Application Support directory. Otherwise
every record reads as tampered with, which is the right answer to the wrong
question.
