# Maugham 0.34.0

_Released 2026-09-02_

The translation pipeline: three people per language instead of one, a Run
that puts all of them to work in seven fixed legs, directives and a glossary
you write against your own English, a round report written to you in your
language, and briefs that Claude can propose but never write. Five plans,
one milestone, shipped whole.

## What's new

- **Three people per language.** Beside the translator, every edition now
  has a **blind reader** — target text only, so it can say the prose does
  not read like this language and can never say it is wrong — and a
  **collator**, who holds both texts and owns the *departures* judgement,
  each departure carrying a **gloss**: a literal back-rendering into your
  language, which is what makes it rulable by somebody who cannot read the
  translation. Names come from literary history like the rest of the cast;
  five languages arrive with all three picked (Spanish, French, German,
  Japanese, and now Serbian: Kiš, Sekulić, Vinaver), and **Rename …** on a
  language row offers all three. The cast sheet names them for an unlisted
  language on its first Run.
- **One Run is seven legs.** Translate, a cold read, fix, read again, fix,
  collate, fix — fixed depth, deliberately not adaptive, each cold session
  sealed (no tools, no bridge, blind by construction). **Run Whole Book**
  walks the desk's standing imprint in binder order; **Cancel** reaches the
  live leg; the row's status line names the leg and the chapter, then
  `Round N · finished 2m ago`, the notes-per-round trend, and a pre-flight
  (`7 legs · ~N words briefed`). Skips are recorded, never silent; a
  failing leg ends the round where it broke and keeps what earlier legs
  wrote.
- **Directives.** Select a passage in the English and press ⌘⌥C
  (**Translator's note…**): "this repetition is deliberate", "one sentence,
  not two". It lands as a paragraph-anchored ruling in the piece's craft
  intent (every edition) or one language's edition brief, reaches the
  translator as an instruction, the collator as the standard for that
  paragraph, and the reader through the brief. A directive whose paragraph
  is gone draws as an orphan with a Remove, never silently dropped.
- **The glossary is a table.** `«October» → «Octubre» (the month, never a
  name)` is a ruling of a recognised shape, drawn as a table above the other
  rulings in the edition brief — readable by an author who cannot read the
  language. Entries come from you, from a round's proposals (**Adopt** /
  **Skip** on the report), or from a proposed brief.
- **The round report.** **Show** on a language row opens it in the centre
  column, over the chapter's own editor: the reader's verdicts from leg 2
  beside leg 4, every paragraph the translator changed with before and
  after, the departures with **Fine** / **Keep mine** / **Make it a rule**,
  the disagreements, the questions for you, the glossary proposals, the
  summary. Every sentence on it is in your language; the translated text
  appears only in a disclosure you open on purpose. A row's reveal line
  takes you to that paragraph in Translation Review. Only declined notes
  and questions reach your queue — as notes from the reader or collator who
  raised them, with the translator's reason.
- **Gloss and Ask the collator.** Two buttons in the Translation pane
  (⌘⌥L) for the paragraph under the caret: a literal gloss of what the
  translation now says, or the collator's full comparison of that one pair,
  with the same Fine / Keep mine / Make it a rule. Both are cold calls;
  neither writes anything.
- **Proposals into statements.** From Claude Desktop or Claude Code, the
  new `edition-brief` and `visual-language` skills interview you and end by
  calling `propose_edition_brief` or `propose_visual_language` — which
  stage a draft and write nothing. A banner in the statement's pane shows
  who proposed what and when, why, a line-by-line diff against your current
  text, and **Adopt** / **Discard**. Adopt replaces the prose through the
  same path as your own typing (one ⌘Z takes it back), leaves your Rulings
  byte for byte, appends the proposal's glossary entries as rulings, and is
  all-or-nothing. A **Proposed** mark on the desk's language row and a mark
  on the ⌘⌥V segment say one is waiting. Craft intent cannot be proposed
  at all: there is no tool for it, on purpose.
- **Two MCP tools** (56 → 58): `propose_edition_brief` and
  `propose_visual_language`. `translation_status` now reports each
  edition's `reader`, `collator` and `last_round`.

## Fixes

- The desk's language row no longer truncates its verbs: Edition Brief,
  Run, Run Whole Book and Cancel sit on a line of their own and wrap when
  the column is narrow.
- Show works with the round's own chapter selected; it used to draw only
  once you moved to the project row.
- The translation cast runs on a 900-second per-turn budget (the compiler
  keeps 300). A translate leg sends a whole chapter in one turn, and long
  chapters were timing out with nothing written.

## Under the hood

- No manifest schema change — Mac-only release; MaughamPhone stays
  current. Reader and collator roles decode as unknown on older builds and
  re-encode verbatim.
- ADR 0030 — three people, seven legs, directives as rulings, and
  proposals into statements — amends ADR 0024 and records that ADR 0028's
  "one door into a statement" survives both a directive (through the door)
  and a proposal (stopping at it).
- Every cold session spawns with `--tools ""`, `--strict-mcp-config` and
  no `--mcp-config` at all; a grep census pins that no cold call can ever
  be handed a bridge.
- `CompilerAllowlistTests`' statement census now catches
  `write_edition_brief` and `propose_craft_intent` while passing exactly
  the two proposal tools.

## Known issues

- A translate or fix leg still sends a chapter's whole work-list in one
  turn; a very long chapter can exceed even the 900-second budget. Chunking
  the work-list is the recorded follow-on.
- Round numbers run per language across chapters; the report header names
  the chapter.
- A translator's reason for declining a note lives in the query's body
  rather than as a reply of its own.
- In a whole-book Run, a closed chapter's earlier queries are not briefed
  to the translator; the open chapter's are.
- The desk names a language two ways: rows and help carry the tag
  (`Serbian (sr)`), the line for a proposed brief with no row does not.
