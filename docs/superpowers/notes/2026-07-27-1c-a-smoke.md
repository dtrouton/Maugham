# 1C-a smoke — the planning canvas

*Branch `feat/planning-canvas-1c-a-2026-07-25`, HEAD `10183b3`. Mac suite 2943/0,
Release green, whole-branch review passed with no Critical.*

This merges the plan's own smoke list with everything the seventeen tasks routed
to your eye. **Parts 1–2 are the ones that catch what this architecture exists to
prevent — don't skim them.** Parts 8–9 are judgment calls, not pass/fail.

Against each check: what you should see, and — the useful half — **what a failure
would mean**, so you can tell me something more precise than "it looked wrong".

## Part 1 — the four that must not be skimmed

**1.1 ⌘1 (Plan).** The canvas fills the centre column; the binder on the left
shows the **research tree**.
*Failure:* the persona registry or one of the seven `BinderSegment` switches is
routing wrong.

**1.2 Double-click empty space, type a sentence.** Words appear on the card.
*Failure:* the editor isn't first responder, or the transparent event view is in
front of it. **Both were live defects during the build.**

**1.3 Now quit with ⌘Q — without clicking away first.** Relaunch, reopen: the
sentence is there.
*Failure:* the writer's words only ever lived in the editor's `NSTextStorage`.
This is the product constitution's must #1. **Do not substitute "click away, then
quit"** — that's a different code path and it passes when this one doesn't.

**1.4 Keep typing past the end of the first line.** The card grows as the text
wraps.
*Failure:* nothing is re-measuring while you type — and the same gap is what
loses the words in 1.3.

## Part 2 — the seam (§7A.2), where drawn text meets edited text

**2.1 Click away, then double-click back into the middle of a word.** The caret
lands where you aimed, and **the text does not move by a hair** on focus or on
blur.
*Failure:* the drawn layout and the edited layout disagree. Tests assert glyph
geometry to within a pixel; only your eye catches a jump inside that tolerance.
This is the failure the whole architecture was chosen to prevent.

**2.2 Watch a card as you click into it.** It animates to level over about a
tenth of a second, and leans back to its angle when you click away.
*Failure:* an instant snap reads as a rendering bug; more than about a fifth of a
second reads as lag.

**2.3 Double-click empty canvas and type immediately.** No characters lost, and
the text doesn't jump. **Both halves matter and they fail in opposite
directions.** What you should see: the words appearing on the card *as it rotates
up to square*, with the caret arriving a beat after them.
*Failure A* — a missing first character or two: the editor isn't mounted until
the card is level, so those keystrokes reached nothing.
*Failure B* — glyphs snapping straight the instant you click, with the chrome
swinging up behind them: the editor was made visible before the card was level.

**2.4 Click into a card, then onto empty canvas, and keep watching it.** It leans
back within about a tenth of a second.
*Failure:* a card that stays perfectly square after you leave means the straighten
clock reported "settled" on blur and stopped — and **from then on nothing else on
the canvas animates either.**

**2.5 Double-click into a second scrap.** You are editing the second one.
*Failure:* an editor still bound to the first is invisible to every automated
check we have.

## Part 3 — camera and ground

**3.1 Pan the canvas. The grain must not crawl.**
*Failure:* the shader is sampling screen space instead of content space.

**3.2 Zoom to ~3× and back out.** Text stays crisp at every step, and the point
under the pointer stays under the pointer.
*Failure:* crisp text is the whole reason zoom is a manual transform rather than
`.scaleEffect`; blur means something reintroduced it.

**3.3 Scroll and pinch with the pointer *over a focused scrap*.** The canvas
still pans and zooms, and the pinch zooms about the point under your fingers.
*Failure:* the editor is swallowing the gesture, or its anchor point isn't being
mapped out of editor space — that compiles fine and zooms about somewhere you
never touched.

## Part 4 — motion (§7.3)

**4.1 Drag a scrap and let go with some speed.** It carries and comes to rest
rather than stopping dead.

**4.2 Move a card quickly, hold it still for a moment, then release.** It stays
where you parked it.
*Failure:* the release is reading the velocity from before your pause and
throwing a card you'd already put down. (Fixed during the build; this is the
regression check.)

**4.3 Drag a scrap's bottom-right corner.** It rewraps, and stays clickable
afterwards.
*Failure:* if it vanishes, a resize cleared the cached height without
re-measuring — an unmeasured card is invisible to hit-testing and to the
renderer. (Also fixed during the build.)

---

## Part 5 — undo

**5.1 ⌘Z once after a drag** undoes the whole drag, and returns the card to where
the drag **started** — not to where it stopped coasting.

**5.2 Type, then drag, then ⌘Z twice.** The drag comes back first, then the
typing. **Then press ⌘Z a third time** — it must either undo something real or do
nothing because the stack is empty.
*Failure:* a ⌘Z that visibly does nothing *while the menu still says it can undo*
is the double-registration defect returning.

**5.3 Type three sentences into one scrap without leaving it, then ⌘Z three
times.** Each press takes back roughly one sentence — not all three at once, not
one character. Then pause a couple of seconds mid-sentence, type more, and ⌘Z
once: only what you typed after the pause comes back.

**5.4 Type in one scrap, click into a second, ⌘Z, then type in the second and
click away.** The first scrap stays reverted.
*Failure:* if your ⌘Z quietly comes undone when you leave the second scrap, the
open gesture's baseline was never refreshed after the undo.

**5.5 Double-click empty canvas, type, ⌘Z, ⌘Z.** After the second the card is
gone — and **the canvas still responds to drags**.
*Failure:* a surface that ignores every drag until you happen to click somewhere.
(Fixed during the build; this is the regression check.)

**5.6 ⌘Z during a coast** — flick a card and press ⌘Z within about a second. It
returns to where the drag started and **stays there** rather than resuming its
skate from the old position.

## Part 6 — accessibility

**6.1 Turn on VoiceOver, focus the canvas, and walk it.** Each scrap announces
itself with its text; entering one lands you in a real text field.

**6.2 With three or four cards down, walk the whole canvas.** Reading order is
rows then columns — roughly level cards read left to right as one row.

**6.3 Flick a card, then press to stop the coast, then walk the canvas in
VoiceOver.** The card announces its *current* position.
*Failure:* pointing at where it was released, up to ~200pt away. (Found and fixed
by the whole-branch review — this is the regression check.)

## Part 7 — persistence

**7.1 Quit with ⌘Q within a second of the last drag.** Relaunch, reopen: the
scrap is where you left it, with its words.
*(This is the path `.onDisappear` alone does not cover.)*

**7.2 Delete `.maugham/canvas.json` by hand and reopen.** The layout is gone; the
words are not.

**7.3 Open `canvas.md` at the project root in any editor.** It's readable — one
`##` heading per scrap, your text beneath.

## Part 8 — judgment calls, routed to your eye

These aren't pass/fail. They're decisions I deliberately didn't make on paper.

**8.1 Dark mode.** Switch the system appearance and look at the canvas properly.
**No test in the slice rasterises dark mode** — both raster fixtures pin light —
so dark-mode legibility rests on a colour-level contrast pin, which is a faithful
proxy but not the same thing. Specifically: are the cards readable, and do they
read as *objects sitting on* the ground rather than holes cut out of it?

**8.2 The ground's material, light vs dark.** Spec §7.1 asks for *"a muted canvas
weave"* in light and *"slate under a lamp — a different material, not the same
texture inverted"* in dark. **As built, one grain algorithm runs in both** and
only the fill colour changes. Does that read as two materials or one recoloured?
If one, the fix is a second noise character in the shader — a real piece of work,
and I'd rather you saw it before I invented it.

**8.3 The focused scrap is announced twice to VoiceOver, once stale.** The
synthetic card stays in the tree beside the real text field while you're editing,
carrying the text as of when you *entered* (it refreshes on leaving). Walk the
canvas mid-edit and judge whether that's confusing enough to fix.

**8.4 The beat between click and caret.** ~120ms by design. Does it read as
responsiveness or as lag?

**8.5 Shadows when zoomed out.** Zoom out to ~0.2× and pan. Shadows may appear a
frame late at the viewport edge. Reasoned to be sub-pixel and invisible at that
zoom, but never measured against the running app.

## Part 9 — known-open, please don't chase

- **The texture disappears below ~0.25× zoom.** Deliberate — it's the anti-moiré
  fade, not a bug.
- **An item/placeholder card** (dashed border, "Item · …") only appears if a
  sidecar already contains one. 1C-a is scraps-only by design; 1C-d builds the
  real thing.
- **A stray double-click leaves a permanent empty card.** There is **no delete**
  anywhere in this slice — ⌘Z immediately after takes it back, but once you click
  away the card is there for good. Now recorded as an open scope question; my
  recommendation is that 1C-b owns it, since regions force the question anyway.
- **Clicking into a focused scrap in an inactive window** may spend the click
  activating the window. One-line fix available; left because it wants exactly
  this kind of hands-on check.
- **The phone suite** is environmentally blocked here (simulator wedged, three
  launch failures, zero tests run). Worth a run on your machine — the branch
  touches no phone or MaughamCore files, so anything other than 221/0 would mean
  I'm wrong about that.
