# Checking Your Writing

Maugham can read what you have just written and check it two ways at once — as a continuity editor, against what you've declared and what the manuscript has already established, and as a reader, reporting what happens in the reading itself. Press **⌘R** — **File ▸ Check Writing** — and the chapter's reader does the reading. Who that is is yours to choose: **Le Guin**, the coach; **your own first reader**, one person you name and describe; or nobody in particular, in which case a check's notes are signed *Claude*.

What comes back is in [To resolve](right-column.md#to-resolve-1) (⌘1): the letter at its head, and what the check found listed under **Checks** (⌘⌥D). The head also names who reads this chapter — *"Le Guin reads this chapter"* (in the project's word for a piece), which is itself the picker — beside **Reread**. A capsule flashes **Checking…**, the same register as ⌘S's; press ⌘R again while one is running and it flashes **Still checking…** instead of starting a second.

The report arrives in parts: your clauses come back first, while the reader's report is still being written, and the head keeps saying **Checking…** until the whole check is done. A check you cancel takes its half-finished report with it and puts the last completed one back.

It runs when you press the key, and at no other time. There is no background check, no linting as you type, no badge while you are mid-sentence. The point is a check you can reach for constantly because it is cheap, not one that reaches for you.

## Who reads a check

**Three answers, and which one is yours to pick**: the coach, your own first reader, or nobody in particular. It is one answer for the whole project, and none of it touches your review passes — a check is filed in no pass.

### The coach

Above the four review passes sits one more editor who is **not** a pass: **Le Guin**, the coach. She is the default reader of your checks — every one, on every chapter, whatever pass it is in. The passes are stages of finishing; she is the reader for work that isn't there yet, and what she writes is a letter about the chapter rather than a stack of line notes.

She sits outside the ladder on purpose. She is never a column in the Book's Status, never a rung on a chapter's ladder, and never something a chapter can be "done" with. A check takes no round number and stamps nothing, so nothing you do here moves a pass's count.

If you'd rather not have a coach, **vacate the seat** in **Project Settings…** (⌘⇧,). A check is then read by nobody in particular and signed *Claude*. Her past letters stay in the chapter's history, and restoring the seat brings her back where she left off.

### Your own first reader

The other person who can read your checks is one you name yourself: **one specific reader you write toward**. Stephen King calls this the Ideal Reader — not an audience but a particular person whose eyebrow you can see going up while you write.

Name her in **Project Settings…**, in the **First reader** row beneath the coach's, then press **Describe…** to pull up her statement (⌘⌥Y) and write down who she is. Typing the name is enough on its own — she can read for you before you have described her, and the check says so rather than inventing a taste for her.

**What to write.** Who she is and what she already knows about this story; what she reads, and loves in it; what she will not sit through. Ordinary prose — there is no form and nothing is parsed out of it. The more particular, the less she reads like anyone.

**The rows beneath the essay are standing instructions**, under **Instructions**: things she is to read under every time. Every check hands them to her. They are made by answering one of her notes (below), and each carries **Edit** and **Revoke**.

**She reports what happened in her, and never a fix.** Four things only: where the dream broke, what she now believes to be true, where she got bored and would have skimmed, and where she lost track of what was happening or who was speaking. She quotes the words that did it. She is forbidden craft words — no *pacing*, no *structure*, no *voice*, no *arc* — and forbidden to propose a change: *"I started skimming around the dinner"* rather than *"the pacing sags"*. A report shaped like a fix is dropped.

**Her letter is short**: the answer to your ask, one sentence on what the chapter seems to be about, what she loved and why, and at most one question. No habits, no exercise, no scene table. A Reread with her reads the whole chapter cold and still writes the short form.

**She isn't told about your ledger or your process.** What you have filed in [What I've Learned](right-column.md#what-ive-learned-g), and the numbers Maugham counts off your edit history, never reach her. Both are about how you write; she is here to tell you what reading it was like.

**The books she loves are how she measures yours.** What the chapter's fold holds reaches her as her taste — the things she reads and loves — and she measures the chapter against them by name. Pinning the writers you want her to have read is how you tune her.

**Answering one of her notes can become a standing instruction.** In To resolve, a note of hers you have not settled carries **Answer as ruling…**. Type the answer and it lands twice: as a dated line under her **Instructions**, and as your reply on the note, which leaves the list. *"I kept waiting for the sister to come back"* is answered once — *"she has read the first two books, so nothing about Marnie is new to her"* — rather than every check.

### Choosing between them

The line *"Le Guin reads this chapter"* (in the project's word for a piece) in To resolve's head is a **picker**. Open it to hand your checks to the coach, to your own first reader, or to nobody in particular. Only readers the project has are offered; if you have not named a first reader, the menu ends with **Define a first reader…**.

The choice is remembered on this Mac and applies to the whole project. If you later take away what it rests on — vacate the seat, clear the name — checks fall back to the default rather than to a reader who isn't there. A chapter you have never checked says who will read it before you press anything: *"Press ⌘R and Nadia reads what you've written."*

Rounds are the other loop, with an editor of their own: see [Review Passes](review-passes.md).

## What it reads

- **Your intent** — the chapter's **Going for** in its [Brief](right-column.md#brief-2), or the book's if the chapter has none — read as checkable clauses, your own sentences quoted back exactly. With no intent at all the check still runs; it simply has no clauses to hold you to.
- **What has already been established** — facts, who knows what, voices — read off your manuscript by earlier checks and kept in Brief under **What Claude has read** (see "The bible, filling quietly" below).
- **What has changed since the last check.** Not the whole chapter: the first check reads everything, and each one after picks up where the last left off, so a check mid-session is about the paragraphs you just wrote.
- **What the chapter's fold holds** — its research and canvas cards, named by title, alongside the tool that would look closer. Claude can't look into a photograph of yours this way, and the check says so.

The head tells you when it last ran and how much it looked at ("3 new, 2 revised ¶").

## Asking for something in particular

**Ask about…** is a one-line field, and To resolve's head has two: one in the check's line, for the reader who checks, and one under Run, for the editor who runs the round. Type what you want this run to look at — *I'm worried the middle sags*, *is her voice distinct from his?* — and press Return. Clicking away saves it too, and so does pressing ⌘R: whatever is in the field when you ask is what the run is briefed with. The ✕ withdraws it.

It's kept per chapter until you clear it, because a worry outlasts one run. The letter answers it **first**, in the same voice and never as a rewrite. **It never starts a run** — the key is still the only trigger. Keep it to a sentence or two: over 400 characters is turned away, your words left in the field to shorten.

## What comes back

### The letter

**The letter comes first**, at the head of To resolve, because it's what you read first. It's a letter about the draft, signed — *— Le Guin · drafting*: a check carries no round number, so the signature names the **stage** it read your draft at. A letter written while you're drafting says so under its title, naming Reread as the way to ask for the whole thing. **Earlier letters** reaches the ones before.

If you asked something, it **answers that first**. Then: what the chapter seems to be **about** as read — one sentence to compare with your own intent; **the one thing** to fix if you fix only one; **What's working**, and why, so the strength is repeatable; **Habits**, a pattern across what was read, with what it costs the reader; **Questions** the draft raised; a **Scenes** table; a line for any lesson in your ledger it looked for and didn't find; and, under *From Maugham's numbers*, one sentence about **your own process** — how you've been coming back to this chapter, what you keep reworking — off numbers Maugham counted. A part with nothing in it draws nothing.

**That is the craft letter.** A check read by your own first reader comes back in her short form instead.

Every reference is the words that paragraph says now, as a chip you click to jump there — never a paragraph id.

A habit can come with an exercise — something to *do*, never a rewrite: *rewrite the scene without a single "was"*. **Accept as To-Do** files it as a [to-do](tasks.md) at the first paragraph the habit shows in. It's one undo step, and pressing it again does nothing.

**The scenes table** has three columns — wants, changes, turn — and a fourth, charge, only when the reading has a charge to report. A blank cell stays blank. Which table you get depends on what you've said:

- **Nothing at all.** Prose gets the weak form: something should change here, as observation, with no charge column.
- **Your own words.** Write *every scene must turn* in your intent — or take the offer below — and a turn-less scene is also raised as a finding, quoting your clause.
- **Not scene-driven.** Write *lyric*, *essayistic*, *meander* or *not scene-driven* and there's no table.

A screenplay is in the strong form by its own form, and so is a prose chapter whose pass brief asks for it. Neither is a sentence you wrote, so neither earns a finding — instead the table ends with **Hold every scene to a turn?** and a button that files *"Every scene must turn."* as a dated decision **in the intent this chapter is measured against**: **Add to intent** for its own, **Add to the book's intent** when it reads against the book's. Taking it never detaches a chapter from the book. The offer goes once a clause exists.

**Keep this letter** saves the whole letter, dated, as a research note — *Letter from <editor> — <date>* — that becomes the chapter's. The run's own letter ages out; a kept one is yours to edit. Keeping twice makes two notes.

**Keep as lesson** files a habit the letter raises into your ledger (⌘⌥G), dated, carrying the letter it came from. Every later check is briefed with it. A lesson already in the ledger stops being offered.

Two verbs belong to a cold read (a Reread, or a round with Fresh Eyes), because they are claims about the whole chapter. **These are all choices** files every habit in the letter as a settled choice in one press — for a finished draft where the "problems" are how you write. And when a cold read looked for a lesson you carry and found no instance of it, the letter offers **Retire**, which retires the lesson in place, dated. A warm check says what it didn't find in its own tense — *I didn't find "…" in what changed* — and offers no button: three paragraphs prove nothing about a habit.

### What the check found

**Findings.** A clause of your intent that the draft pulls against is a finding, listed under **Checks** at the paragraph it is about: your clause quoted back, and one sentence on what pulled against it, never a fix. [Brief](right-column.md#brief-2) marks the same sentences in Going for — a solid underline where the draft pulls against one, a dotted one where the draft says nothing about it — and a solid mark's click comes back here.

**Notes from the check.** A continuity question and a reader's report arrive as ordinary notes, captioned *From the check*. A continuity question ends as a question rather than a verdict — *"Is the dock standing again by this scene?"* rather than *"Contradiction: the dock burned"*. A reader's report is one of four things — where the **dream broke**, what the reader now **believes**, where the reading **dragged**, or where they were **lost** — quoting the words, and **at most the sharpest three**; continuity questions have no cap. They stay until you settle them.

**Two lifetimes.** Findings are replaced by your next check. Notes stay until you answer them. To resolve draws the two differently so you can tell them apart. A dot on To resolve's button and on the Checks chip means a check's notes you haven't seen yet.

A clean check says so plainly: *"Nothing to flag."* It says *"Nothing new to flag."* when everything it raised is already open in another pass — *"1 finding it raised is still open in Structural."* — and that is live: settle the note there and the clean bill comes back.

**Drift is one line, not a note.** When a clause has strained the same way across three checks running, a line appears in the head — *"Your line may have moved — '…' has strained three runs running. Draft's right, or intent's right?"* Press it and Brief opens; the pattern breaking is what takes it away.

A **drifted** verdict also leaves a small mark on the [line above the prose](editor-and-focus.md#the-line-above-the-prose) — *"intent may trail the draft"* — until a later check comes back holding, or you edit the intent.

## What Maugham counts

Beside what you've declared, Maugham counts how you actually work, off nothing but your own edit history — no model involved. Three numbers: sessions since your **frontier** (the newest page you've drafted forward, not just revised) last moved; how often in the chapter's last five sessions a paragraph has been reworked; and how long you've been away. They show in two places only: the **Practice** section of **File ▸ Show Project Statistics**, where every row jumps to its chapter, and the letter's *From Maugham's numbers*. A quiet week says nothing: the frontier has to sit still for **3 sessions running**, or one paragraph be reworked **5 times within the last 5 sessions**, before there's a sentence to write — coming back after two weeks away earns one too.

## Reread

**⌘⇧R** (**File ▸ Reread**, or **Reread** in the head) asks for a different read: it ends the warm session and reads the whole chapter cold, briefed on your intent, your decisions, your lessons ledger and the bible, but not on anything an earlier check found. A reread is still a check — no pass, no round number.

Rounds are their own commands: **File ▸ Run Gould's Round** (⌃⌘R, titled with the editor of the chapter's pass) and **… with Fresh Eyes** (⌃⇧⌘R), and **Run** in the head. See [Review Passes](review-passes.md#rounds-and-fresh-eyes). Your checks never enter a round's memory.

## Reading a chapter for the first time

The first time you check a chapter with some real prose in it, To resolve offers before it runs anything: *"I haven't read this chapter. Read it whole and take notes?"* — **Read** starts the same check ⌘R would, and **Not now** declines. Say no once and Maugham doesn't ask again for that chapter; a stub — a title and a line — isn't offered at all.

## The bible, filling quietly

Every check reads the new writing and notices what your prose establishes — what a character knows and since when, what's physically true, a voice. These land in the chapter's Brief, under **What Claude has read**, with three actions: **Confirm** (make it a decision, in your words), **Correct** (edit it first), and **Dismiss** (it may come back if the manuscript establishes it again). Nothing there is true until you act on it.

Confirming or correcting is permanent in a way dismissing is not: once a reading is a decision of yours, later checks never offer it again. Dismissing says *not so*, and your manuscript is allowed to argue back. See [Decisions](right-column.md#decisions).

## What you can do with a finding or a note

**Answer.** A finding asks you something, and offers **Answer**: type why, press Return, and your sentence becomes a **decision** — a dated line under **Decided** in the chapter's Brief, making the intent if it didn't exist. The finding goes away, and the next check reads what you said, so answering is permanent. A line under the head says where it was filed. Escape closes the field without writing anything. Answers go into the *chapter's* intent, never the book's: the book's stays something you write deliberately.

**Answer as ruling…** is on notes rather than findings, and files into a statement other than an intent: a translator's question into that edition's brief, a first reader's note as a standing instruction in hers. Either way it lands in the same act as your reply. See [Translating Your Book → The round report](translation.md#the-round-report).

**Translator's Note…** (⌘⌥C, Edit ▸ Translator's Note…) writes a *directive* — a decision anchored to the paragraph under the cursor — into this chapter's intent by default, or into one edition's brief if you choose "This edition only". Every translator, reader and collator is briefed with it. On disk it is one plain line under `## Rulings`: `- ¶k7mq: keep the three "and"s — ruled 28 Aug 2026, translator's note`.

**Make a To-Do** turns a finding into a [to-do](tasks.md) on the chapter, carrying its words plus one line recording who raised it, when, in which model, and what it was checked against — your intent as it stood then. To-dos sync and survive; findings don't. ⌘Z takes it back.

**This is a choice.** A question raised under a habit your ledger already names carries **This is a choice**. Press it and the habit is filed in your ledger as a decision you've made, so later checks stop raising it, and the note is stetted. ⌘Z reopens the note; the ledger entry stays. **Stet** a question under a habit a second time and Maugham asks — *Make "filter words" a choice?* — with **Make it a choice**, **Just stet** and **Cancel**. Filing the same habit twice does nothing the second time.

**Keep as lesson…** on a craft note you've **accepted** opens a field prefilled with the note, so you can shorten it to one sentence for your ledger, attributed to the editor who raised it.

**Not this** dismisses a finding. A finding you leave alone is replaced by the next check, which raises it again if it still stands.

**While a check is still running, you can read but not act.** Findings carry no **Answer** and no **Make a To-Do** until it finishes: cancel it and they were never raised; let it run and it may say something different by the end.

## Choosing how hard it looks

The model menu, in To resolve's head beside Run, picks **Fast**, **Standard** (the default), **Deep** or **Exhaustive**, for checks and rounds alike. It is per project and remembered. Changing it never interrupts a run already going; the next run starts a fresh session behind the scenes, so it's a few seconds slower once.

Exhaustive earns its cost on a cold read of a long chapter. Reach for it there rather than as your everyday setting.

**Cancel** appears in the head while a check runs; whatever it had already shown goes with it, and the last completed check comes back.

## What you need for it to work

- **Claude Code installed on your Mac.** The check runs it for you; you don't open it. If it is missing, To resolve says so.
- **Claude access turned on** — Settings → General → *Allow Claude to connect (MCP)*. Turning it off stops any check at once.

Checks use your own Claude account.

## What it will not do

It reads your manuscript and writes nothing into it. It cannot edit your prose, your research, your palette or your intent — including the decisions and the bible it reads you against, which only you add to, through **Answer**, **Confirm** and **Correct**. It never writes your lessons ledger either.

Findings live on the Mac that produced them and are not synced. Losing them costs nothing: press ⌘R again.
