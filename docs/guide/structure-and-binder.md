# Structure & the Binder

The binder, on the left, is where you choose what the window is about. Everything the window can show is a row in it.

### The rows

Top to bottom:

- **The Book** — the row with your project's title. The whole book.
- **Inbox** — captures from your phone. It appears once there is one, with a dot while captures wait. See [Research → Capture inbox](research.md#capture-inbox).
- **Canvas** — the planning canvas. See [Getting Started → The planning canvas](getting-started.md#the-planning-canvas).
- **The manuscript** — your chapters, and the Parts that hold them. A Collection lists its pieces; a screenplay, its script and its scenes.
- **Figures** — the book's tables and images, once there is one (below).
- **Research** — the book's own notes, files and links. See [Research](research.md).
- **Palette** — your palette cards. See [The Sense Pass](sense-pass.md).
- **Editions** — the book's own language, labelled **Source**, then one row per translated edition. See [Translating Your Book](translation.md).
- **Trash**, and **Removed Elsewhere** when it has something in it, at the foot.

### The click rule

A click goes to the thing without throwing away the centre if it can help it:

1. **If the centre already holds it, it is found there.** With the canvas in the centre, clicking a chapter lights that chapter's cards and dims the rest. With an edition's book in the centre, clicking a chapter scrolls the book to it. In a screenplay, clicking a scene scrolls the script to it.
2. **If it is reference material, it opens beside the centre** — a research note, a palette card, a card in a fold — in the right column's [Pulled up](right-column.md#pulled-up-3). Click the same row again to put it away.
3. **Otherwise it becomes the centre.** A chapter opens in the editor; the Book or a Part shows its views.

**A double-click always opens a thing in the centre**, and so does **View ▸ Open in Centre**, or ⌘↓ on a selected row.

With the Book's Cards, Outline or Status in the centre, a click on a chapter opens the chapter: those views are for finding your way, and the chapter is where you were going.

The binder shows two things at once: the row open in the centre is tinted, and the row you clicked last is selected. They differ when a click was answered by rule 1 or 2.

**From the keyboard:** ↑ and ↓ move the selection; ↩ or Space opens it (a card or note pulls up, and ↩ again puts it away); ⌘↓ opens it in the centre.

### The Book and its views

Click the Book and the centre shows the whole book. Choose how with the view switch in the toolbar, or ⌃⌘1, ⌃⌘2, ⌃⌘3:

- **Cards** — one card per chapter, with its line. Click a card to open the chapter.
- **Outline** — a table of chapters, with their tags.
- **Status** — where each chapter stands: its stage, each [review pass](review-passes.md), the notes open and the to-dos open. A count opens the chapter on what it counts; a row at the foot, *The Book — 3 to-dos*, opens the Book's own. ↑, ↓ and ↩ move and open. Come back to Status and it marks the chapter you last opened.

**A Part is a small Book.** Click a Part and the same views show its chapters.

The Book row is there even when the manuscript is empty, so deleting your last chapter never leaves the window with nothing to show. It can't be renamed or dragged.

**In a Collection**, the Book heads the pieces. A new Collection has none, and the Book is where you write the collection's own intent first. **In a screenplay**, the Book is followed by **Script**, the one file, and its scenes; clicking a scene scrolls the script there (see [Screenplays](screenplay.md)).

### A chapter's fold

A chapter's row opens to a **fold** holding what the chapter draws on: its research, the canvas cards and regions bound to it, its palette cards, its figures, and its links, both the ones it makes and the ones made to it (a link made to it carries a return arrow, and its tooltip says which chapter links here). Past about six things the fold shows them in groups with counts: Research, Cards, Palette, Figures.

**Clicking a chapter opens its fold** when it holds anything, for a while: the next chapter you click closes it again, unless you opened it yourself or did something in it. A fold you closed stays closed. ⌘⌥E (View ▸ Show the Chapter's Fold) opens the fold of the chapter the right column is about, with the Parts above it.

**Clicking a row in a fold pulls it up** beside your text: a note, a card, a region (its cards down the column), a palette card, a figure. Click it again to put it away. A link takes you to the chapter it names.

A short story keeps its fold too, on its one row.

### The marks on a row

A chapter's row can carry three small marks at its right end, in this order:

1. **A coloured dot** — its stage: grey while a draft, orange while revising, green when final, read off its [review passes](review-passes.md#passes-and-the-ladder). It appears once you have set any pass.
2. **A padlock** — the chapter is in [read-through](annotations-and-suggestions.md#read-through).
3. **The last dot** — [To resolve](right-column.md#to-resolve-1) has something for it. Never a number. A hollow ring in its place means a chapter Maugham couldn't read.

A Part has the last dot when a chapter inside it does, and the Book has one for anything in the book. In a screenplay, a scene gets it when something falls in it. An edition with a brief Claude proposed reads **Proposed**.

### Adding items

Right-click a row, or use **File**:

- **New Chapter** — right-click a Part, or empty space in the binder; type its name. (The menus use the project's own word: in a Report, **New Section**; in a Collection, **New Prose Piece**, **New Script** and **Link Existing Project…**.)
- **New Part** — a group of chapters ("Part One"). Drag chapters into it.
- **Duplicate** — a copy of the chapter or Part.
- **Delete** — to Maugham's own Trash (below).

A chapter's row menu also has **Tags…**, **Link to Another Chapter…**, **Link Research…**, **Target…**, **Publishing…** (once publishing is set up) and **History**. The same settings are under **Details** at the foot of the chapter's [Brief](right-column.md#brief-2).

**A screenplay is the exception.** It is one file, so there is nothing to add: its scenes are its sluglines, and you type those in the script.

### Dragging

Drag rows to reorder them, move chapters between Parts, link research to a chapter, bind a card, file a capture or send a figure. **While you drag, the binder shows what the drop would do**: a dashed ring around a row means *into it*; a line with a small hollow circle at its left end means *here, between rows*. Where nothing would land, nothing shows, and the drag springs back.

Folders move on disk with the chapters. Filenames keep an `NN-` prefix so Finder's order matches the binder's. If gaps appear after deletes, **Tidy Filenames** (right-click a Part, or File ▸ Tidy All Filenames) renumbers them.

### Trash and Removed Elsewhere

Deleted rows go to **Trash** at the foot of the binder; right-click → **Restore** puts one back, and ⌘⌥Z restores the last deletion in place. Deleted items are kept for 30 days.

**Removed Elsewhere.** If a chapter leaves the binder because another Mac saved an older copy of the book's outline — rather than because anybody deleted it — it doesn't go to Trash. The same happens to a chapter another Mac *added* when the two outlines crossed and the other won. Its words are still in the book, and it appears under **Removed Elsewhere**, with the date it left. **Restore** puts it back where it was (at the top of the manuscript if its Part is gone too). A chapter you deleted and then let go of from Trash isn't offered back.

### Figures in the binder

Once a project has a figure (a table or an image made with Insert), a **Figures** section appears. A figure in the Trash or sent to Research is not listed. Each row shows the figure's number in the book's own words ("Table 2", "Figure 1"; "—" until a line uses it), its caption or name, and small tags: **Research** (it reads values pinned from Research), **changed** (a source it reads has changed) and **unused** (no line uses it).

- **Click a figure** in the Figures section and it takes the centre: a table's grid, or the image large. In a chapter's fold, a figure pulls up beside your text instead.
- **Filter by chapter.** A chapter row that holds figures shows how many; click the count and Figures lists only that chapter's. The chip in the section's header clears it.
- **Drag from Research.** Drop a Research table, picture or PDF on the **Figures** header to make a figure of it (as **Use in Document** does, see [Research](research.md#tables-and-pictures-from-research)). Notes, groups and other figures are refused.
- **Drag to Research.** Drop a figure on the **Research** header, or on a group inside it, to send it there. Dropped on a chapter's row or a row in its fold, it goes to that chapter's research. Maugham asks once ("Send Table 3 to Research?") because the figure's line leaves the text and **this can't be undone**.
- **Lines still pointing into Research.** A figure line written by hand, or by an older version, that points at a file in `research/` is listed too, tagged **in Research**. Right-click it for **Move into Figures**, which makes a figure of the file and points the line at it. **Show in Text** takes you to the line.
- **Right-click a figure** for **Open in Window**, **Send to Research** and **Rename**. In a read-only share, in a chapter in read-through, or where this Mac may not change figures, only Open in Window is offered.

A figure whose Research source moved on also appears in [To resolve](right-column.md#to-resolve-1): *Table 2's source data changed*, with **Compare…** and **Open**.

### Wiki links

Type `[[Chapter 2]]` in any document. Maugham draws it as a link; click it to go there. Rename Chapter 2 in the binder and the links to it update.

### Find

- **⌘F** — find within the current document (⌘G next, ⌘⇧G previous).
- **⌘⌥F** — find across the whole project. The search takes over the binder, with results grouped by document, and case and whole-word toggles. Click a result to go there. **Escape** or ✕ closes it and the binder comes back.
