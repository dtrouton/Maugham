# To Resolve, Brief & Pulled Up

The right column shows one of three places at a time — **To resolve** (⌘1), **Brief** (⌘2) and **Pulled up** (⌘3) — chosen with the three buttons at its top or the keys. [Getting Started](getting-started.md) says in a line what each is for; this page is the whole of it.

**What the column is about follows the binder.** Click a chapter and To resolve and Brief are that chapter's. Click a Part and they gather that Part's chapters. Click the Book and they gather the whole book. Click an edition and they are that edition's. A quiet line at the top names what you are looking at: *Chapter Three*, *The Lighthouse*, *Spanish edition*. When the centre is the canvas or an edition's book, clicking a chapter in the binder turns the column to that chapter and leaves the centre where it is.

⌘⌥0 (View ▸ Hide Right Column) hides the whole column; every key below shows it again.

## To resolve (⌘1)

One list of everything raised on what you picked: notes from rounds and from people, a check's findings, your own to-dos, a translator's questions and what a round left to look at, a design waiting for your answer, a compile that stopped, and a table whose source data changed. A row's click takes you to its place in the text.

A new chapter's list is empty, and says *Nothing to resolve*. Nothing appears on the page because of this list: what Claude wrote stays here, beside your text, never on it.

### The head

**The head is folded until you open it**, so the list has the room. Folded, it is three short rows:

- **Where the chapter stands** — *Revising · Line in progress*, *Final · all four passes done*. The chapter's name leads the line only when it isn't the chapter open in the middle (a Brief link, or a chapter you haven't opened); otherwise you know which it is.
- **Run Round**, and **Check Writing** on a chapter never checked or **Reread** once it has been (and **Cancel** while something runs), offered and refused exactly as in the open head. On a chapter that isn't open, the line that opens it stands here instead.
- **One line, only when there is something to say**: a run in progress (always shown while it runs), a failure, a run on another chapter holding the session, a check that found nothing new, the latest letter (*Letter from Lish · 2 hours ago*), the pass-order nudge when there's no letter, or what you've asked about (*Asking about: the middle sags*), since **Ask about…** still briefs the next run with the head folded. A new letter shows as that line rather than springing the head open; click the line to open it.

⌘R, ⌃⌘R, ⇧⌘R and ⌃⇧⌘R (**Fresh Eyes**, which has no folded button) work folded or open, and **File ▸ Translate…** is answered folded, in that line.

The chevron at the end of the first row opens the head and folds it again; the window remembers which, for every chapter and language. Open, the first row is just the chevron (and the name, by the same rule): the ladder below says where the chapter stands. When the window is short, the open head scrolls within itself and the list keeps its room.

Open, above the list, for a chapter:

- **Where it stands** — the chapter's [review passes](review-passes.md#passes-and-the-ladder) as a ladder, with the status they add up to, and a quiet nudge when you work a later pass while an earlier one is still open.
- **The latest letter** — the newer of the last check's letter and the last round's. **Earlier letters** reaches the ones before it.
- **Run** — the round, with the pass picker, **Run Round** and **Fresh Eyes**, and below it the check's own line: who reads this chapter, **Reread**, and **Ask about…**. See [Checking Your Writing](compiler.md) and [Review Passes](review-passes.md#rounds-and-fresh-eyes).

For a Part or the Book, the head is one line counting what is open across it. A translated edition's head and a chapter read in a language fold the same way: folded, *Spanish edition · Cortázar* or *Spanish translation* (with the chapter's name by the same rule), the Translate button, and one line for a round running or stopped, a notice, or the translator's latest letter. The Source edition's head is short and does not fold.

### Filters

Chips across the top filter by **where an item came from**: one per pass (*Structural*, *Copyedit*…), **Mine** (your own notes and to-dos), **Checks**, **Questions** (a translator's), and **Others**. An edition adds **To look at**, **Design** and **Compile**. Only sources with something in them get a chip.

The keys open the list on one filter: ⌘⌥A for the rounds, ⌘⌥D for Checks, ⌘⌥T for Mine, ⌘⌥L for Questions.

A second control appears once you have marked any note **Do**, **Discuss** or **Decline**: it shows **All**, one mark, or **Untriaged**. Marking a note is a plan, not an answer — a declined note is still open until you settle it.

The **…** at the end of the row holds **New To-Do** and **Select Notes**, so the row stays one line however narrow the column.

### How the list is laid out

For a chapter: **Whole chapter** first, for items about the chapter as a whole, then one group per paragraph, in the order of your text. A screenplay groups by scene. An item whose paragraph you have since deleted sits under **No longer in the text**, last. For a Part or the Book: the Book's own to-dos first, then one group per chapter, in binder order.

**A short list has no group headers.** With fewer than five items, every item shows, each with a small caption saying where it sits (*Whole chapter*, or the paragraph's opening). Five or more, and the groups come back with their headers, counts and folds.

**Two lifetimes, and they look different.** A check's findings are replaced by your next check. Everything else stays until you settle it.

**A long list gets quieter.** Past about a dozen items, the chips choose one source at a time, and only the group under your cursor stays open; the rest fold to their headers. Move the cursor and the open group follows it.

### Acting on an item

A row on the chapter open in the centre offers its verbs: on a suggestion or a craft note, **Accept** and **Reject…**; on a comment, **Got it**; on a question, **Reply…**; on all of them, **Stet** and **Archive**; on a finding, **Answer**, **Make a To-Do** and **Not this**; on a to-do, its checkbox. What each does is in [Notes & Suggestions](annotations-and-suggestions.md), [Checking Your Writing](compiler.md#what-you-can-do-with-a-finding-or-a-note) and [To-Dos](tasks.md).

**You act on a chapter you are looking at.** In a Part's or the Book's list, a chapter that isn't open reads but offers no verbs, and its group says *Open this chapter to answer its notes*. Click a row and that chapter opens on the paragraph, with the list staying where it was.

**Several at once.** **Select Notes**, in the **…** menu beside the chips, selects notes for one batch: see [Working a batch](annotations-and-suggestions.md#working-a-batch).

**Settled** sits at the foot, folded. Open it for notes you resolved (with **Reopen** and **Revert**), notes you deleted (**Restore**), and done and archived to-dos.

**Where an answer goes is said.** When an answer files something into a Brief — **Answer**, **Add to intent**, **Keep as lesson**, **This is a choice**, **Make it a rule** — a line under the head says where — *Filed in Brief*, *Filed under What I've Learned* — and clicking it shows it there.

### The binder's dots

The last of a binder row's [marks](structure-and-binder.md#the-marks-on-a-row) is a small dot, there when To resolve would list anything for the row: a chapter with an open note, to-do or finding; a Part when a chapter inside it does; the Book for anything in the book. Never a number: the numbers are in the Book's [Status](structure-and-binder.md#the-book-and-its-views). A hollow ring means a chapter Maugham couldn't read.

## Brief (⌘2)

What you've said this chapter — or Part, or book — is meant to do, in your own words and no one else's.

**For a chapter**, in this order:

1. **The line** (optional) — one sentence for the cards, the outline and the line above the prose. Left empty, those show the opening of Going for. ⌘⌥I puts the cursor here.
2. **Going for** — what the chapter is trying to do, as prose. It can be several paragraphs. Type straight in; the first keystroke makes the file. ⌘⌥N puts the cursor here, and **Open in Centre** gives you the whole page to write it at length.
3. **Decided** — your dated decisions about the chapter, newest first, with the older ones folded. It appears once there is something in it.

Then two quiet lines that open in place: **From the book**, what applies from the book's own Brief; and **What Claude has read**, what a check noticed in your text and you haven't confirmed yet (see [The bible, filling quietly](compiler.md#the-bible-filling-quietly)).

At the foot, one line holds the chapter's settings as values — *3,000 words · rome, draft · 2 links* — then **Details**, a menu of every setting (Tags…, Link to Another Chapter…, Target…, Publishing…), then **History**.

**The last check's reading, marked on your own sentences.** After a check, Going for shows your intent as you wrote it, with **Edit** to change it. A **solid underline** marks a sentence the draft pulls against; a **dotted underline** marks one the draft says nothing about yet; a sentence the draft holds is left alone. The marks carry none of the check's words: click a solid one and To resolve shows what the check said. Edit the intent and the marks go until the next check. They appear only here, never above the prose.

**A Part** has no intent of its own, so its Brief gathers its chapters' decisions, one section per chapter, each with a way to that chapter's Brief.

**The Book's Brief** is the book's own Going for, and every standing decision in the book in one place, filterable by where it was made: the book, a chapter, What I've Learned, First Reader, an edition. Below them are links that pull up **What I've Learned**, **First Reader** and **Visual Language**, and **History** for the project.

**An edition's Brief** is its edition brief, who is cast for it, and the book's Visual Language: see [Translating Your Book](translation.md#the-edition-brief-and-the-cast).

**The line above the prose** is the Brief in a sentence: the chapter's line, or the opening of what it's going for. Click it to open Brief. If a check found the draft may have moved away from what you said, it carries a small mark, *intent may trail the draft*.

### Decisions

A decision is intent about something specific — "Kelly only ever acts on what she's actually heard" — on a line of its own rather than in a paragraph. **Decisions are made by pressing something, not by typing a heading.** Answering a check's finding files your sentence under Decided, dated, with where it came from; so do **Confirm** and **Correct** in What Claude has read. Each has **Edit** and **Revoke**. Revoke takes a line out (one ⌘Z brings it back exactly as it was); Edit changes the words and keeps the date, because a correction isn't a new decision.

**A directive** is a decision about one paragraph — the note you leave a translator with **Translator's Note…** (⌘⌥C), or one a round report writes when you keep your own wording. If its paragraph is gone, it shows dimmed with *This paragraph no longer exists* and offers **Remove**. Nothing is silently dropped or moved to another paragraph.

**Glossary** entries — a term and how it's rendered, for the whole book — show as a table: term, rendering, note, with **Revoke** beside each. They belong in an edition brief.

**Claude can propose an edition brief or a visual language; you adopt it or don't.** From Claude Desktop or Claude Code, the `edition-brief` and `visual-language` skills interview you and stage a draft (nothing is written). A waiting proposal shows a banner — *Claude proposed a Spanish edition brief · 12 minutes ago* — with its reasons, a line-by-line comparison with your text, and **Adopt** / **Discard**. Adopt replaces the prose (one ⌘Z takes it back) and leaves your decisions as they were. A dot on Brief's button means a proposal is waiting. Claude can't propose a chapter's intent at all: there is no tool for it, on purpose.

Typing a `## Rulings` heading yourself doesn't make the section; let the buttons write it. On disk the statement is plain Markdown, written out from its own history like every document, so edit it in Maugham, not in Finder.

## Pulled up (⌘3)

One thing at a time, read beside the centre:

- a research note, table, PDF or picture, or a palette card — click it in the binder;
- a card, a region or a figure in a chapter's fold;
- **What I've Learned** (⌘⌥G), **First Reader** (⌘⌥Y), **Visual Language** (⌘⌥V);
- **History** (⌃⌘H) — the chapter's, or the project's when no chapter is picked.

A research note and a palette card are editable here; a canvas card is read-only, because its words live on the canvas. A region shows its cards down the column, in the order they sit on the canvas.

**It stays until you close it**, as you move from chapter to chapter. Pull up something else and it takes the place. The header carries:

- **Back** (⌘[) — pull up again what was pulled up before.
- **Open in Centre** — move it into the centre. The column empties.
- **Close** — put it away. Esc does the same.

Clicking the same row again in the binder also puts it away.

**With nothing pulled up**, the place shows the centre's own details: the canvas's selected card, region or line; a figure's details; a research item's citation and versions. With a figure or a research item in the centre *and* something pulled up, a folded line **Details of <name>** sits above it. On the canvas, selecting a card shows its details over what is pulled up, with a line naming what is covered and **Show it** to bring it back.

The Pulled up button is hidden while there is nothing it could show.

### What I've Learned (⌘⌥G)

Your lessons ledger — what you've learned about your own writing, one for the whole project. It is rows, not an essay: each entry is a dated line under **Ledger**, with **Edit** and **Revoke**.

Two ways in: **Keep as lesson** on a habit in a check's letter, or on a craft note you accepted; and **This is a choice**, which files a habit as a decision you've made. Or type. Every check reads what stands here, in every chapter, so a lesson filed once is a standard the next check measures you by. A lesson you've stopped working on is **retired** in place, dated, never deleted. Its file is `lessons.md` at the project root.

### First Reader (⌘⌥Y)

Who reads this project, and what she knows — one for the whole book. The essay is who she is: what she knows of the story, what she reads for, what bores her. The rows beneath, under **Instructions**, are standing instructions she reads under every time. See [Your own first reader](compiler.md#your-own-first-reader). Its file is `first-reader.md`.

### Visual Language (⌘⌥V)

How the book looks — typography, cover direction, the references you steer by. One for the book. **It takes pictures**: drop image files on the strip at the bottom, or paste one with ⌘V; each is copied into `visual-language_assets/` and a Markdown image line is added. Claude reads this before it designs your book's template, so the look comes from your description rather than its taste.

### History (⌃⌘H)

The chapter's history, newest first: typing, notes, checkpoints. See [Backups & History](backups-and-history.md#history).
